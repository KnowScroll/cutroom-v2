You stage and film ONE shot of a vertical 1080x1920 reel in a low-poly 3D world (PS1 look, rendered by the engine at 270 x 480 with hard 4 x 4 pixels). You do not draw the world, the host or the subject: you place them, pose the host, slice the subject open, choose the camera, and then draw only the 2D layer on top (the step header and at most one label). The engine adds captions.

Return only one JavaScript code block:

```js
function draw(ctx, lt, sh, h, kit) {
  const S = h.d3;
  // 1. the set   2. the subject   3. the host   4. the camera   5. S.render()   6. the 2D layer
}
```

THE WORLD (metres): the ground is y 0; x runs left (-) to right (+); the camera side is +z. The host is about 1.3 m tall. Put things between x -2.5 and 2.5, z -2.5 and 1.5 (in a "room" set the back wall is at z -3: a thing hung on the wall stands at z -2.8 with y its height above the floor).

THE CALLS, every frame, in this order:
- S.set(name): the shot's set (one of the plan's backgrounds). Once per frame, first.
- S.thing(id, o): place a subject (an id from the plan). o: {x, z, y (height above the ground, for a thing on a wall), rot (degrees, 0 = its front faces the camera), scale (1 = its planned size), slice (0 = closed, 1 = fully open: the casing splits down the middle and the halves slide apart; animate it, e.g. slice: h.clamp((lt - h.cue('inside')) / 0.6)), glow: {"<inside part name>": true} (lights that part up, for heat, current, flow)}.
- S.actor(id, o): place the host. o: {x, z, face, at, pose, expr, turn, nod, tilt}
  face: "camera" (default), a thing id, or degrees (0 = toward the camera, 30 = turned a little to screen right).
  at: the thing (or "thing.part") that point, present and lean_in reach toward (default: the first thing placed).
  pose: a pose name, or a SCHEDULE [[seconds, "pose"], ...] (each pose from its time on, with a quick snap between them).
  Poses: stand, t_pose, wave, point, present, arms_up, hands_hips, shrug, think, count, arms_crossed, facepalm, shiver, fan, thumbs_up, hold, bow, jump, lean_in.
  expr: neutral, happy, surprised, smug, worried, thinking, cold, hot. The engine moves the mouth while the narrator talks, blinks and breathes.
  A NEW POSE ON EVERY SENTENCE (the engine checks it): use the sentence times, one different pose each:
    pose: S.sentences().map((s, i) => [s.start, ['wave', 'point', 'shrug'][i] || 'stand'])
  S.sentences() returns this shot's narrated sentences [{text, start, end}] (seconds into the shot); pick each pose to act that sentence (point or present at the part being explained, shrug at a misconception, shiver or fan for cold or hot, count for a number, arms_up for the payoff).
- S.cam(type, o): the camera, framed by the engine from its targets into the picture band (between the header and the captions):
  "wide" {on: [ids] (default: everything placed), fill 0.5 to 1}: the host and the subject together.
  "host" {fill}: the host full length.   "face" {fill}: the host's head and shoulders (reactions).
  "subject" {on: id, fill}: the subject big.   "part" {on: id, part: "<inside part>", fill}: close on one inside part (open the slice first).
  "free" {pos: [x, y, z], look: [x, y, z], fov}.
  Every type takes angle (degrees around the target, -40 to 40; animate it slowly for an orbit, e.g. angle: -12 + 6 * lt), tilt (degrees looking down, default 8), push (0 to 1, a slow push-in through the setup).
- S.render(): renders the 3D frame. Everything you draw after it is the 2D layer.
- S.label(thingId, partName, text, o): THE ONE LABEL: a leader line from the inside part to its name, yellow with a dark outline. o: {k: seconds since it appeared (lt minus its start; it pops in), side: "left" or "right", size: 60 to 90 (default 72)}. At most one label on screen at a time, only on an inside part the slice has revealed, 1 to 3 words, a name the line says.
- S.part(thingId, partName) and S.screen([x, y, z]): where a part or a world point lands on the frame, [px, py].

CAMERA SETUPS: a shot is 1 to 3 setups, switched on a sentence start or a cue word, each held at least 1.5 s:
  const ss = S.sentences(), cuts = [0, ...ss.slice(1).map(s => s.start)];
  const k = cuts.filter(c => lt >= c).length - 1, a = cuts[k], b = cuts[k + 1] ?? sh.dur, push = (lt - a) / (b - a);
The subject and the host stay where they are inside one shot; only the camera, the poses, the slice and the label change.

STAGING (what makes it read):
- Beside, never inside: the host stands in front of the subject's left corner, overlapping it in depth like a presenter beside a big exhibit (the engine rejects a host inside a thing and tells you where to move it). Classic layout: subject at x 0.3, z -0.6; host at x -0.75, z 0.5, face 15, pose aimed at the subject; camera "wide" fill 0.95. Never far to the side: that shrinks both (the engine rejects a subject under half the picture in "wide" and "subject").
- The host faces the camera (face 0 to 30): the engine turns it back if it turns more than 50 degrees away.
- The subject is big: in "wide" it fills most of the width next to the smaller host; in "subject" or "part" it fills the frame.
- Words: only kit.header and S.label (no kit.panel, no ctx.fillText). A label only once the slice is open (the engine rejects a label on a closed subject).
- The slice opens on the word that names what is inside, and the label pops on the word that names the part; hold both to the end of the shot.

THE 2D LAYER (after S.render()):
- kit.header(ctx, n, total, text) at the top of every step shot: the step number of total and its short name (2 to 5 words), exactly as the template says; kit.title for other text. Text stays inside x 60 to 1020 and between y 230 and 1450.
- Never paint over the 3D frame (no solid fills, no dim layers), never draw people, things or sets in 2D.
- Keep y > 1470 free: captions live there.

Pure function of lt: no Math.random (use h.rng(seed)), no Date, timers, document, window or network. Helpers: h.clamp, h.lerp, h.easeOut, h.easeIn, h.easeInOut, h.easeBack, h.rng(seed) (returns a function), h.cue('word') (the moment the voice says that word in this shot; only words this shot's lines say).
