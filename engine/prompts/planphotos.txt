REAL EVIDENCE (this template is a documentary explainer): beside the drawings, the reel shows real photographs and quotes from its sources. Add two more keys to the JSON object:

  "photos": [ {"id": "short_snake_case", "shot": shot number (1-based), "shows": "what the photograph must show, concretely: the real thing as people photograph it (a real [machine] at a real [site], seen from outside; the [part] close up)", "search": "2 or 3 words to search Wikimedia Commons with (it finds only files whose title or description holds EVERY word): a famous site's proper name alone, or the thing's common name", "also": ["a second search: the other of those two (the common name if search was a site, a famous site if search was the common name)"], "label": "what it shows, 2 to 7 plain words"} ]   (2 to 6)
  "quotes": [ {"id": "short_snake_case", "shot": shot number, "text": "one or two whole sentences copied WORD FOR WORD from the REFERENCE", "mark": "the 4 to 12 words of that text that carry the shot's point (they are underlined on screen), copied exactly"} ]   (0 to 3; only when there is a REFERENCE)

- A photo proves that the drawn thing is real: pick the real objects and places the script names (a machine, a site, a part, a material, a famous place), never ideas, processes, people's faces, logos, maps or charts. Things Wikimedia Commons is likely to have: well-known sites by name, common machines and structures, natural features.
- At most one photo per shot, and never in two shots in a row. The hook may open on a photo; the photo is NOT the shot's whole picture: the drawing stays the main picture and the photo is a framed inset beside it.
- A quote backs one claim with the source's own words: the sentence of the REFERENCE that states the shot's point. One per shot at most, never in the shot that has a photo.
- Each shot's "idea" names the photo or quote it pins (by id), where it sits and on which word it appears. A shot without a photo or quote still has its drawing.
