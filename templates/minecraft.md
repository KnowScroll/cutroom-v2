# minecraft

## about
A voxel build explainer in a blocky block-game world: the subject assembles itself one block at a time on a grassy hill, one named part per shot, layer by layer, every block dropping in with a bounce while the narrator explains that part under a white pixel-style title naming the step. It opens on the finished build, shows the one unit it is made of, builds it from the ground up in the real order (base first, then each part), can open it up to show the hidden part inside, and ends when the last block slams in and the build comes alive (lamps light up, smoke or water starts to flow), with a one-word payoff. For how a thing is built, how a structure or machine works part by part, or how something grew over time in stages (monuments, towers, bridges, dams, ships, machines, a city, a tree, a reef). Not for comparisons of several things, history with no physical result, character stories or abstract ideas.

## look
- One blocky world seen from a fixed three-quarter FRONT view, a little from above and from the right (like looking at a model on a table): every object is made of equal cubes on a grid, and every cube face carries a 16 x 16 pixel texture. No outlines, no gradients on blocks, no circles or curves on any object: the only gradients are the sky and the fog. Cube edges read only from the tone step between faces: top face lightest, front face the material's own tone, right face darkest (light from the upper left, in front).
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() after them. h.asset('id', x, y, scale, params) takes NO ctx either.
- Colour: write every tone as a CSS string `oklch(L C H)` or `oklch(L C H / alpha)` (L 0 to 1, C chroma, H hue in degrees); the canvas takes it directly. Make each face shade by changing L only: top face L + 0.05, front face L, right face L - 0.12; build these strings ONCE in makeKit. h.mix and h.rgba read only hex: never pass an oklch string to them. The plan's palette stays hex: bg = the sky's top blue, ink = the text shadow, accent = the lit lamp gold (the engine colours the caption bar with it), accent2 = the grass green.
- Block geometry, exact px at scale 1 (grid: x to the right, y up, z away from the viewer, into the picture):
  - S = 64: the front face is a 64 x 64 square. One step of depth (z + 1) moves a block DX = 20 px right and DY = 24 px up.
  - A block whose front face has its bottom-left corner at (X, Y): front face = the square X to X + 64, Y - 64 to Y; top face = the parallelogram (X, Y - 64), (X + 64, Y - 64), (X + 84, Y - 88), (X + 20, Y - 88); right face = (X + 64, Y), (X + 64, Y - 64), (X + 84, Y - 88), (X + 84, Y - 24).
  - Block (x, y, z) of a build whose block (0, 0, 0) sits at (X0, Y0) is drawn at X = X0 + 64 * x + 20 * z, Y = Y0 - 64 * y - 24 * z.
  - Painter's order: sort by z descending (far first), then y ascending, then x ascending, and draw in that order. Face culling: skip a face when the neighbour on that side is a settled solid block of the same list (front face: the block at z - 1; top: y + 1; right: x + 1). Glass and water never hide a neighbour's face.
- Textures, 4 px per texel: the front face is 16 x 16 texels, the top face 16 wide x 6 deep, the right face 5 deep x 16 tall. Map each face with ctx.save(); ctx.transform(...); fill; ctx.restore():
  - front: ctx.transform(4, 0, 0, 4, X, Y - 64), texel (i, j) = ctx.fillRect(i, j, 1, 1), i 0 to 15 across, j 0 to 15 down.
  - top: ctx.transform(4, 0, 20 / 6, -4, X, Y - 64), i 0 to 15 across, j 0 to 5 from the front edge back.
  - right: ctx.transform(4, -4.8, 0, 4, X + 64, Y - 64), i 0 to 4 from the front edge back, j 0 to 15 down.
  - Speed (a frame must draw in well under 180 ms): compute every texture ONCE in makeKit, never per frame: for each material and each face size, a grid of tone indices from its rule below (with its own h.rng), turned into runs [tone, i, j, length] of the texels that differ from the face's most common tone. To draw a face: fill the whole face (ctx.fillRect(0, 0, cols, rows) under the transform) with that most common tone, then each run with ctx.fillRect(i, j, length, 1), setting fillStyle only when the tone changes. Make 2 variants per material (two seeds) and pick variant (3 * x + 5 * y + 7 * z) % 2 for each block, so neighbours differ. No h.mix, string building or h.rng calls inside the per-frame face loops.
- Materials (kit.MAT), each a ramp of tones (L values at one chroma and hue) and a texture rule on the face's texel grid:
  - grass: top face: grass greens L 0.56, 0.59, 0.63, 0.65, 0.68, 0.71, C 0.14, H 136, picked per texel (the middle tones twice as often). Front and right faces: dirt with a jagged green fringe: each texel column gets a fringe depth of 3 + (r() < 0.55 ? 1 : 0) + (r() < 0.22 ? 1 : 0) texels from the top in the greens (L - 0.02), the rest dirt.
  - dirt: L 0.41, 0.44, 0.49, 0.52, 0.55, 0.57, C 0.06, H 60, picked per texel; 4% of texels a grey pebble (L 0.58, C 0.007, H 75).
  - stone (cobble): stones L 0.58, 0.64, 0.70 and mortar L 0.42, 0.50, C 0.004, H 90. Rule: 10 seed points (r() * 16, r() * 16, one stone tone each), wrapping round the face edges; a texel is mortar when (distance to the second-nearest point - distance to the nearest) < 1.15 texels, otherwise the nearest point's stone tone.
  - planks: boards L 0.60, 0.63, 0.67, 0.70, C 0.085, H 75; seams L 0.46, C 0.06, H 69. Rule: boards 4 texel rows tall; the 4th row of each board is seam; each board has one seam texel column (a butt joint) at its own random x; the other texels take one board tone per 2-texel pair, one step lighter or darker at random.
  - darkplanks: the planks rule with boards L 0.39, 0.42, 0.46, 0.49, C 0.075, H 50 and seams L 0.29, C 0.05, H 51.
  - log: bark L 0.36, 0.41, 0.45, 0.49, C 0.055, H 69: one tone per texel column (vertical stripes), 16% of texels L 0.31. Top face: square rings round the centre alternating L 0.59 and 0.67 (C 0.08, H 78) inside a 1-texel bark rim.
  - leaves: L 0.43, 0.47, 0.52, 0.57, 0.62, C 0.135, H 139 per texel, 13% of texels a dark hole (L 0.32, C 0.09, H 140).
  - glass: a 1-texel frame L 0.94, C 0.02, H 226; inside L 0.90, C 0.04, H 226 at alpha 0.3 (see-through); 2 short diagonal glint lines (3 to 5 texels) L 0.99.
  - lamp (the blocks that light up at the end): off: L 0.50, 0.55, C 0.06, H 70 with a 2-texel frame L 0.36; on: L 0.84, 0.86, 0.89, 0.93, C 0.14, H 83 per texel with the 2-texel frame L 0.62, C 0.12, H 66.
  - metal: L 0.80, 0.84, 0.88, C 0.01, H 250 per texel, a 1-texel border L 0.66, a corner rivet texel L 0.60 at each corner, one sheen row L 0.93 at texel row 3.
  - brick: bricks L 0.47, 0.50, 0.53, C 0.11, H 35; mortar L 0.80, C 0.02, H 80. Rule: rows 4 texels tall (3 brick rows, 1 mortar row); a vertical mortar texel every 8 texels, shifted by 4 on every other row.
  - sand: L 0.83, 0.86, 0.89, C 0.06, H 90 per texel.
  - water: L 0.55, 0.60, 0.65, C 0.13, H 250 at alpha 0.8, with 2 rows of lighter wave texels (L 0.75) per face.
  - wool: one colour for a part that has no real material: L 0.55, 0.60, 0.65 at C 0.14 and the hue the style guide names for it, per texel. A build may use several wools, each with its own hue.
  Build every material the style guide names, plus grass, dirt, stone, planks, log, leaves, glass and lamp always.
- The kit returns, besides palette, font, bg, panel, title and post (assets and shots use these instead of drawing blocks their own way):
  - kit.S = 64, kit.DX = 20, kit.DY = 24, kit.MAT (the materials).
  - kit.block(ctx, X, Y, m, o): one block of material name m at (X, Y) as above. o.f, o.t, o.s = false skip the front, top or right face; o.sq squashes it about the bottom centre of its front face (x scale 1 + 0.45 * o.sq, y scale 1 - o.sq; o.sq may be negative = stretch); o.sc scales it about its centre (0.5 to 1 while falling); o.a = opacity; o.lit (0 to 1, lamp blocks only) draws the lamp's on texture over its off texture at that opacity.
  - kit.voxels(ctx, list, X0, Y0, o): draws a list of blocks {x, y, z, m, dy, sq, sc, a} in painter's order with face culling (only blocks with dy, sq both 0, sc 1 or missing and a 1 or missing count as settled); dy lifts a block by dy px. o.lit (0 to 1) lights every lamp block and then, after all blocks, draws each lamp block's square glow: its front face square grown by 10 px on every side at alpha 0.22 * lit and by 22 px at alpha 0.10 * lit, in L 0.80, C 0.155, H 66 (square, never round).
  - kit.fall(a, slam): the drop of one block, a = seconds since its landing moment (negative before it lands). Returns null while a < -D (not shown yet), where D = 0.2 s (0.23 s if slam). While falling (p = 1 + a / D, 0 to 1): {dy: H0 * (1 - p * p), sq: 0, sc: 0.5 + 0.5 * p} with H0 = 154 px (352 px if slam). After landing: {dy: a < 0.16 ? B * Math.sin(Math.PI * a / 0.16) : 0, sq: Q * Math.exp(-15 * a) * Math.cos(36 * a), sc: 1} with B = 13 px and Q = 0.22 (B = 4 px and Q = 0.4 if slam); from a = 0.5 on it returns exactly {dy: 0, sq: 0, sc: 1} (settled).
  - kit.cube(ctx, x, y, size, L, C, H, alpha): a small plain cube (no texture) whose front face is the square x to x + size, y - size to y, with its top face (depth size * 0.31 right and size * 0.375 up) at L + 0.05 and its right face at L - 0.12; for dust, smoke, sparks and other particles.
- kit.bg backgrounds (1080 x 1920; any other name draws "hill"; kit.bg draws its terrace edges with the kit's own block function and its trees with its own voxels function, so the kit's own check runs them):
  - SPEED (the engine rejects any frame over 180 ms, and a textured hill drawn texel by texel took 177 ms on its own): draw everything that does not move ONCE, inside makeKit itself, never in a frame: const BG = {}; for (const name of ['hill', 'close']) BG[name] = h.cache(name, 1080, 1920, g => { draw that background's static layers (sky, far field, trees, plateau, speckles, terraces, tufts, flowers) on g with the same block and voxels functions (g as their ctx) }); then kit.bg(ctx, lt, name) only does ctx.drawImage(BG[name === 'close' ? 'close' : 'hill'], 0, 0) and draws the moving parts on ctx (clouds, drifting fog). kit.bg then costs under 10 ms a frame, leaving the time for the build. The textures must survive this: every block face in the cached image shows its 4 px texels in several tones (the engine measures the background's detail and rejects a flat one).
  - "hill" (every build shot):
    - Sky y 0 to 760: a vertical gradient from L 0.67, C 0.15, H 260 at the top to L 0.86, C 0.06, H 257 at the horizon (y 760).
    - Clouds: 10 to 12 flat white slabs made of cells on a 48 px grid: each cloud 3 to 8 cells wide and 1 or 2 rows of 24 px, the second row shorter and shifted by 24 px, white (L 0.98) at alpha 0.92 with a 10 px underside strip L 0.88, C 0.03, H 257; at y 120 to 260 and 420 to 640 (the title band y 280 to 400 stays clear sky), drifting right at 18 px per second and wrapping round.
    - Far field y 760 to 980: grass ground L 0.65, C 0.14, H 136 with sparse 4 px grass speckles; a fog of the horizon colour fading from full at y 740 to nothing at y 980; 6 to 8 small far trees on the horizon (a 10 x 40 px bark trunk and 2 stacked leaf rectangles 64 x 34 and 40 x 22 px) at 55% opacity.
    - Two near trees made with the voxels function: a trunk of 4 or 5 log blocks and a crown of leaves blocks (2 layers of 5 x 5 with 0 to 2 corners missing, then a 3 x 3 layer, then a plus of 5 on top), one standing on the plateau's back edge at the left (trunk at x 60 to 124) and one at the right (trunk at x 900 to 964), both partly cut by the frame edge.
    - Plateau (where the build stands) y 980 to 1228: the grass top colour with dense 4 px speckles of the grass greens (one speckle per 40 square px). The build's front-bottom edge stands at y 1180, so 2 rows of plateau show in front of it.
    - Terraces, stepping down toward the viewer from y 1228 to the bottom: 4 steps, each a row of grass blocks' front faces (the block function with only the front face; the green fringe on top, dirt below) followed by the next terrace's grass top band, 150 to 200 px tall, speckled like the plateau. Break each edge into runs of 3 to 6 blocks; every other run sits one depth row back (24 px higher and 20 px right), with the right face of the last block of each forward run drawn, and the gap in front of a set-back run filled with speckled grass top.
    - Grass tufts: 100 to 120 tufts on the plateau sides, the far field and the terraces (never on the build's place, x 200 to 880 on the plateau): each tuft 3 or 4 blades 4 px wide and 16 to 40 px tall, each blade in 3 stacked bands from dark at the base (L 0.42, C 0.12, H 138) to L 0.58 at the tip, the tips swaying 4 px with Math.sin(lt * 1.6 + i). Pixel flowers: 14 to 18 on the terraces, a 4 px stem 14 px tall and a 12 x 12 px head with a 4 px dark centre, in red (L 0.55, C 0.21, H 25), yellow (L 0.87, C 0.17, H 97) and blue (L 0.61, C 0.18, H 264).
  - "close": the same world from closer and lower, for a single object: the same sky and clouds with the horizon at y 860, the plateau grass from y 1000 to the bottom of the frame with bigger tufts (blades 6 px wide, 24 to 60 px tall), flowers and no terraces.
- kit.panel: a plate for numbers: fill L 0.22, C 0.02, H 60 at alpha 0.8, a 6 px inner border L 0.42, C 0.02, H 60 at alpha 0.7, square corners. kit.title draws the text; the panel never draws text itself.
- kit.title (text only, no box): bold Cascadia Mono, white (L 0.98), with a hard drop shadow (ctx.shadowColor L 0.29, C 0.012, H 67; ctx.shadowOffsetX and Y = Math.round(size * 0.076); ctx.shadowBlur 0), lowercase as given, textBaseline 'middle', centred on x. Characters are drawn one by one at x0 + i * adv (adv = ctx.measureText('M').width; x0 = x - adv * length / 2). With opts.k (seconds since the text appeared), character number n (spaces not counted) starts at a = k - 0.03 * n: hidden while a < 0; for a < 0.12 it falls from 70 px above (dy = -70 * (1 - q * q), q = a / 0.12); for a < 0.24 it hops (dy = -14 * Math.sin(Math.PI * (a - 0.12) / 0.12)); then it rests; dy is rounded to whole steps of 6 px. With opts.slam the whole word appears at once and scales about its centre: 1.6 to 0.86 over the first 0.05 s, to 1.07 by 0.10 s, to 1 by 0.16 s. With opts.out (seconds since it started leaving) it fades out over 0.08 s while sliding down 18 px.
- kit.font(size, weight) = `${weight} ${size}px Cascadia Mono`. kit.post: a very light vignette only (corners at most 15% darker); no grain, no colour wash.

## motion
- Motion shows something new (blocks dropping in, the camera moving to the new part), then holds; drifting clouds and swaying tufts are optional ambient life.
- Blocks drop in with kit.fall: each block of the part being built has its own landing moment, spread evenly over the part's build time (dur), so a part arrives as a quick cascade, about 0.03 to 0.08 s between blocks, never all at once. Each block falls 2.4 blocks in 0.2 s, growing from half size, then hops, squashes and settles.
- The order within a part is a sweep the eye can follow: a ring or wall goes round the footprint by angle (starting front left, going round); a filled floor or slab spreads out from its centre; a roof or deck row snakes left to right, then back right to left on the next row; a post or column goes bottom to top. Layers go bottom to top; the next layer starts while the last few blocks of the layer below are still landing (about 0.05 s of overlap).
- The finishing block (slam): the build's last block falls from 5.5 blocks up (kit.fall with slam), lands with a big squash, a puff of dust cubes and a camera shake; 0.1 s later the build comes alive.
- Coming alive (lit): the lamp blocks switch on in 3 quick steps (lit 0.35, then 0.7 after 0.04 s, then 1 after 0.08 s) and then flicker gently (lit * (1 + 0.05 * Math.sin(t * 29) + 0.035 * Math.sin(t * 61 + 1.3))). The build's real sign of working starts at the same moment, as a stream of small plain cubes (kit.cube): smoke or steam rising from a vent, water pouring, sparks, light beams, product coming out, whichever is true for the subject.
- Camera (shots transform the background and the build together; the text is drawn after, untransformed): in build shots a slow push-in from scale 1 to 1.06 about the build's centre plus a sideways drift of 20 to 30 px (easeInOut) may carry the view to the new part, then hold; the hook pulls back from 1.12 to 1; the inside shot pushes in to 1.2 on the exposed inner part; on the slam the whole frame shakes by 12 * Math.exp(-13 * a) px (a = seconds since the slam, x with Math.sin(a * 71), y with Math.cos(a * 83)) for 0.3 s. Never scale below 1 (the background must cover the frame).
- Titles: each step's title drops in letter by letter from the start of the line that names the part (kit.title with k = lt - that line's start); it leaves with opts.out in the last 0.1 s of its shot. The payoff word slams in (opts.slam) at the moment the finishing block lands and holds to the end of the shot.
- Easing: easeOut for things arriving, easeInOut for the camera; the bounce and squash come from kit.fall only.

## plan
- First decide the build: the real subject as a block model with its REAL silhouette and proportions (a tower is tall and thin, a bridge long and flat, a dam a wall across a valley, a pyramid stepped), made of 3 to 5 named parts in the REAL order they are built or grew (from the ground up, inside before outside), plus the finishing block: ONE block that is really placed last (the top piece, the key piece, the last stone), which is a part of its own, the last part. Each part has its real material from the material table (stone is stone, timber is planks or log, glass is glass, steel is metal, bricks are brick, water is water; a part with no material of its own gets a wool of its own hue), and two parts that touch never share a material. Name the materials the build uses in style.description. style.font is "Cascadia Mono"; style.backgrounds are exactly two, named "hill" and "close". The lamp blocks are the blocks that light up when the build comes alive (its lights, a glowing core, screens, a beacon).
- Size: the footprint NX blocks wide (x) by NZ deep (z), with NX from 5 to 13 and NZ from 3 to 8, and NL layers tall; NL * 64 + NZ * 24 must stay at most 640 px at scale 1. A taller real shape is modelled with more layers and drawn at scale s = 640 / (NL * 64 + NZ * 24).
- Structure: 7 to 9 shots, one beat each, in this usual order (a story may merge or reorder beats):
  1. hook: the finished build, whole and alive (every part including the finishing block, lamps lit, its sign of working running), standing on the hill; the title is the subject's name (at most 16 characters); the panel one surprising real number about it with its unit. Line: a question about it, or the surprising number, naming the subject.
  2. unit: the one unit the subject is made of many times over (a stone block, a brick, a beam, a module, a cell), alone and big on the "close" background, bobbing; title "the [unit]"; panel its real size or weight. Line: how many of them it took, or what one weighs.
  3. base: an empty plateau; the first part (the ground part: the footing, foundation, base or roots) drops in. Title "1. [part]". Line: what it is and why it comes first.
  4. part (one shot per further part, 2 to 4 shots): the next part drops in on top of the ones already standing (they are all there from the shot's first frame). Title "[n]. [part]" counting on from the base. Line: what this part does.
  5. inside (optional, only if the subject has a hidden part that explains it): the build without its finishing block opens up (the front half of its outer parts lifts away) and the camera pushes in on the inner part; title: the inner part's name (no number); panel: its real number. Line: how the inner part works.
  6. finish: every part stands except the finishing block; the title names it ("[n]. [finishing block]"); 60% into the line the finishing block slams in, the build comes alive, the title leaves and the payoff word slams in: one short lowercase word that names what the finished thing is or does, ending with a full stop (at most 9 characters with the stop). No panel. Line: the finishing piece and what the whole thing does now.
  7. stats (optional): the finished, alive build while the camera pulls back; no title; 2 or 3 stat cards ONE AT A TIME in the same place, each a short label and a big real number with its unit. Line: the most striking real numbers of the whole build.
- Numbers on screen: every build shot's panel shows ONE real number with its unit about that step, and the SAME kind of quantity for every part when the topic allows (all heights, or all years, or all weights, or all build times), so the steps compare; for something that grew over time, the year of each stage. Written the way people read them (146 m, 2.5 t, 1,200 yrs, 1931). Every number must be real and come from the REFERENCE; if a value is not known, choose another quantity that is, never invent one.
- Titles and panels: lowercase titles of the real part names in the narration's words, at most 16 characters ("[n]. [part]"); panels at most 12 characters. Never a colour or material name as a title.
- Each shot "idea" starts with exact lines the animator copies: "TITLE: [text]", "PANEL: [text or none]" (stats: "CARDS: [label] / [number unit]; ..."), the finish also "PAYOFF: [word.]", and for build shots "BUILD: parts [n], since line [i or none], dur [seconds], lit [0 or 1], cut [0 or 1], slam [yes or no]", then the camera and what moves on each line.
- Lines: one narrator (speaker "narrator"; a friendly young American man, upbeat, medium pace). The request's word budget decides the length, and a shot takes the words its part needs (one to three lines). Plain, concrete, one new fact per line; numbers written as words. Say the subject's exact name in line 1. Never repeat a fact.
- Assets, 2 or 3:
  1. "build" (hero): the whole subject as a block model. Its description, in compact form, gives NX, NZ and NL, then each part in build order ("part 0 [name]: [material], layers 0-1, filled 9 x 6"): which layers, which cells (a ring, a filled floor, columns, rows, a stepped slab), its material; which blocks are lamp blocks; where its sign of working comes out and what it is; which part is the inner part (if any); and the last part, the finishing block (1 block, where it sits). Params: "parts" (number 0 to the number of parts including the finishing block: the parts shown; the last one shown is the one being built), "since" (number, seconds since that last part started dropping; missing means every part is already standing), "dur" (number, seconds that part takes to land, default 2), "lit" (number 0 to 1: lamps on and the sign of working), "cut" (number 0 to 1: the front half of the outer parts lifts away), "slam" (boolean: the last part shown is the finishing block and it drops in as the slam, with dust). States: {"parts": <all>, "lit": 1}, {"parts": 2, "since": 0.6, "dur": 1.5}, {"parts": <all>, "cut": 1}. Box w = NX * 64 + NZ * 20 + 160, h = (NL + 4) * 64 + NZ * 24 + 40.
  2. "unit": the one unit the subject is made of, as 1 to 8 blocks in its real proportions (a brick is 2 x 1 x 1 blocks), in its material, drawn so its larger side is about 320 px. Param "bob" (number 0 to 1: floats up to 24 px with its shadow shrinking). Box 400 x 400.
  3. Only if the subject was really built or run with one machine or helper the lines name (a crane, a ramp, a pump): that helper as a block model, built the same way. Otherwise no third asset.
  Particles, dust, smoke, glows, titles, panels and the terrain are drawn by the kit, the build asset or the shots, never as assets.
- Music: feel Bright Bouncy Uplifting, 100-120 bpm (the reference is silent: a light, playful build-up); payoff_shot = the finish shot (the music peaks as the finishing block lands). Sound effects: a knock when the first block of each part lands (once per build shot), a boom on the finishing block's slam, a pop when each stat card appears. Nothing else.

## assets
- Draw blocks ONLY through the kit: build a list of blocks and call kit.voxels (or kit.block for a single block); never draw your own block faces or textures. Particles are kit.cube.
- Canvas helpers: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); r()); h.rr and h.poly take no ctx.
- "build": in its own coordinates, block (0, 0, 0) of an NX x NZ footprint sits at X0 = -(NX * 64 + NZ * 20) / 2, Y0 = BH / 2 - 40, where BH is the box height you declared (the footprint's front-bottom edge, centred, 40 px above the bottom of the box). Never name a variable h: h is the helpers. List every block once as {x, y, z, m, part, n}: part = the index of its named part (0 = base), n = its order inside the part (the sweep: round a ring by angle, a floor from its centre outward, roof or deck rows snaking left and right, posts bottom to top, layer after layer). Make the list with loops (rings, filled rectangles, stepped slabs, columns), the same list on every call.
  - Each frame: blocks of parts below p.parts - 1 are standing; blocks of later parts are not drawn; each block of part p.parts - 1 lands at T = dur * n / Math.max(1, count - 1) and is drawn with kit.fall(p.since - T) (skip it while kit.fall returns null). When p.since is missing every shown block is standing. With p.slam the last shown part is the finishing block: it lands at T = dur and uses kit.fall(p.since - T, true); around its base a dust puff of 30 to 36 kit.cube cubes (12 to 24 px, L 0.72 to 0.94, C 0.01, H 80) bursts out over 0.5 to 0.85 s: each flies out along its own angle on an ellipse 1 wide by 0.375 tall (the ground plane) 60 to 200 px with easeOut, hops up to 40 px, and shrinks to nothing.
  - p.lit: pass lit * (1 + 0.05 * Math.sin(p.t * 29) + 0.035 * Math.sin(p.t * 61 + 1.3)) to kit.voxels (lamp blocks and their square glows, with a gentle flicker); while lit > 0 the sign of working runs from its place: a stream of 10 to 12 kit.cube cubes, one leaving every 0.11 s on a 1.3 s cycle (use (p.t - i * 0.11) % 1.3), each rising 180 to 220 px and drifting 40 to 60 px sideways, growing from 20 to 56 px, fading after half its life, its tone lightening from L 0.65 to 0.95 (C 0, smoke or steam; use the real colour of whatever comes out).
  - p.cut (0 to 1): blocks with z < NZ / 2 of every part except the base and the inner part rise by cut * 192 px and fade (a = 1 - cut); at cut 1 they are not drawn, so the exposed faces inside show (culling counts only drawn, settled blocks).
