# stick-figure

## about
A bright cartoon stick-figure "what if / why" explainer, 30 to 45 s: a question hook on a character, "picture this" drops the viewer into the scene as a stick person ("you"), a problem builds through slapstick, one clean diagram shot shows the mechanism with a real number, a real test proves it, and the last line flips the hook. Stick people with big white heads and expressive faces in a saturated, ink-outlined world that never stops moving. For everyday "why do people [do or wear or use this]?" questions, myths with a surprising real reason, history oddities, body quirks and "what would happen to you if...". Not for part-by-part machine breakdowns, comparisons of many items, data trends or long stories.

## look
- Flat 2D vector cartoon drawn in code: saturated flat fills, ONE ink colour for every outline, one hard-edged cel shadow per shape, additive glows for every light source, soft contact shadows under everything that stands. No textures, no noise, no gradients on objects (gradients only for skies, floors, glows and tints).
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() or ctx.stroke() after them.
- Ink: a very dark warm violet-grey (OKLCH lightness 0.24, chroma 0.014, hue about 326), never pure black. Outline weights: 5 px on props and scenery, 4 px on small details, 10 to 12 px on the big diagram object; stick people get theirs from h.stick. Clouds are the one exception: outlined in pale sky blue at 55%.
- Shading recipe for every object: base fill; then, clipped to the shape, a hard band of dark violet (rgba of the ink at 16 to 18%) over the right 30 to 40%; a thin pale strip (white at 18 to 25%) near the left edge; then the ink outline. Light comes from the top-left. Contact shadow: an ellipse 0.4 x 0.07 of the object's width, ink at 22%, under its base.
- Colour is a set of relations (OKLCH); keep them whatever the topic:
  - Characters are the most saturated things on screen: each shirt one pure primary, chroma 0.14 to 0.21. Hero shirt: warm red (lightness 0.60, chroma 0.21, hue about 28) with off-white stripes. Insider shirt: golden yellow (lightness 0.81, chroma 0.16, hue about 82), about 55 degrees round from the hero. The others (extras): strong blue (lightness 0.52, chroma 0.17, hue about 262), the hero's complement, with off-white stripes. Spare hues for hats: violet (lightness 0.53, chroma 0.18, hue about 298) and green (lightness 0.64, chroma 0.14, hue about 150). Heads and stripes: warm off-white (lightness 0.96, chroma 0.01, hue about 85).
  - Day world: warm ground under a cool sky, a complementary pair. Ground and wood: hue 50 to 65, lightness 0.62 far to 0.73 near, chroma about 0.11; its lines and planks lightness 0.45. Sky: hue about 243, lightness 0.70 and chroma 0.14 at the top, fading to lightness 0.92 and chroma 0.04 at the horizon. Far landmarks: 3 warm tones (lightness 0.88 / 0.78 / 0.66, chroma 0.08 to 0.11, hue 77 / 62 / 47 from light to dark) mixed 40 to 60% toward the sky colour the farther they are. Greenery hue about 146, lightness 0.67 with a 0.78 highlight. A water or open-ground band: hue 208 to 229, lightness 0.82 at the horizon to 0.56 at the bottom, chroma 0.08 to 0.11.
  - Dim inside: the same wood hues at lightness 0.37 to 0.46, lit by one warm light (lightness 0.84, chroma 0.14, hue about 76) added with 'lighter'.
  - Darkness: lightness 0.11 to 0.13, chroma 0.01, violet hue about 305, with a navy tint (hue 270) at 25%.
  - Diagram glow, hot: centre lightness 0.96 (chroma 0.09, hue 98), middle 0.86 (chroma 0.15, hue 85), edge 0.73 (chroma 0.16, hue 58). Cold: centre lightness 0.30 (chroma 0.08, hue 269), middle 0.23, edge 0.15 (chroma 0.03).
  - Accents: pop yellow (lightness 0.91, chroma 0.16, hue about 97) for numbers, "!" marks, stars, arrows and impact bursts; impact red (lightness 0.57 to 0.60, chroma 0.21, hue about 27) for an impact word and the stamp; tick green (lightness 0.61, chroma 0.15, hue about 148); sweat blue (lightness 0.85, chroma 0.09, hue about 233); paper cream (lightness 0.94, chroma 0.034, hue about 86) with dark brown writing (lightness 0.31, chroma 0.06, hue about 46).
  - palette names: ink, skin, hero, helper, others, sky, wood, night, glow, accent (pop yellow), accent2 (impact red), green, paper. Derive darker or lighter mixes of these, nothing else.
- Backgrounds (kit.bg), exactly these five names. Every background paints a 200 px margin beyond each edge (x -200 to 1280, y -200 to 2120) so camera moves and the whip never show an edge:
  - "outside": the topic's everyday place up close, by day. Back to front: (1) sky gradient from the top to the horizon at y 760; (2) a sun at about (900, 160), radius 70: an additive radial glow out to 4.5 radii, 12 thin ray wedges at 22% turning 0.15 rad/s, a pale disc with a slightly yellower inner disc; (3) 4 clouds, each 5 overlapping circles on a flat bottom, white with a pale blue band along the bottom, drifting right 4 to 6 px/s; (4) 5 or 6 tall far landmarks of the place on the horizon (cliffs, towers, trees, hills, buildings that fit the topic), each 3 tones with the dark band on the right, 6 to 9 thin horizontal detail lines, an outline, sorted far (hazier, smaller) to near; (5) at the horizon a band of the place's open ground or water with short sliding dashes; (6) across the frame a back wall, fence or railing of the place, top at y 860 to 1000, 130 to 150 px tall, posts every 160 px, a cap rail with a highlight strip; (7) from its foot to the bottom edge a floor in perspective: a gradient from the darker wood at the wall to the lighter wood at the bottom, 26 plank or tile lines converging to a vanishing point at (540, -1100), every third plank 7% lighter, staggered seam ticks across, a 14 px shadow along the wall's foot.
  - "far": the same world from far away, for the arrival. Sky and sun as above (sun at about (820, 330), radius 95), the landmarks with their bases at y 930, and from the horizon to the bottom edge the open band (water, field or plain): a gradient light to deep with 90 short curved white dashes (2 to 6 px, 55%) sliding sideways, the near ones larger and faster, and a 5 px bright line on the horizon. Leave room for small figures and vehicles to travel.
  - "inside": a dim interior of the topic's place: back wall of horizontal boards (lines every 70 px), vertical posts every 330 px (58 px wide, darker, outlined), a ceiling beam across the top, a darker floor from y 1330 with 30 lines converging toward the centre, a 20 px shadow along the floor line. ONE warm light: a light shaft from the top (a trapezoid 380 px wide at the top, 700 px at the floor, added with 'lighter', 50% at the top fading to 0) or a lamp glow (radial, 520 px), flickering by plus or minus 5%, with 15 dust motes drifting in it.
  - "black" (only when the topic really happens in the dark): darkness (mark it plain: true). Near-black over the whole frame with the "inside" posts and boards only 4 to 8% lighter than the ground, a navy tint at 25%, and 20 faint dust dots drifting.
  - "glow": the diagram stage. A full-frame radial gradient centred (540, 900), radius 1300, in the hot colours, with 16 thin white ray wedges from (540, 980) at 18%, turning 0.2 rad/s.
- kit.title draws text only (no box): kit.font bold (weight 800 to 900), fill pop yellow unless opts.color, an ink stroke 19% of the size drawn first (lineJoin round), and an entrance from opts.k: scale 0.6 to 1 with easeBack over 0.25 s. Type: kit.font "Selawik". The proof card's title and rows use Gelasio bold, set by the shot.
- kit.panel is the proof card only: paper cream, rounded 14 px, 8 px ink outline, a drop shadow (ink at 30%, offset 20 px down and right).
- kit.post: a soft vignette (radial from radius 500 around (540, 900) clear, to dark violet at 32% at radius 1250) and light grain (700 dots of 2 px, half white half ink, at 5%, reseeded each frame from Math.floor(lt * 30) % 4).

## motion
- Every shot has its own camera, applied to everything it draws: ctx.save(); translate to the focus point, scale by z, translate back, plus a handheld drift; ctx.restore() at the end. Default: a slow push-in, z from 1.0 to 1.04 to 1.10 over the shot (easeInOut). A light handheld drift (the look's signature; it may stay on while a payoff holds): dx = 3 * (Math.sin(lt * 4.6 + 2) * 0.6 + Math.sin(lt * 2.6 + 3.3) * 0.4), dy = 3 * (Math.sin(lt * 4.1 + 4.1) * 0.6 + Math.sin(lt * 2.1 + 2.4) * 0.4) px, and a roll of plus or minus 0.006 rad. Never zoom below 1.0.
- Punch-in on a reveal or a trick: z 1.0 to 1.6 to 1.75 over 1.2 s (easeInOut), centred on the face. The last shot opens punched in at 1.6 and pulls out to 1.0 over 2.4 s, then holds.
- Whip out: in the last 0.2 s of every shot except the last, the camera slides left by up to 180 px (easeIn) while 30 horizontal white streaks (3 to 8 px thick, 200 to 600 px long, 30%) sweep across the frame. The engine adds its own smear entering the next shot.
- Acting is keyframed with h.easeBack overshoot: anticipation (a 0.1 s dip) before a jump or a swing; on landing the figure squashes (squash 0.82 back to 1 over 0.25 s with easeBack) and two cream dust puffs (4 to 6 outlined circles each, ink outline, fading over 0.5 s) appear at the feet; a jump follows an arc with the hands_up pose; a run moves 600 to 900 px a second; a hand reaching to the face or an object takes 0.3 s; eyes dart left and right (look -1 to 1 in 0.15 s steps) when someone looks around; blinking is built in.
- An impact at time T: a jagged burst (24 points alternating radius 230 and about 125 px with h.rng jitter, pop-yellow fill, ink outline 16 px) with a smaller pale burst inside and the impact word on it in impact red with an ink stroke, scaling 0 to 0.62 with easeBack in 0.12 s and gone by T + 0.5 s; the camera shakes 30 px decaying to 0 over 0.25 s (sum of two sines as in the drift, 3 times faster) and rolls 0.03 rad; a full-frame warm flash, rgba of the warm light at 35% fading to 0 over 0.17 s; then 3 pop-yellow five-point stars orbiting the head (an ellipse 190 by 45 px, one turn a second).
- Pop-ins: scale 0 to 1 with easeBack over 0.25 to 0.3 s. A number lands with a 25% scale pop. A reveal gets a white four-point sparkle (two thin diamonds) that grows and shrinks over 0.5 s.
- Small life: sweat drops (sweat-blue teardrops with an ink outline sliding down 50 px a second beside the head), yellow "!" marks (120 px, ink stroke 14 px) above heads for 0.55 s when panic starts, dust motes in light shafts, clouds and dashes sliding in the backgrounds.
- Darkness reveal (seeing in the dark): a circular hole opening from the centre, radius 0 to 1500 px over 2.8 s (easeIn), drawn as ONE radial gradient over the scene (transparent inside the radius, near-black at 96% from radius + 260 px out) with a thin light-blue ring at its edge fading as it grows. Never a solid fill.
- Hold every payoff (the number, the trick, the stamp, the last line) still and readable for at least 0.8 s, with only the drift and small life moving.

## plan
- Structure: 8 or 9 shots in this usual order, usually one line each, every shot naming its beat. The narrator talks to the viewer as "you"; the viewer's stand-in is the hero on screen. The beats follow the TOPIC: who and what appears comes from the brief's real situation, never from another story (no crowd, chase, darkness or disguise unless the topic really has one).
  1. hook: one real question that ends with "?" about something everyone has seen or felt ("Why do [people or things] [the odd thing]?", "What really happens when [everyday action]?"). It must be TRUE as asked: never a premise the facts contradict (no "what if X had nothing to do with Y" when it does). On screen: the hero in a giant close-up (head and chest fill the frame) meeting the thing, curious or alarmed, the place blurred behind; on the line's last words a reaction (eyes wide, a "?" pop, a sparkle).
  2. picture: "Picture this. You [are in the everyday situation, specific place and moment]." On screen: the place, the hero arriving (walk) and starting the everyday action with the thing.
  3. problem: what you notice happening to you, said plainly and a little funny ("[the symptom], and [the funny consequence]"). On screen: it happening to the hero as cartoon slapstick: the reaction pose and face, sweat drops, stars, a burst, shake and flash on the hit. Other people (h.stick extras) only if the topic really has them.
  4. mechanism (1 or 2 shots): what really happens, one step per line, in plain words ("[The cause] [does this], which [does that]"). On screen: the "glow" stage, the mechanism asset big in the middle changing state across the line (p.state 0 to 1), with small arrows or particles showing the step. ONE of these shots carries the reel's one real number with its unit (a clock or counter icon above, the number counting up to its real value beside it); the only number on screen.
  5. effect: what that does to you, in one line ("That's why your [body part] [reacts]"). On screen: back in the place, a medium shot of the hero's reaction, the cause (particles, a beam, a wave) visibly reaching them.
  6. turn: "But [the trick, the fix or the surprising part]." On screen: the helper (someone who would know: a cook, a doctor, a mechanic, a grandparent; drawn with h.stick) does the trick in warm light with an easeBack snap and a sparkle, the hero watching; or the hero does it, if no helper fits.
  7. proof (optional, only with a real test, study or record from the REFERENCE): "[Who] tested it, and [found the real result]." On screen: the hero (450 to 600 px tall) beside the proof card: the idea's name as a 2 to 4 word title, 3 rows restating the reel's steps (3 to 5 words each) ticked one by one, then a stamp with the one-word real verdict. Without a real test, drop this beat.
  8. payoff: answers the hook in plain words with a wry twist ("So [the plain answer], and [the small win you now have]"). On screen: the hero doing the trick, triumphant, the helper beside them, a wink and a sparkle on the last words; opens punched in, pulls out.
- Required: hook, picture, problem, mechanism, turn, payoff. Drop proof first when there is no real test, then the second mechanism shot.
- Lines: conversational second person, one new fact per line, never a fact twice, never a line that only announces ("the documented mechanism explains why..."): every line says the actual thing. Numbers in spoken lines are written as words; the on-screen number is digits with its unit. The number is real and standard (from the REFERENCE); never invent a number, a test or a verdict.
- Lines are delivered with tones that follow the story: the hook curious, picture playful, problem urgent then wry, mechanism matter-of-fact, turn playful or confident, proof excited, payoff wry and warm.
- Narrator: one narrator (speaker "narrator"; a laid-back, confident American man, the closest to the reference's voice), brisk at about 3 words a second.
- Cast on screen: the hero ("you") and the helper (who knows the trick); extras only when the topic has other people. Only the narrator speaks.
- Assets, exactly 3, in this order:
  1. hero, kind "character": the viewer's stand-in, a stick person drawn with h.stick: hero shirt colour with off-white stripes, a hat that fits the place (hair, cap or captain; bandana or sailor only when the topic is at sea), a belt. Box 560 x 900 (680 x 900 if any pose points). Params: face (one of h.stick's faces), look (number -1 to 1, where the eyes look sideways), flip (boolean), dark (boolean: silhouette with glowing eyes), squash (number 0.8 to 1.1). In every shot idea describe the body pose on each line with h.stick's pose words (idle, walk, run, point, wave, hands up, cheer, hands on hips, shrug, think, facepalm, sit, reaching to the face, holding [thing]), so the pose sheet uses them.
  2. helper, kind "character": built the same way, helper shirt colour (no stripes), its own hat and hat colour, holding the tool or thing of the trick (worn only if it really is worn), with a string param "item" listing its 2 or 3 states (for example held, used, shown) plus the same face, look, flip, dark and squash params.
  3. mechanism, kind "prop": the one object whose change of state explains the answer (a body part, a device, a material, a plant), drawn as a big diagram in the same cartoon style: its silhouette in simple shapes, a cut or cross-section only if it explains, a number param "state" from 0 to 1 that animates the change (opening, filling, growing, heating). Box about 760 x 560. No text inside.
- Extras (only when the topic has other people) are NOT an asset: shots draw them with h.stick directly (others' shirt colour with off-white stripes, one shared hat). Never draw a sign, board, card or label without its words (an empty board reads as a mistake). Props the characters use (a light, a tool, a sign, a rope, a door, a hole) are drawn by the shots.
- Each shot idea gives: background(s), who is on screen, where and how tall (px), their pose and face on each line, the camera (push-in, punch-in, pull-out), the visual beat and the payoff; for the mechanism the number and unit; for the proof the card title, the 3 rows and the verdict word; for an impact, its 3 to 6 letter impact word.
- Music: feel Bouncy, Humorous, Bright (a plucked, playful underscore), 125 to 145 bpm (measured about 140; the reference's own track is a 132 bpm jig); payoff_shot is the turn shot, where the music swells into its bright section.
- Sound effects: dense, cartoon, each exactly on its visual hit (the reference has about 15 audible hits in 37 s): a whoosh on every whip, a pop on every pop-in (a number landing, a tick, a sparkle, "!" marks), a knock on landings and footsteps, a boom on each impact and on the stamp. About 2 per shot.

## assets
- Every character asset is drawn with h.stick, never by hand: const hgt = <the box height>; const r = h.stick(0, hgt / 2, hgt * 0.95, o); with o = {pose, face: p.face || 'smile', t: p.t, mouth: p.mouth, look: [p.look || 0, 0], flip: p.flip, squash: p.squash || 1, shirt, stripe, belt, hat, hatColour}. Shirt, stripe, hat and hatColour come from the kit palette and stay fixed for that character, so every pose is the same person.
- Map p.pose to h.stick's named poses: a pose named like one of them uses it (idle, walk, run, point, wave, cheer, hips, shrug, think, facepalm, sit; hands_up becomes 'handsUp'). Any other pose uses the nearest named pose plus a reach: o.reachR (or reachL) = [dx, dy] px from the feet for that hand (the face is at about [0, -0.77 * size], the top of the head at [0, -0.96 * size], the chest at [0, -0.45 * size]). A landing pose adds squash 0.85, a running pose lean 0.12, a lying pose rotates the whole figure 90 degrees around its middle.
- What a character holds or wears is drawn AFTER h.stick at the returned points: r.handR / r.handL for held things (a light, a tool, a sign), r.head and r.top for worn things (hat extras, the helper's item), in the same style: flat fill, cel band, 5 px ink outline, a small highlight.
- p.dark: o.silhouette = the night colour lifted by 2 to 4% (so the figure is barely visible against the "black" background) and o.eyesGlow = true: only the white eyes show.
- The helper's item follows p.item through its states and sits exactly at the head or hand point in every pose.
- The mechanism asset: a big readable silhouette (fills 85 to 95% of the box width), 10 to 12 px ink outlines, flat fills with one cel band, a few internal "read" details (rings, radial lines, chambers, fibres) and 2 white highlights; p.state from 0 to 1 changes it smoothly (a size, an opening, a level, a colour mixed between two palette colours). No text, no background, no clock or number (the shot draws those).
- Stay inside the box: characters stand on its bottom edge; the box is 560 to 680 px wide by 900 px tall so arms and held things fit.

## shots
- Draw order: ctx.save(); camera transform; kit.bg; far props; characters back to front (smaller and higher further back); near props; effects (bursts, puffs, sparkles, stars); in-scene text; ctx.restore(); then the whip streaks in the last 0.2 s.
- Sizes (px on 1080 x 1920), feet on the floor line between y 1300 and 1460 unless stated:
  - Medium shot: the hero 700 to 900 px tall (h.asset scale 0.78 to 1.0 on a 900 px box), centred around x 400 to 680.
  - Giant close-up (hook, punch-ins): the figure 1500 to 1700 px tall with its feet at y 2000 to 2150, below the frame, so the head and chest fill y 450 to 1400. Behind it, kit.bg drawn with ctx.filter = 'blur(5px)' (set ctx.filter = 'none' right after).
  - Dark close-up: a silhouette 2200 to 2600 px tall, head centred at about (540, 950), only the glowing eyes and a sweat drop visible.
  - Wide and "far" views: figures 250 to 450 px tall; the others 450 to 550 px at different depths, the front ones lower and bigger.
  - A figure lying knocked out: rotated 90 degrees, 380 to 420 px long, on the floor at y 1700 to 1850, with circling stars.
- Keep faces, the number and the card out of y 1480 to 1640 (the word captions pop in at y 1560). Bodies and floors may run behind it.
- Extras, when the topic has them: drawn with h.stick directly, the others' shirt with off-white stripes and one shared hat, 1 to 3 of them, each with its own face and timing (pass seed 1, 2, 3 so they blink apart).
- Mechanism layout: the mechanism asset centred at (540, 1010), 640 to 760 px wide, its state animated over the line; a clock or counter icon centred at (540, 470), radius 165 (outlined disc in pop yellow mixed toward paper, a face with 12 ticks, hands, a filled wedge in sky blue at 35% that grows with the count), popping in with easeBack; beside it the number with its unit, kit.title 92 px, pop yellow with ink stroke, centred at x 800 to 830, y 510, tilted 0.06 rad, counting from 0 to the real value over 0.9 s (easeOut) and popping 25% when it lands. Arrows (pop yellow, 10 px) show the direction of the change. Nothing else on screen.
- Proof layout: the "inside" background; the card kit.panel(ctx, 130, 290, 820, 1140), tilted -0.04 rad around its centre; the title in Gelasio bold 96 to 104 px, dark brown, at y 380 and 490 (one or two lines), a 6 px underline at y 560; a simple doodle icon of the idea at (540, 790), radius 110; 3 rows at y 1060, 1160 and 1260: a 64 px rounded checkbox at x 200 and the row text in Gelasio 52 px left-aligned at x 300; ticks (tick green, 14 px stroke) pop in 0.35 s apart with easeBack. Then the stamp: a wooden handle and red block slam down from above the frame (easeIn, 0.55 s), and where it hits, the verdict word in impact red, 120 to 128 px bold, rotated -0.22 rad, inside a 22 px rounded double border about 760 x 210, centred at (560, 800) (over the doodle, clear of the title and the rows), with 70 small holes knocked out of the ink (destination-out dots from h.rng) and two cream dust puffs, plus the impact shake.
- The impact word is 3 to 6 letters, a sound (never a word from the line); at most one in-scene word or number group on screen at a time, all inside x 60 to 1020 and y 180 to 1440. Never write the narration on screen: the engine captions it.
- Shots with darkness use the "black" background; a darkness or tint over a lit place is a radial gradient or under 50% opacity, never a solid fill. Never paint a solid colour over kit.bg.
- A shot may cut once inside itself to a closer view (switch the background and staging at a word of its line, with 0.15 s of whip streaks), as the picture shot does; otherwise one place per shot.
- Motion shows something new: a character acting (a new face, pose or move when a line gives one), effects on a hit; the drift and background life are optional.

## director
- Flag: a character not drawn with h.stick, or looking different from shot to shot (shirt, stripes, hat); characters too small to read their faces in a medium shot (under 600 px tall); a still frame (no drift, no acting, no background life); a missing impact (burst, shake and flash) where the line has a hit; a mechanism shot without its big object, its icon or its real number with a unit, or with other clutter; a proof card that is empty, overflowing, or whose stamp covers its title or rows; text inside y 1480 to 1640 or outside x 60 to 1020; the narration written on screen; a solid fill over the background; darkness drawn as a flat black rectangle over a room; a payoff line that does not flip the hook.
- Check the mechanism number and the proof against the reference and standard knowledge; remove a test, a verdict or a number rather than keep a doubtful one, and hedge theories.
