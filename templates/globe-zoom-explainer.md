# globe-zoom-explainer

## about
A dark-space globe explainer driven by one continuous camera over a real sphere: the Earth with its real map, or another planet or moon drawn as itself (its colours, bands, craters, clouds, ice caps and rings). It opens on the whole body floating in dark space; the camera then moves only to show something new: down onto a place (a striped marker drops on it and it is named, with the land or features around it still in view), out to frame several places together, along a measurement, or out to the body on its orbit around its star or planet. Each measurement grows on the sphere (a line, a ring, a parallel or meridian) while a big counter rolls up to the real value with its unit, and the answer puts the key numbers side by side at the top in gold and ice blue over what they measure. For places, positions, distances and sizes on the Earth, and for one planet or moon at a time: coordinates, extremes (furthest, highest, deepest, remotest), routes, time zones, map distortion, where things are; a body's size, surface features, day, year, tilt and orbit. Not for chains of many things compared by size or distance (how big is X next to Y next to Z), journeys through space, stars, galaxies or black holes as the subject, machines, history without a map, biology or character stories.

## look
- Clean above all: the frame holds the body (whole, or zoomed so its surface fills the frame), or in an orbit view the body, what it circles and the orbit; the markers, lines and rings the current point needs and their labels (each one something the line talks about; places the line names together are shown together); and the counter row or the title at the top. Nothing else: no panels, cards, boxes, legends, compasses, icons, free-floating arrows or insets.
- Everything on a body is placed by its real longitude and latitude through the kit's projection (kit.geo, below), so the camera can zoom and spin and every marker, line and label stays on its place. The Earth's land, coasts and borders are the engine's real map; another body's look is its bands and spots at their real latitudes, longitudes and sizes; both are drawn by h.planet. Never draw a coastline, a continent or a feature's outline by hand.
- h.planet(v, o) is the engine's body renderer (the same projection as kit.geo): it draws a planet, moon or star as a lit sphere at the view v = {x, y, r, lon0, lat0}, inside the current transform, and returns nothing. o = {base (its base colour; the Earth's ocean), bands ([[lat1, lat2, colour, alpha], ...]: belts and zones, an ice cap reaching latitude 90 or -90), spots ([[lon, lat, radius in degrees, colour, alpha], ...]: craters, maria, storms, volcanoes, cloud patches), soft (degrees of soft edge on bands and spots: 2 to 4 for clouds and gas bands, 0 for craters), land, coast, borders (colours: the Earth's real map, only for the Earth), atmo (its air's colour: a glow on the rim and a halo; halo is its width in body radii, default 0.08, a star's glow 0.3 to 0.6; no atmo for a body without air), rings ([[inner, outer, colour, alpha], ...] in body radii, only for a body that really has rings), light ([x, y, z] toward the light: x right, y down, z toward the viewer; the default lights it from the upper left and front; false for a star, which shines itself), night (how dark the night side is, default 0.65)}. Colours are hex strings.
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() or ctx.stroke() after them. h.asset('id', x, y, scale, params) takes NO ctx either (its first argument is the asset id string). Never pass a maxWidth to fillText (squeezed text is rejected): shrink the font until the text fits instead.
- After kit.bg, never paint a solid rectangle over the frame or a large part of it: kit.bg is the whole background and the bodies sit on it.
- Colour is a set of relations (OKLCH); keep them, whatever the topic:
  - Space: near-black navy (lightness 0.11 to 0.14, chroma about 0.04, hue about 265), a soft glow behind the body up to lightness 0.20.
  - The Earth: ocean deep navy blue (lightness 0.22 to 0.26, chroma 0.07 to 0.08, hue about 273); land warm dark grey-taupe, nearly neutral (lightness 0.34, never above 0.36, chroma 0.012, hue about 60); coasts a thin lighter edge (lightness 0.48 at 70%); country borders thin cream lines at 25% opacity; air a light blue-grey (lightness 0.50 to 0.55, chroma 0.06, hue about 260).
  - Another body: its real colours from the reference, simplified to a base and 2 to 4 tones of it (lightness 0.35 to 0.85, so it reads on the navy), never the Earth's blue ocean and grey land; its air, if it has any, in its real tint (a thick haze: halo 0.1 to 0.14). A star: its real colour, bright, with a wide glow.
  - Graticule (latitude and longitude lines): cream at 10 to 16% opacity.
  - Measurement colours: GOLD (lightness 0.87, chroma 0.13, hue about 85) for the first key quantity, its line or ring and its counter; ICE BLUE (lightness 0.86, chroma 0.07, hue about 235) for the second; ORANGE (lightness 0.77, chroma 0.15, hue about 61) for the catch (a correcting or contrasting number); WHITE (lightness 0.98) for the familiar comparison and for labels and titles. Never more than these four on screen.
  - Marker: candy stripes of RED (lightness 0.60, chroma 0.20, hue about 29) and cream (lightness 0.95), a small ORANGE flag.
  - Ink (for text shadows, outlines and line casings): lightness 0.10, hue 265.
  - palette.accent is GOLD (the engine colours the caption bar with it); palette.accent2 ICE BLUE; palette.orange; palette.white; palette.bg the space navy; palette.ink; palette.red; palette.cream; palette.body the main body's base colour and palette.body2 its main tone (the Earth: palette.ocean and palette.land instead).
- Backgrounds (kit.bg), 2 of them:
  - "space": the space navy with a radial glow centred at (540, 960) of radius 900 (lightness 0.30 at the centre, 0.20 at radius 500, 0.10 at the corners: the engine rejects a background whose brightness varies too little), a vignette, and 80 to 110 star dots (2 to 4 px, cream at 25 to 60% opacity) at seeded positions that twinkle (opacity breathing with lt, each star its own phase) and drift 10 px per second; the glow breathes (radius +- 6% over 4 s).
  - "space_far": the same glow lightness (0.30 at the centre to 0.10 at the corners) with radius 700, and the same stars, used when the body is small (pulled back, or the orbit view).
- Type: kit.font(size, weight) returns `${weight} ${size}px "Selawik"`; counters use weight 900, labels and titles 800. Water and ocean names use ctx.font = `italic 600 ${size}px Gelasio`.
- kit.title(ctx, text, x, y, o) draws white uppercase text (weight 800, o.size default 76) with an 8 px ink stroke under it (strokeText, lineJoin round) and a soft ink shadow; it shrinks the size until the measured text is at most 900 px wide; o.k pops it in (scale 0.85 to 1 with easeBack over 0.25 s, opacity 0 to 1 over 0.15 s).
- kit.panel(ctx, x, y, w, hgt, o) draws a small dark pill: ink at 60% opacity, corners hgt / 2, a 2 px ICE BLUE border at 50%. The shots never use it under labels, counters or titles (those carry their own ink stroke).
- The kit also returns these helpers (the shots and the body assets call them; write them exactly). Shapes are the same everywhere: a CAMERA is always an object {lon0, lat0, s}; a VIEW is always an object {x, y, r, lon0, lat0}; a POINT is always an array ([lon, lat] on a body, [x, y, z] on screen). Never index an object (c[0]) or read a field of an array (p.x).
  - kit.view(c) returns the view {x: 540, y: 960, r: 500 * c.s, lon0: c.lon0, lat0: c.lat0} of the camera c: the main body drawn with h.asset('globe', 540, 960, c.s, {lon0: c.lon0, lat0: c.lat0, zoom: c.s}). The place at (lon0, lat0) is always at the frame point (540, 960).
  - kit.geo = h.geo (the engine's orthographic projection, the same one h.planet uses): kit.geo(lon, lat, v) returns [x, y, z]; z > 0 means the point faces the viewer, z <= 0 that it is behind the body. Never write your own projection.
  - kit.dist(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2, R = 6371) = h.geoDist(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2, R): the great-circle distance in km on a body of radius R km (the Earth's by default; a shot on another body passes the radius its idea's BODY line gives).
  - kit.frame(pts) returns the camera {lon0, lat0, s} that shows every [lon, lat] point of pts together inside the clear part of the frame: lon0 and lat0 are the points' centre (add up their 3D unit vectors [cos(lat) cos(lon), cos(lat) sin(lon), sin(lat)], then lon0 = atan2(y, x) and lat0 = asin(z / the sum's length), in degrees); then, with each point projected by kit.geo(lon, lat, kit.view({lon0, lat0, s: 1})) to [x, y, z], s = Math.min(8, 430 / max |x - 540|, 400 / max |y - 960|) (a max of 0 counts as 0.001), never below 0.95, and 0.95 when any point has z < 0.15 (they need the whole body).
  - kit.cam(lt, A, B, t0 = 0, dur = 1.2) returns the camera {lon0, lat0, s} moving from camera A to camera B between lt = t0 and t0 + dur, e = h.easeInOut of the progress (0 before, 1 after). lon0 and lat0 are interpolated with e (lon0 the short way round: if B.lon0 - A.lon0 > 180 subtract 360, if < -180 add 360). s = A.s + (B.s - A.s) * e; when the two centres are more than 40 degrees apart, the camera pulls back through the whole body: s = s + (Math.min(1, A.s, B.s) - (A.s + B.s) / 2) * Math.sin(Math.PI * e). After the move (lt > t0 + dur) the camera holds B.
  - kit.geoLine(ctx, pts, v, o): draws a line through pts (an array of [lon, lat]), each projected with kit.geo; a point with z <= 0 breaks the line (start a new sub-path at the next visible point). o = {color, width (default 6), k (0 to 1, default 1: how much of the line is drawn, from its first point; draw the first k share of the points), dash (optional [a, b]), glow (default true)}. lineCap and lineJoin round. First an ink casing (the same path, width + 6, ink at 55%) so the line reads on a pale body; with glow, the same path at width * 4 at 10% opacity and width * 2 at 25% (breathing +- 5% with a 1.5 s period); then the core at full opacity.
  - kit.ring(lon, lat, km, R = 6371, n = 120) returns n + 1 [lon, lat] points: the circle of every point km kilometres from (lon, lat) on a body of radius R km (the Earth's by default; a shot on another body passes its radius). For each bearing b = 2 * PI * i / n, with dd = km / R and p = lat in radians: p2 = asin(sin(p) * cos(dd) + cos(p) * sin(dd) * cos(b)); lon2 = lon + atan2(sin(b) * sin(dd) * cos(p), cos(dd) - sin(p) * sin(p2)) in degrees; lat2 = p2 in degrees.
  - kit.arc(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2, n = 64) returns n + 1 [lon, lat] points along the great circle between the two places (convert both to 3D unit vectors, interpolate with slerp, convert back with lat = asin(z), lon = atan2(y, x)).
  - kit.parallel(lat) returns the points of the latitude line at lat (lon from -180 to 180 in 2-degree steps); kit.meridian(lon) the points of the longitude line at lon (lat from -90 to 90 in 2-degree steps).
  - kit.counter(ctx, value, unit, x, y, o): a big number. o = {color (default palette.accent), size (default 120), decimals (default 0), k, maxW (default 900)}. The value is written with thousands commas and o.decimals decimals (value.toLocaleString('en-US', {minimumFractionDigits: dec, maximumFractionDigits: dec})), in kit.font(size, 900); the unit after it in kit.font(size * 0.42, 800), uppercase, 14 px gap, both on one baseline. A unit that starts with "°" (°N, °S, °E, °W) is drawn differently: the number and the "°" are ONE text (value + "°") in kit.font(size, 900); measure that whole text, then draw the hemisphere letter in kit.font(size * 0.6, 900) starting 10 px after the measured end (the letter never overlaps the "°"). Measure both parts; if the total width is over o.maxW shrink size until it fits. The whole line (number and unit together) is CENTRED on (x, y) (textBaseline 'middle' for the number; the unit's baseline matches the number's). Each part gets a 10 px ink stroke under the fill (strokeText, lineJoin round). o.k = seconds since the number landed on its final value: when k is between 0 and 0.25 the line pops (scale 1.15 to 1 with easeOut). The SHOT passes the rolling value.
  - kit.label(ctx, text, x, y, o): a place or body name, with NO panel or pill behind it. o = {kind: 'place' (white uppercase, kit.font(40, 800), ctx.letterSpacing = '3px', 6 px ink stroke under it) or 'water' (italic Gelasio 46 px, ICE BLUE, mixed case, 6 px ink stroke), align ('left' or 'right' or 'center'), k (pop in: scale 0.85 to 1 with easeBack over 0.25 s)}. The whole text is ONE strokeText and ONE fillText call (never letter by letter); reset ctx.letterSpacing to '0px' after.

## motion
- One continuous camera: every shot starts from the previous shot's camera, and the camera moves only to show something new (the next place, places framed together, a measurement framed whole, the whole body): kit.cam from the previous camera to the shot's own over its first 0.8 to 1.6 s, then it holds. A shot that stays on the same view keeps the camera still (A = B). A move between places more than 40 degrees apart pulls back through the whole body, spins, and zooms back in (kit.cam does it). The orbit view is reached by a cut, not a camera move.
- A body turns only when its turning is the point (a day, a rotation), and travels its orbit only when its path is the point (a year, a season): at a speed the eye can follow, while the line talks about it, then it holds.
- Things arrive on the word that names them, one at a time, 0.3 to 0.6 s apart: a marker drops (falls 80 px with easeBack over 0.35 s, its foot ring grows from 0 to full with easeOut), a line draws itself from its first point (k from 0 to 1 over 0.6 to 1.0 s, easeOut), a ring sweeps round (k from 0 to 1 over 0.8 s), a label pops (scale 0.85 to 1, easeBack, 0.25 s).
- Counters roll: the number counts up from 0 (or from the previous value of the same colour) to its real value over 1.0 to 1.4 s (easeOut) while what it measures appears (its line or ring draws, or the body turns or travels the span it counts), lands on the word that says it, pops, and then holds still and readable while that shows (at least 0.8 s). It leaves with it (fade over 0.3 s).
- When a line or ring is drawn, two small node dots (12 px white discs with a 3 px ink ring) sit on its ends or where it touches the thing it measures to, each with a soft ring pulse (a 2 px circle growing from 12 to 40 px and fading over 1 s, repeating).
- Quiet life between changes: the marker's flag waves, glowing lines breathe, the stars twinkle.
- Easing: easeInOut for the camera, easeOut for things arriving, easeIn for leaving, easeBack for pops.

## story
- The fact sheet holds every number a shot measures or counts, each real and with its unit, from the reference: the key quantity the answer turns on (GOLD); the second quantity it is read against when the answer sets two numbers side by side (ICE BLUE: the same measure for a second place or body, or the same place's second measure); a familiar comparison that makes the scale felt (WHITE), when the story uses one; and the catch (ORANGE), only when the topic has a real one. Each quantity is one fact with one value: never the same number in two colours.
- For a body other than the Earth the sheet also holds its mean radius in km and the real values the story compares (its day, its year, its tilt, its distance from what it circles), each from the reference.
- Every place the reel visits is on the sheet with its real coordinates on its body.
- The story sets the shots: as many as it needs, in the order that explains best, each one of the shot kinds under plan. A kind the story does not need is left out, and a kind may come back (two measures, two places).
- The cast has one speaker, "narrator" (voice chris: a casual American storyteller, male, fast and lively like the reference). Conversational and concrete, numbers written as words; a line says the number its shot counts, and a counter shows only in the shot whose line says its number (and in the answer).

## plan
- First decide the BODY, the PLACES and the NUMBERS, all real and from the fact sheet:
  - BODY: the body the reel is about ("Earth" for anything on the Earth) with its mean radius in km, and for any other body its real look in h.planet's terms for the artist: base colour; bands (latitude ranges and colours: belts, zones, ice caps); spots (craters, maria, storms, volcanoes: real positions and sizes in degrees where the reference gives them); a soft edge for gas and clouds; its air's colour (none for an airless body); rings only if it really has them (inner and outer radius in body radii). Each other body the story shows on screen (the star it circles, a moon, a planet it is compared with) gets the same; a star gets its colour and glow and no shading.
  - PLACES: the places the reel visits, each with its name and real coordinates on its body (longitude and latitude in degrees to 1 decimal, east and north positive, west and south negative; a longitude given from 0 to 360 east is minus 360 when above 180).
  - NUMBERS: each colour is one quantity from the fact sheet with its unit (KM, KM², °N, °S, °E, °W, H, M, DAYS, YEARS); only the colours the story uses. When the answer shows GOLD and ICE BLUE side by side they belong together: the two measures of the same place, or the same quantity for two places or bodies; never one place's first measure beside another place's second measure.
- Shot kinds (each shot's beat; the story picks which, how many and in what order):
  - hook: the whole body (s 0.95) turning to the subject (the first shot's camera starts 30 degrees east of its target); ONE thing glows GOLD: the line, ring or place the reel is about, or the body itself under its name as the TITLE. Line: a surprising true fact with a real number, or a question that ends with "?", naming the brief's subject.
  - locate: the camera moves down onto a place, but only as far as the frame still shows what tells the viewer where it is: B = kit.frame of the place and the nearest land, coasts or features around it (the ones its lines name, else the nearest real ones); never a frame of only empty ocean or blank surface. The marker drops on the place; its name is the TITLE (subline: the country, the body, or what kind of place it is); a LABEL on each neighbour shown.
  - measure: a line, ring, parallel or meridian grows on the body while its counter rolls to the real value; the camera frames the whole measurement (B = kit.frame of its points), so both ends of a line or the whole ring are in the frame. Line: says that number.
  - compare: the WHITE comparison drawn on the same body (a line between two well-known places, or one unit of the measure marked as a short line) with its WHITE counter, so the viewer feels the scale.
  - together: the places a line names together, framed together (B = kit.frame of all of them), each with its marker or node dot and LABEL, with the LINES the line talks about; counters only for the numbers this line says.
  - spin: the body turning on its axis (a day, a rotation): the camera holds while the body's lon0 changes with the turned angle (decreasing for a body that spins west to east, increasing for a backward spin), a marker on its surface riding round with it, its counter counting the time.
  - orbit: the body on its path around its star or planet (the orbit view, under shots): the body travels along its orbit while the line says so (a year is once round), its counter counting the time.
  - turn: the catch in ORANGE: the camera moves to where the catch lives; its line or ring and its counter. A catch is a real number that changes how the others read (the same measure is very different somewhere else; a number that disproves a common belief; where the measure stops working). A fact that only adds a name is not a catch.
  - answer: the key numbers side by side at the top (GOLD left, ICE BLUE right) over what they measure: their lines or rings, the camera framing them all (kit.frame), or the body or bodies whose turn and orbit they count, in the view that shows both; with one key number, its counter over its measurement. Line: what the numbers mean together. This is the music's payoff_shot.
  - close: the camera returns to the hook's view (the hook's camera B); the line lands the takeaway, ideally echoing the hook. Counters only if this line says their numbers.
- Every shot idea starts with these lines, then says what moves on which line:
  - On a body other than the Earth: "BODY: Name, radius [R] km" (the shot passes R to kit.dist and kit.ring).
  - "CAMERA: from {lon0, lat0, s} to {lon0, lat0, s} at 0 s over 1.2 s" (every shot but the first starts where the previous one ended; a shot that stays writes "CAMERA: hold {lon0, lat0, s}"). A framing camera: "CAMERA: from {lon0, lat0, s}, framing Name [lon, lat], Name [lon, lat], ..." (the shot uses kit.frame of those points). The orbit view: "CAMERA: ORBIT of Name around Name". Scale guide: the whole body 0.95; a continent 1.6 to 2.2; a country 3 to 5; a city's surroundings 6 to 8 (never above 8).
  - "OVERLAYS:" each thing on the body with its exact numbers, every place written as its name followed by its real coordinates: MARKER at Name [lon, lat] (pole on land, buoy at sea); LINE from Name [lon, lat] to Name [lon, lat] in [colour]; RING of [N] km around Name [lon, lat] in [colour]; PARALLEL at latitude [N] in [colour]; MERIDIAN at longitude [N] in [colour]; LABEL "[TEXT]" at Name [lon, lat] (place or water); in the orbit view, BODY Name on its orbit from [a0] to [a1] degrees. The engine checks every named place's coordinates and every LINE's km counter against the real ones and sends the plan back when they are wrong.
  - "TOP:" TITLE "[NAME]" / "[SUBLINE]", or COUNTER [colour] [value] [unit], or COUNTERS gold [value] [unit] and ice blue [value] [unit] (the answer), or nothing.
- Text: titles at most 16 characters, sublines at most 24, labels at most 14, all uppercase except water names; labels name places and bodies only (numbers live in the counters); counters are digits with their unit, the way people read them: thousands with commas, degrees with one decimal and the hemisphere letter as the unit.
- Every number on screen and in the lines must be real and agree with its counter; the coordinates in the OVERLAYS must be the places' real coordinates on their body. Read every line as a geography or astronomy teacher would: when a line says how something is measured or drawn, it must be the real way (which line, from where, in which direction), and a range must be its real range.
- A LINE joins only places whose distance or relation is the point of that shot; never a decorative line between unrelated places.
- Assets: "globe" (the hero: the BODY) and "marker" first, then one asset per other body the story shows on screen (the star it circles, a moon, a planet it is compared with), named for the body. Everything else (lines, rings, node dots, the orbit line, labels, counters, titles) is drawn by the shots with the kit's helpers.
- Music: Bright, Driving (a light curious pulse under a fast voice), 95 to 110 bpm; payoff_shot = the answer shot. Sound effects: a pop on each counter landing and each marker drop (about 2 per shot, each exactly on its visual hit); a whoosh only on a pull-back through the whole body.

## assets
- "globe" (kind "scenery", hero, animated false; w and h 1100, or 1000 * (1 + its halo) + 100 for a halo wider than 0.05; a body with rings: w = 1000 * its outer ring radius + 100): the BODY as a sphere of radius 500 centred on (0, 0) of its box. Params lon0 and lat0 (the place facing the viewer, degrees, default 0), zoom (the scale the shot draws it at, default 1; keep every stroke width divided by zoom so lines stay the same on screen), grid (0 to 1, the graticule's opacity factor, default 1) and light (optional, passed on to h.planet). Draw:
  1. const v = {x: 0, y: 0, r: 500, lon0: p.lon0 || 0, lat0: p.lat0 || 0}; h.planet(v, look), with the BODY's look written out as numbers and palette colours. The Earth: {base: palette.ocean, land: palette.land, coast: the coast colour at 70%, borders: cream at 25%, atmo: the air colour, light: p.light}. Another body: {base, bands, spots, soft, rings, atmo, light: p.light} from the plan's BODY line, and no land, coast or borders (those are the Earth's map).
  2. Graticule: every 15 degrees of latitude and longitude, kit.parallel and kit.meridian points projected with kit.geo(lon, lat, v), only the visible parts (z > 0), 2 / zoom px cream at 13% * grid opacity.
- "marker" (kind "prop", w 140, h 300, animated true): the place pin. Params kind ("pole" or "buoy") and drop (0 to 1, how far its foot ring has grown, default 1). Its FOOT (the exact place) is the bottom centre of its box, (0, 150): shots place it with h.asset('marker', px, py - 150 * m, m) where m is its scale (0.8 to 1). Pole: a 22 px wide rod from y -120 to y 140 with 6 red and cream bands, rounded top, a dark pointed tip at the foot, a light streak down its left third; an orange pennant flag at the top right (58 x 36), its free edge waving with p.t (sine, 2 Hz). Buoy: a short barrel (80 px wide, 70 tall, centred at y 90) in red and cream bands, a 6 px mast with a small glowing cream light on top. Both: at the foot an ellipse ring 110 x 26 px (cream 3 px line at 70%, scaled by drop) and a second fainter one 150 x 36. No text.
- Each other body (kind "prop", named for the body, animated false, its box sized as above): the same sphere of radius 500 drawn with h.planet and that body's look, params lon0, lat0 and light. A star: light: false always (it shines itself; it never takes p.light), its glow as atmo with halo 0.3 to 0.6, so its box is 1000 * (1 + halo) + 100 square.
- All assets: no backing plate or frame; the box corners stay empty.

## shots
- Draw order: kit.bg, the bodies (in the orbit view the farther one first), highlighted lines and rings (kit.geoLine), node dots, markers, labels, then the top text (title or counters). Never paint a solid fill over the background.
- The camera: A and B are cameras {lon0, lat0, s} from the idea's CAMERA line (a framing camera is B = kit.frame([[lon, lat], ...])); const c = kit.cam(lt, A, B, 0, 1.2); const v = kit.view(c); h.asset('globe', 540, 960, c.s, {lon0: c.lon0, lat0: c.lat0, zoom: c.s, grid: 1}). Use this same v for EVERY kit.geo, kit.geoLine and marker in the frame, so everything sits on its real place. Never guess a screen position for a place. In a spin, the turned body's lon0 goes into both the asset's params and the view (kit.view({lon0, lat0: c.lat0, s: c.s})), so markers ride with the surface.
- The orbit view: the star (or the planet a moon circles) with its asset at (540, 900), scaled to a radius of 70 to 110 px; the orbit a dashed WHITE ellipse around it (ctx.ellipse(540, 900, 420, 150, 0, 0, 2 * Math.PI), 3 px, dash [14, 12], 50% opacity); the body at angle a on it (x = 540 + 420 * Math.cos(a), y = 900 + 150 * Math.sin(a)) with its asset scaled to a radius of 30 to 50 px and light: [540 - x, 900 - y, 0], so its lit side faces the star; when Math.sin(a) < 0 it passes behind, so draw it before the star. A place LABEL under each body. The orbit view is not to scale; when the story compares sizes or distances, show them in the body view.
- Markers: const [px, py, z] = kit.geo(lon, lat, v); draw it (and its label) only when z > 0.1; h.asset('marker', px, py - 150 * m, m, {kind, drop}) with m 0.8 to 1.
- Lines: PARALLEL at N = kit.geoLine(ctx, kit.parallel(N), v, {color, k}); MERIDIAN at N = kit.geoLine(ctx, kit.meridian(N), v, {color, k}); LINE = kit.geoLine(ctx, kit.arc(...), v, {color, k}); RING = kit.geoLine(ctx, kit.ring(lon, lat, km, R), v, {color, k}) with R the radius on the idea's BODY line (6371 on the Earth).
