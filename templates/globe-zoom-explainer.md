# globe-zoom-explainer

## about
A dark-globe geography explainer driven by one continuous map camera. It opens on the whole Earth floating in dark space, zooms down onto one place, drops a striped marker on it and names it, then MEASURES it: a line or ring grows on the globe while a big number counter rolls up to the real value with its unit. Every new idea is another camera move over the same globe (glide along, zoom out, spin to the other side) and another measurement. The payoff puts the two key numbers side by side at the top in two colours (gold and ice blue) over their lines on the globe, and the reel closes pulled back on the whole Earth. For anything about places, positions, distances and sizes on Earth: coordinates, extremes (furthest, highest, deepest, remotest), routes, time zones, map distortion, where things are. Not for machines, history without a map, biology or character stories.

## look
- Clean above all: at any moment the frame holds the globe (whole, or zoomed so its surface fills the frame), at most 2 markers, at most 2 highlighted lines or rings, the counter row or the title at the top, at most 3 labels. Nothing else: no panels, cards, boxes, legends, compasses, icons, arrows floating free, insets or diagrams beside the globe.
- Everything on the Earth is placed by its real longitude and latitude through the kit's projection (kit.geo, below), so the camera can zoom and spin and every marker, line and label stays on its place. The real land, coastlines and country borders come from the engine's map data (h.landPath, h.coastPath, h.borderPath); never draw a coastline or a country's shape by hand.
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() or ctx.stroke() after them. h.asset('id', x, y, scale, params) takes NO ctx either (its first argument is the asset id string). Never pass a maxWidth to fillText (squeezed text is rejected): shrink the font until the text fits instead.
- After kit.bg, never paint a solid rectangle over the frame or a large part of it: kit.bg is the whole background and the globe sits on it.
- Colour is a set of relations (OKLCH); keep them, whatever the topic:
  - Space: near-black navy (lightness 0.11 to 0.14, chroma about 0.04, hue about 265), a soft glow behind the globe up to lightness 0.20.
  - Ocean: deep navy blue (lightness 0.22 to 0.26, chroma 0.07 to 0.08, hue about 273); on the whole globe a soft light pool toward the centre facing the viewer (up to lightness 0.34, chroma 0.06) darkening to lightness 0.12 at the rim.
  - Land: warm dark grey-taupe, nearly neutral: palette.land at lightness 0.34 (never above 0.36: it reads lighter than you expect next to the navy), chroma 0.012, hue about 60, with relief texture a little lighter (lightness + 0.06) and darker (lightness - 0.05); coasts a thin lighter edge (lightness 0.48); country borders thin cream lines at 25% opacity.
  - Atmosphere: a thin light blue-grey rim hugging the globe's edge (lightness 0.50 to 0.55, chroma 0.06, hue about 260) fading outward into a halo (lightness 0.18).
  - Graticule (latitude and longitude lines): cream at 10 to 16% opacity.
  - Measurement colours: GOLD (lightness 0.87, chroma 0.13, hue about 85) for the first key quantity, its line or ring and its counter; ICE BLUE (lightness 0.86, chroma 0.07, hue about 235) for the second; ORANGE (lightness 0.77, chroma 0.15, hue about 61) for the catch (a correcting or contrasting number); WHITE (lightness 0.98) for the familiar comparison and for labels and titles. Never more than these four on screen.
  - Marker: candy stripes of RED (lightness 0.60, chroma 0.20, hue about 29) and cream (lightness 0.95), a small ORANGE flag.
  - Ink (for text shadows and outlines): lightness 0.10, hue 265.
  - palette.accent is GOLD (the engine colours the caption bar with it); palette.accent2 ICE BLUE; palette.orange; palette.white; palette.bg the space navy; palette.ocean; palette.land; palette.ink; palette.red; palette.cream.
- Backgrounds (kit.bg), 2 of them:
  - "space": the space navy with a radial glow centred at (540, 960) of radius 900 (lightness 0.30 at the centre, 0.20 at radius 500, 0.10 at the corners: the engine rejects a background whose brightness varies too little), a vignette, and 80 to 110 star dots (2 to 4 px, cream at 25 to 60% opacity) at seeded positions that twinkle (opacity breathing with lt, each star its own phase) and drift 10 px per second; the glow breathes (radius +- 6% over 4 s).
  - "space_far": the same glow lightness (0.30 at the centre to 0.10 at the corners) with radius 700, and the same stars, used when the globe is small (pulled back).
- Type: kit.font(size, weight) returns `${weight} ${size}px "Segoe UI"`; counters use weight 900, labels and titles 800. Water and ocean names use ctx.font = `italic 600 ${size}px Georgia`.
- kit.title(ctx, text, x, y, o) draws white uppercase text (weight 800, o.size default 76) with an 8 px ink stroke under it (strokeText, lineJoin round) and a soft ink shadow; it shrinks the size until the measured text is at most 900 px wide; o.k pops it in (scale 0.85 to 1 with easeBack over 0.25 s, opacity 0 to 1 over 0.15 s).
- kit.panel(ctx, x, y, w, hgt, o) draws a small dark pill: ink at 60% opacity, corners hgt / 2, a 2 px ICE BLUE border at 50%. The shots never use it under labels, counters or titles (those carry their own ink stroke).
- The kit also returns these helpers (the shots and the globe asset call them; write them exactly):
  - kit.view(s, lon0, lat0) returns {x: 540, y: 960, r: 500 * s, lon0, lat0}: the view of the globe drawn with h.asset('globe', 540, 960, s, {lon0, lat0, zoom: s}). The place at (lon0, lat0) is always at the frame point (540, 960).
  - kit.geo = h.geo (the engine's orthographic projection, the same one its map data uses): kit.geo(lon, lat, v) returns [x, y, z]; z > 0 means the point faces the viewer, z <= 0 that it is behind the globe. Never write your own projection.
  - kit.dist = h.geoDist: kit.dist(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2) returns the great-circle distance in km.
  - kit.frame(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2) returns the camera [lon0, lat0, s] that shows two places together: their midpoint on the great circle (the middle point of kit.arc(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2)) and s = Math.min(8, Math.max(0.95, 57 / (kit.dist(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2) / 111))), so the line between them spans about half the frame width.
  - kit.cam(lt, A, B, t0 = 0, dur = 1.2) returns {lon0, lat0, s}: the camera moving from A = [lon0, lat0, s] to B = [lon0, lat0, s] between lt = t0 and t0 + dur, e = h.easeInOut of the progress (0 before, 1 after). lon0 and lat0 are interpolated with e (lon0 the short way round: if B[0] - A[0] > 180 subtract 360, if < -180 add 360). s = A[2] + (B[2] - A[2]) * e; when the two centres are more than 40 degrees apart, the camera pulls back through the whole globe: s = s + (Math.min(1, A[2], B[2]) - (A[2] + B[2]) / 2) * Math.sin(Math.PI * e). After the move (lt > t0 + dur) the camera keeps drifting gently: with a = lt - t0 - dur, lon0 = B[0] + 1.5 * a / B[2] (degrees per second, slower when zoomed in) and s = B[2] * (1 + 0.012 * a) (a slow push-in), so no frame is ever still but the motion stays calm.
  - kit.geoLine(ctx, pts, v, o): draws a line through pts (an array of [lon, lat]), each projected with kit.geo; a point with z <= 0 breaks the line (start a new sub-path at the next visible point). o = {color, width (default 6), k (0 to 1, default 1: how much of the line is drawn, from its first point; draw the first k share of the points), dash (optional [a, b]), glow (default true)}. With glow: stroke the same path 3 times, lineCap and lineJoin round: width * 4 at 10% opacity, width * 2 at 25%, then the core at full opacity; the glow opacity breathes +- 5% with a 1.5 s period.
  - kit.ring(lon, lat, km, n = 120) returns n + 1 [lon, lat] points: the circle of every point km kilometres from (lon, lat) on the Earth (radius 6371 km). For each bearing b = 2 * PI * i / n, with dd = km / 6371 and p = lat in radians: p2 = asin(sin(p) * cos(dd) + cos(p) * sin(dd) * cos(b)); lon2 = lon + atan2(sin(b) * sin(dd) * cos(p), cos(dd) - sin(p) * sin(p2)) in degrees; lat2 = p2 in degrees.
  - kit.arc(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2, n = 64) returns n + 1 [lon, lat] points along the great circle between the two places (convert both to 3D unit vectors, interpolate with slerp, convert back with lat = asin(z), lon = atan2(y, x)).
  - kit.parallel(lat) returns the points of the latitude line at lat (lon from -180 to 180 in 2-degree steps); kit.meridian(lon) the points of the longitude line at lon (lat from -90 to 90 in 2-degree steps).
  - kit.counter(ctx, value, unit, x, y, o): a big number. o = {color (default palette.accent), size (default 120), decimals (default 0), k, maxW (default 900)}. The value is written with thousands commas and o.decimals decimals (value.toLocaleString('en-US', {minimumFractionDigits: dec, maximumFractionDigits: dec})), in kit.font(size, 900); the unit after it in kit.font(size * 0.42, 800), uppercase, 14 px gap, both on one baseline. A unit that starts with "°" (°N, °S, °E, °W) is drawn differently: the number and the "°" are ONE text (value + "°") in kit.font(size, 900); measure that whole text, then draw the hemisphere letter in kit.font(size * 0.6, 900) starting 10 px after the measured end (the letter never overlaps the "°"). Measure both parts; if the total width is over o.maxW shrink size until it fits. The whole line is CENTRED on (x, y) (textBaseline 'middle' for the number; the unit's baseline matches the number's). Each part gets a 10 px ink stroke under the fill (strokeText, lineJoin round). o.k = seconds since the number landed on its final value: when k is between 0 and 0.25 the line pops (scale 1.15 to 1 with easeOut). The SHOT passes the rolling value.
  - kit.label(ctx, text, x, y, o): a place name, with NO panel or pill behind it. o = {kind: 'place' (white uppercase, kit.font(40, 800), ctx.letterSpacing = '3px', 6 px ink stroke under it) or 'water' (italic Georgia 46 px, ICE BLUE, mixed case, 6 px ink stroke), align ('left' or 'right' or 'center'), k (pop in: scale 0.85 to 1 with easeBack over 0.25 s)}. The whole text is ONE strokeText and ONE fillText call (never letter by letter); reset ctx.letterSpacing to '0px' after.

## motion
- One continuous map camera that never stops: every shot moves the camera with kit.cam from the previous shot's camera to its own over its first 0.8 to 1.6 s, then the globe keeps spinning slowly. A move between places more than 40 degrees apart pulls back through the whole globe, spins, and zooms back in (kit.cam does it).
- Things arrive on the word that names them, one at a time, 0.3 to 0.6 s apart: a marker drops (falls 80 px with easeBack over 0.35 s, its foot ring grows from 0 to full with easeOut), a line draws itself from its first point (k from 0 to 1 over 0.6 to 1.0 s, easeOut), a ring sweeps round (k from 0 to 1 over 0.8 s), a label pops (scale 0.85 to 1, easeBack, 0.25 s).
- Counters roll: the number counts up from 0 (or from the previous value of the same colour) to its real value over 1.0 to 1.4 s (easeOut) while its line or ring draws, lands on the word that says it, pops, and then holds still and readable to the end of the shot (at least 0.8 s).
- When a line or ring is drawn, two small node dots (12 px white discs with a 3 px ink ring) sit on its ends or where it touches the thing it measures to, each with a soft ring pulse (a 2 px circle growing from 12 to 40 px and fading over 1 s, repeating).
- The marker's flag always waves; glowing lines breathe; the stars twinkle.
- Easing: easeInOut for the camera, easeOut for things arriving, easeIn for leaving, easeBack for pops.

## plan
- First decide the PLACES and the NUMBERS, all real:
  - PLACES: 1 to 3 real places the reel visits, each with its name and real coordinates (longitude and latitude in degrees to 1 decimal, east and north positive, west and south negative).
  - NUMBERS: the GOLD number (the first key quantity), the ICE BLUE number (the second key quantity), the WHITE comparison number and the ORANGE catch number. Each is a real value with its unit (KM, °N, °S, °E, °W, H, M, KM²); if you are not certain of a value, choose another quantity you are certain of.
  - GOLD and ICE BLUE must belong together, because the payoff shows them side by side as one answer: EITHER the two measures of the SAME place (its two coordinates, its two distances, its size and its height) OR the SAME quantity for two places (both distances in km, both latitudes). Never one place's first measure beside another place's second measure.
  - WHITE gives the viewer a feel for the scale: what one unit of the quantity means in everyday terms, or a well-known distance or size between famous places, as a real number with its unit.
  - ORANGE is the catch: a real number that shows where the simple picture breaks (a place or line where the same measure gives a surprising different value).
  - Every counter shows the number its shot is about, and it is never 0 or a trivial value (a starting line that is zero by definition is named in the line, not counted). The payoff's two counters repeat the GOLD and ICE BLUE values exactly as the measure and second shots showed them; the close shows the same two counters again.
- Every shot idea starts with these lines, then says what moves on which line:
  - "CAMERA: from [lon0, lat0, scale] to [lon0, lat0, scale] at 0 s over 1.2 s" (the first shot starts 30 degrees east of its target; every other shot starts where the previous one ended). The "to" camera centres on what the shot is about: for one place its own coordinates; for a shot about two places write "to FRAME Name [lon, lat] and Name [lon, lat]" and the shot uses kit.frame (their midpoint, zoomed so both show). Scale guide: the whole globe 0.9 to 1.0; a continent 1.6 to 2.2; a country 3 to 5; a city's surroundings 6 to 8 (never above 8).
  - "OVERLAYS:" each thing on the globe with its exact numbers, every place written as its name followed by its real coordinates: MARKER at Name [lon, lat] (pole on land, buoy at sea); LINE from Name [lon, lat] to Name [lon, lat] in [colour]; RING of [N] km around Name [lon, lat] in [colour]; PARALLEL at latitude [N] in [colour]; MERIDIAN at longitude [N] in [colour]; LABEL "[TEXT]" at Name [lon, lat] (place or water). The engine checks every named place's coordinates and every LINE's km counter against the real ones and sends the plan back when they are wrong.
  - "TOP:" either TITLE "[NAME]" / "[SUBLINE]", or COUNTER [colour] [value] [unit] (and for the payoff and close, COUNTERS gold [value] [unit] and ice blue [value] [unit]).
- Structure: exactly 8 shots in this order, one beat each (this template's count overrides any other shot count in the request):
  1. hook: the whole globe (scale 0.95) slowly spinning to the subject; ONE thing on it glows (the line, ring or place the reel is about) in GOLD. Line: a surprising fact with a real number in it, or a question that ends with "?", naming the brief's subject; never a definition, a summary or a claim about what the subject does in general.
  2. locate: the camera zooms down onto the place (scale 3 to 7); the marker drops on it; its name as the TITLE at the top (a short subline under it: the country or what kind of place it is); one LABEL nearby for context. Line: names the place and why it matters.
  3. measure: the GOLD measurement grows on the globe (a line, a ring or a highlighted parallel or meridian) while the GOLD counter rolls up to the real value with its unit. Line: says that number.
  4. compare: the WHITE comparison drawn on the same globe (a line between two well-known places, or one unit of the measure marked as a short line) with its own WHITE counter, so the viewer feels the scale. Line: the comparison.
  5. second: the camera glides or spins to the second measure (the same place's other line, or the second place); its line or ring and its counter in ICE BLUE. Line: the second number.
  6. twist: a catch that changes how the numbers read: the camera moves to where the catch lives; its line or ring and its counter in ORANGE (a contrasting or correcting real number). A catch is one of: the same measure gives a very different value somewhere else; a number that disproves what most people believe; where the measure stops working or hits its limit. Line shape: "But [the same measure] at [another place or line] is [the surprising real number]". A fact that only adds a name (another line, another label, another place where two lines meet) is not a catch.
  7. payoff: the two key numbers side by side at the top (GOLD left, ICE BLUE right) with both of their lines or rings visible on the globe; the camera pulled back so both fit. Line: what the two numbers mean together (the answer). This is the music's payoff_shot.
  8. close: the camera pulls back to the hook's exact composition (the same CAMERA as the hook's end), both counters still at the top, fully shown from the first frame (they do not roll again); the line lands the takeaway, ideally echoing the hook's claim.
- Text: titles at most 16 characters, sublines at most 24, labels at most 14, all uppercase except water names; labels name places only (numbers live in the counters); counters are digits with their unit, the way people read them: thousands with commas, degrees with one decimal and the hemisphere letter as the unit.
- Every number on screen and in the lines must be real and agree with the counters; the coordinates in the OVERLAYS must be the places' real coordinates. Read every line as a geography teacher would: when a line says how something is measured or drawn, it must be the real way (which line, from where, in which direction), and a range must be its real range (where it starts, where it ends, in which units).
- A LINE joins only places whose distance or relation is the point of that shot; never a decorative line between unrelated places.
- Lines: one narrator, chris (a casual American storyteller, male, fast and lively like the reference). One line per shot; the request's word budget decides the length: split it evenly over the 8 shots and count every line before returning (too short is as wrong as too long). Conversational, concrete, one new fact per line, numbers written as words; never repeat a fact.
- Assets, exactly 2: "globe" (hero) and "marker" (described under assets). Everything else (lines, rings, node dots, labels, counters, titles) is drawn by the shots with the kit's helpers.
- Music: Bright, Driving (a light curious pulse under a fast voice), 95 to 110 bpm; payoff_shot = the payoff shot (the two numbers side by side). Sound effects: a pop on each counter landing and each marker drop (about 2 per shot, each exactly on its visual hit); a whoosh only on a pull-back through the whole globe.

## assets
- "globe" (kind "scenery", hero, w 1100, h 1100, animated false): the Earth as an orthographic globe of radius 500 centred on (0, 0) of its box, params lon0 and lat0 (the place facing the viewer, degrees), zoom (the scale the shot draws it at; keep every stroke width divided by zoom so lines stay the same on screen) and grid (0 to 1, graticule opacity factor, default 1). It projects every point with kit.geo(lon, lat, {x: 0, y: 0, r: 500, lon0: p.lon0, lat0: p.lat0}). Draw in this order:
  1. Halo: a radial gradient ring from r 500 to r 545: light blue-grey (palette.accent2 mixed with the ocean) at 45% at the edge, fading to transparent outward.
  2. Ocean: a disc of r 500 filled with a radial gradient centred at (-60, -80): lightness 0.34 at the centre, the ocean colour at 60% of the radius, lightness 0.12 at the edge.
  3. Land: ctx.save(), clip to the disc (ctx.beginPath(); ctx.arc(0, 0, 500, 0, Math.PI * 2); ctx.clip()); const v = {x: 0, y: 0, r: 500, lon0: p.lon0, lat0: p.lat0}; const land = h.landPath(v, zoom) (the engine's real Natural Earth land as one Path2D, already projected, culled and closed along the rim); ctx.fillStyle = palette.land; ctx.fill(land, 'evenodd') (lakes such as the Caspian are holes).
  4. Relief: still clipped, ctx.clip(land, 'evenodd') too, then build two paths of short strokes: for every cell of a 1-degree grid within 70 / zoom degrees of (lon0, lat0) (and a 0.25-degree grid within 18 / zoom degrees when zoom is over 2.5), at a seeded jitter inside the cell (h.rng seeded with the cell's integer indices, so the strokes stay fixed to the land), project the point with kit.geo and skip it when z <= 0; a stroke 0.6 cells long at a seeded angle: one path collects the light strokes (lightness + 0.06, 18% opacity), the other the dark strokes offset 1.5 / zoom px down-right (lightness - 0.05, 22% opacity). Stroke each path once (never one stroke() call per mark).
  5. Coasts and borders (still clipped to the disc): ctx.stroke(h.coastPath(v, zoom)) with a 2 / zoom px line of the coast colour (lightness 0.48) at 70%; ctx.stroke(h.borderPath(v, zoom)) with a 1.4 / zoom px cream line at 25%. Restore.
  6. Graticule: every 15 degrees of latitude and longitude, kit.parallel and kit.meridian points projected, only visible parts (z > 0), 2 / zoom px cream at 13% * grid opacity.
  7. Rim: inside the disc, a radial darkening from r 420 (transparent) to r 500 (ink at 55%); then a 6 / zoom px ring at r 500 of the atmosphere colour at 80%.
  Never write coastline coordinates yourself: h.landPath, h.coastPath and h.borderPath hold the real map.
- "marker" (kind "prop", w 140, h 300, animated true): the place pin. Params kind ("pole" or "buoy") and drop (0 to 1, how far its foot ring has grown, default 1). Its FOOT (the exact place) is the bottom centre of its box, (0, 150): shots place it with h.asset('marker', px, py - 150 * m, m) where m is its scale (0.8 to 1). Pole: a 22 px wide rod from y -120 to y 140 with 6 red and cream bands, rounded top, a dark pointed tip at the foot, a light streak down its left third; an orange pennant flag at the top right (58 x 36), its free edge waving with p.t (sine, 2 Hz). Buoy: a short barrel (80 px wide, 70 tall, centred at y 90) in red and cream bands, a 6 px mast with a small glowing cream light on top. Both: at the foot an ellipse ring 110 x 26 px (cream 3 px line at 70%, scaled by drop) and a second fainter one 150 x 36. No text.
- Both assets: no backing plate or frame; the box corners stay empty.

## shots
- Draw order: kit.bg, the globe, highlighted lines and rings (kit.geoLine), node dots, markers, labels, then the top text (title or counters). Never paint a solid fill over the background.
- The camera: const c = kit.cam(lt, A, B, 0, 1.2) with A and B from the idea's CAMERA line (a "to FRAME Name [lon, lat] and Name [lon, lat]" camera is B = kit.frame(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2)); const v = kit.view(c.s, c.lon0, c.lat0); h.asset('globe', 540, 960, c.s, {lon0: c.lon0, lat0: c.lat0, zoom: c.s, grid: 1}). Use this same v for EVERY kit.geo, kit.geoLine and marker in the frame, so everything sits on its real place on the globe. Never guess a screen position for a place.
- Markers: const [px, py, z] = kit.geo(lon, lat, v); draw it (and its label) only when z > 0.1; h.asset('marker', px, py - 150 * m, m, {kind, drop}) with m 0.8 to 1.
- Lines: PARALLEL at N = kit.geoLine(ctx, kit.parallel(N), v, {color, k}); MERIDIAN at N = kit.geoLine(ctx, kit.meridian(N), v, {color, k}); LINE = kit.geoLine(ctx, kit.arc(...), v, {color, k}); RING = kit.geoLine(ctx, kit.ring(lon, lat, km), v, {color, k}). Width 6; a comparison line 5 with dash [18, 12]. A LINE's km counter ends on Math.round(kit.dist(lon1, lat1, lon2, lat2)), and both ends of a LINE get a marker or a node dot and their LABEL.
- Fixed layout:
  - Top (y 260 to 460): EITHER the title (kit.title at (540, 300), 76 px, and its subline at (540, 372), 40 px) OR the counters: one counter centred at (540, 380), size 120; two counters side by side centred at (290, 380) and (790, 380), size 100, maxW 430 each, GOLD left and ICE BLUE right. Never the title and a counter at the same time (the title leaves 0.3 s before a counter arrives).
  - The place of interest sits at the frame point (540, 960) (the camera centres on it); its marker 210 to 280 px tall.
  - Labels: inside x 80 to 1000 and y 520 to 1400, beside their place (offset 40 px to the side with more room), never over a marker or another label.
  - Nothing important below y 1440 (the caption lives there).
- Every frame moves: the camera glides or the globe spins, lines breathe, flags wave, node dots pulse, stars twinkle.
- The close shot's camera B is the hook's camera B, so the reel ends on the opening composition.

## director
- Flag: a globe with no land on it (the ocean and grid alone), continents that do not look like the real ones, or land drawn outside the globe or over the space behind it; a globe that is a flat disc without its ocean light pool, rim and halo; markers, lines or labels that do not sit on their real place (check every coordinate against standard knowledge) or that float off the globe; a frame that does not move; more than 2 markers, 2 highlighted lines or 3 labels at once, or any panel, card, legend, icon or arrow; a counter whose value or unit differs from the line that says it, or that jumps instead of rolling; the title and a counter at the top at the same time; counters in colours other than gold, ice blue, orange and white, or the same colour used for two different quantities; the payoff without both numbers side by side; text cut off or squeezed; a close that does not return to the hook's view; anything important below y 1440.
- Check every place, coordinate and number against standard knowledge; remove a number rather than keep a doubtful one.
