# low-poly

## about
A PS1-era low-poly story reel: a true, emotional or strange story told by a quiet narrator in short, plain sentences, in five moves: a place, a character, what happened, the turn, the ending. Every shot is a small low-poly diorama seen by a slow game camera, in the look of a late-90s console game: chunky 3x pixels, dithered warm colour, flat-shaded blocky models with tiny pixel textures, a lone small character in a big, cosy, lamp-lit place. Time jumps by a big foreground object wiping across the lens; when it clears, the world has changed around the character. Warm, melancholic, never sentimental. For true stories of animals, people, places and odd events (friendships, disappearances, last survivors, lost places, strange coincidences, small heroes). Not for explainers of mechanisms, data comparisons, lists or how-tos.

## look
- PS1 pixels (check "pixel grid: 360"): the engine shrinks every shot's artwork to 360 x 640 art pixels with nearest-neighbour and blows it back up 3x, then draws all text crisp on top. Draw for that grid: every line, seam, gap and detail at least 6 px (2 art pixels), texture cells 9 px, nothing thinner than 3 px (it flickers or vanishes). No ctx.shadowBlur, no ctx.filter, no blur, no soft glows, no smooth gradients: a sky, a light pool or a fade is 4 to 8 flat bands. Edges will step and crawl a little as the camera moves; that is the PS1 wobble, wanted.
- Low-poly 3D faked in 2D: every model, prop and piece of architecture is built from blocks (boxes, tapered boxes, wedges, pyramids and cones; round things as 6 or 8-sided polygons, never circles or curves), flat-shaded with 2 or 3 tones per block: the top face lightest, the front face the base tone, the side face darkest. No outlines anywhere: blocks are told apart by their tones and by 6 px dark seams painted where they meet (plank gaps, tile joints, door edges, frames).
- Pixel textures on every surface: seeded speckles, 9 x 9 px squares snapped to a 9 px grid (Math.floor(x / 9) * 9), each 6 to 9% lighter or darker than its face (mix the face tone toward the ink or the cream), about one per 900 px2 of face, clipped to the face; wood as 2 or 3 tone bands with 6 px dark gaps, tiles and bricks with 6 px seams, fur and cloth as speckles only. Compute each face's 2 speckle shades once with h.mix and reuse them.
- Colour is a set of OKLCH relations; keep them, whatever the story:
  - Base hue: the colour of the place's light; warm amber (hue about 65 to 75) by default, for interiors, evenings and cosy places. In a cold or night story walls and ground may turn cool (blue-grey, hue about 250, chroma at most 0.04), but every light source stays warm amber.
  - Surfaces: one 7-step ramp in the base hue family from lightness 0.30 to 0.89, chroma highest (about 0.10) at lightness 0.70 to 0.76 and low (0.03 to 0.05) at both ends; the hue turns about 35 to 40 degrees from the darkest step (redder) to the lightest (yellower). Each block uses neighbouring steps: top +1 or +2, front the base step, side -1 or -2.
  - Ground and darks (palette.bg): lightness 0.22 to 0.30, chroma about 0.05, hue about 20 degrees redder than the base (dark wood, shadowed floor), about a quarter of the frame. Ink (palette.ink: eyes, seams, gaps, contact shadows): lightness 0.13 to 0.18, chroma about 0.03, the same reddish hue. Never pure black.
  - Second family, about 55 degrees round from the base (olive green on an amber base): lightness 0.35 to 0.55, chroma 0.07 to 0.10, on 4 to 10% of the frame: lamp shades, painted metal, plants, a coat, a vehicle's paint.
  - Lights: cream (lightness 0.95 to 0.97, chroma 0.06 to 0.08, hue about 40 degrees yellower than the base) for bulbs, lit windows and blank boards.
  - Skies (windows and outdoors): 6 to 8 flat horizontal bands from warm cream at the horizon through orange and rose to dusky violet at the top (lightness 0.88 down to 0.40, the hue turning about 100 to 120 degrees from bottom to top), each seam between two bands a 6 px tall checker of 3 x 3 px squares of both colours; a dark violet-brown silhouette (lightness 0.30) of whatever stands on the horizon.
  - palette.accent is the caption yellow: lightness about 0.94, chroma about 0.20, hue about 40 degrees yellower than the base (a pure, bright yellow); the engine draws the narrator's caption in it (lowercase, bold, dark outline, no box, centred at y 1500) and the stamps use it.
  - Contrast: the lightest light about 10:1 against the ground.
  - Palette names (in the plan, by role, so the kit and every shot find them): bg, ink, cream, accent, accent2, base (the ramp's middle step), lit (two steps lighter), shade (two steps darker), sky_high, sky_low; the kit derives the other ramp steps with h.mix.
- Backgrounds (kit.bg), one per place; each is a one-point-perspective diorama that fills the whole frame, seen from a low game camera a little above the character's head:
  - The vanishing point at x 540, y 650 to 820. The floor or ground runs from there to the bottom edge with perspective seams: 12 to 16 lines converging to the vanishing point and cross seams at y = vy + 30 * Math.pow(k + 1, 1.5) for k = 0, 1, 2 ... while y < 1920; seams 6 px, about 30% darker than the floor.
  - Interiors: a back wall from about y 250 to the floor line with a lower band of darker wood planks (y vy - 90 to vy), 2 or 3 windows showing the banded sky with block frames, 2 posters or boards as colour blocks with stripes, a door, a bench or shelf; posts or pillars in front of the wall; a ceiling of tiles or beams converging to the vanishing point at the top, darkest at the frame's top edge; at least one hanging lamp with a cone-shaped shade.
  - Exteriors: the banded sky above a horizon at y 650 to 820; hills as triangles with a light left face and a darker right face; trees as 2 or 3 stacked cones or a 6-sided canopy on a box trunk; houses as blocks with pyramid roofs; water as flat bands with 6 px pale dashes sliding sideways.
  - Light: around each light source a pool of 3 to 5 concentric flat ellipses of the light colour at 6 to 10% each (banded, never a radial gradient), breathing plus or minus 5% with lt; a hanging lamp swings 1 to 2 degrees.
  - Fog: things near the vanishing point mixed 25 to 50% toward the fog colour (palette.bg lightened to about 0.40), so the place has depth.
  - The frame's edges are darker because of the place itself (the dark ceiling at the top, dark posts at the sides, a dark foreground edge or shadowed floor at the bottom), never a rectangle or frame stroked around the picture.
  - Always moving: square particles drift down (6, 9 or 15 px squares, 30 to 80 of them, 30 to 80 px per second, swaying plus or minus 20 px): dust motes in the light, or the real petals, leaves, flakes, rain (3 x 15 px streaks) or embers of the story's season and weather.
  - The same place at two times is two backgrounds with exactly the same layout ("[place]" and "[place]_later"); only the light, the particles and the wear change (another time of day in the windows, another season's particles, a thin layer of what fell lying on the tops of things, overgrowth as small green blocks at the foot of walls, cracks as 6 px dark lines).
  - No readable words anywhere in the world (signs, posters, screens, books and labels are colour blocks and stripes): in pixel mode the engine draws every fillText crisp on top of the finished picture, so a word inside the world floats in front of everything.
- kit.post(ctx, lt) draws only the PS1 colour dither, on the 3 px art-pixel grid, in screen space (the host calls it after the pixelation): the 2 x 2 core of the 4 x 4 Bayer matrix, one dashed stroke per cell class (a dash pattern restarts at every moveTo, so each row starts at its own column):
  ctx.save(); ctx.setTransform(1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0); ctx.lineWidth = 3; ctx.lineCap = 'butt'; ctx.setLineDash([3, 3]);
  for (const [rp, cp, col, a] of [[0, 0, palette.ink, 0.07], [1, 1, palette.ink, 0.03], [0, 1, palette.cream, 0.03], [1, 0, palette.cream, 0.07]]) { ctx.beginPath(); for (let j = rp; j < 640; j += 2) { ctx.moveTo(3 * cp, 3 * j + 1.5); ctx.lineTo(1080, 3 * j + 1.5); } ctx.strokeStyle = h.rgba(col, a); ctx.stroke(); }
  ctx.restore();
  Nothing else in kit.post: no vignette, grain, wash or pixelation of its own.
- Type (kit.font): "Segoe UI", bold. kit.title draws text only, in the reference's caption look: lowercase as given, palette.accent fill, an 8 px palette.ink outline (lineJoin round) and a second ink copy 4 px lower behind it as a drop; no box. kit.panel is a plain dark plate (palette.ink at 80%, square corners, no border); the style shows no panels, so shots never draw one.
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() after them. h.asset(id, x, y, scale, params) takes no ctx either. h.mix and h.rgba take hex colour strings.

## motion
- Calm and slow everywhere (this template asks for calm motion: no anticipation, overshoot, easeBack, squash and stretch, speed lines, bursts or shakes). Every move eases in and out (easeInOut) over 0.6 to 1.2 s.
- The slow game camera: each shot is one continuous camera move over its whole length, easeInOut: a push-in from scale 1.00 to 1.06 to 1.12 centred on the character, or a sideways truck of 30 to 60 px, or a slow rise. Never a fast move, a whip or a shake inside a shot. A near layer (a post, a railing, an edge, near-lens particles) moves 1.5 times as far as the place behind it.
- The character is never frozen: breathing (body scale 1 to 1.5%, period about 3 s), a blink every 3 to 4 s (eyes 1 art pixel tall for 0.12 s), a small sway of ears, hair, cloth or tail (2 to 4 degrees, period 1.5 to 2.5 s).
- Acting is small and slow, one beat about every 5 s, each landing on the words it belongs to: the head turns to look somewhere (left, right, up, down) over 0.5 s, the pose changes (sits down, stands, lies down, steps forward), the expression changes (eyebrows and eye height only).
- The world drifts: particles fall and sway, the lamp swings, its light pool breathes, the sky bands slide 2 to 4 px per second, a far figure may walk slowly past.
- The wipe (the time shot only): see shots.
- The ending's last second is nearly still: only particles, breathing and the camera's last ease.

## plan
- Structure: 6 to 8 shots, one beat each, in this order (place and bond may be left out when the budget is tight):
  1. hook: the [character] alone in [the place], medium shot, the camera pushing in slowly; stamp "[place], [year]". Line: the strangest or saddest true fact of the story with a real number in it, or a question that ends with "?".
  2. place: a wide view of the place as it was, alive (small block figures passing far behind, lights on); the character still at least a quarter of the frame tall. Line: where and when, and what the place was.
  3. bond: who or what the character was tied to (the second asset) and their daily routine, both in the frame at the same scale, one small warm action (walking side by side, sharing food, a hand on a head). With no second character, the routine alone.
  4. event: what happened, shown by a change in the same place (a door closes, a light goes out, a thing breaks or falls, the crowd is gone, the water rises); the character turns to look. Line: the event as a concrete moment the hook did not tell (who last saw them, the last thing done, the last entry, the sound, the weather that night), with its date if the reference has one; never the hook's fact again in other words.
  5. time: opens with the wipe; when it clears, the same place later (the "[place]_later" background), with the character where the story puts them by then; stamp "[number] years later" (or the real span). Line: how much time passed, and what changed or stayed the same.
  6. turn: the reveal: what people found out, why it happened, or what it meant; a closer view (the character 45 to 65% of the frame height) and one new thing in the frame (others watching from behind, a found object, a light coming on). Line: the reveal in one short sentence; the shortest line of the reel.
  7. ending: what remains: the place today, the empty spot, what the story left behind; the slowest push-in, held; stamp "[place], [year or today]". Line: a quiet last fact that lingers (what was never found, the question still open, the one object that is still there), never a moral, a lesson, a call to follow or an unrelated present-day fact.
- Facts: a TRUE story from the REFERENCE: every name, place, date, number and event comes from it; no invented dialogue, no thoughts or feelings stated as facts ("[character] must have felt ..."). When the reference does not say, leave it out.
- Lines: one narrator, george (a warm British male storyteller), quiet and unhurried. Plain past-tense sentences, like someone telling it by a fire: one concrete true detail per line (a date, a count, a distance, a name); a shot's line may be two short sentences. Numbers in lines are written as words. Say the brief's subject by its name in line 1 or 2. Tones: quietly or mysterious for the hook, warm for place and bond, grave or slow for the event, quietly for time, sad or grave for the turn, gentle or hopeful for the ending; never excited, urgent or dramatic.
- Stamps (the only on-screen words): at most one per shot, only in the hook, time and ending shots, 2 to 5 words, lowercase: a place, a year, a date or a span of time from the reference. Write each stamp in its shot's idea. Never a stamp that repeats the line.
- Assets, 2 or 3, all built in the block style: FIRST the main character (kind "character": a person or animal, as the reference describes them: species or build, colouring, one or two features that make them recognisable), with params "look" (front, left, right, up, down: where the head turns), "expression" (calm, alert, sad, happy) and "eyes" (0 to 1, for blinks). SECOND the one other thing the story turns on: the second character (kind "character", same params) or the key object (a vehicle, a boat, a house, a lamp, a tool) seen in at least two shots. A THIRD only for a set piece the character sits or lies in or on in several shots. Places are backgrounds; small props, extra figures, particles and the wipe are drawn by the shots.
- Asset descriptions: the silhouette as blocks with proportions (head about a third of the height for animals and children, a quarter for adults; a box torso, box or tapered-box legs and arms, wedge or cone ears, a box snout or nose), which tones go on which faces, the real colouring, and the face: two dark square eyes, short eyebrow bars, a short dark mouth bar.
- Backgrounds: 3 or 4: "[place]" (as it was), "[place]_later" (exactly the same layout at the later time), and 1 or 2 other real places of the story. Describe each as its diorama: interior or exterior, what stands on the back wall or horizon, the floor or ground, the light source and its colour, the time of day in the sky, the particles.
- Each shot idea gives: the background, the camera move (push-in toward what, or truck which way), the character's pose, look and expression on each line, the other asset or the props and figures the shot draws, the particles, and the stamp text if any. The time shot's idea also names its wipe object.
- Music: feel Somber Calming (Mysterious instead of Calming for a strange story), 70-90 bpm (the reference is silent, so the band comes from the kind: a slow, warm, nostalgic bed); payoff_shot is the turn shot. Sound effects: one soft whoosh as the wipe passes; nothing else.

## assets
- Build everything from one block helper written once inside drawAsset: block(x, y, w, hgt, d, tones) draws a box seen from slightly above and to the left: the front face (a rectangle, or a trapezoid for a tapered box) in the base tone, the top face (a parallelogram from the front's top edge to the same edge shifted by (0.5 * d, -0.4 * d)) in the light tone, the right side face (front's right edge to the same edge shifted the same way) in the dark tone; then the pixel speckles clipped to each face. Cones and wedges (ears, noses, roofs) as 2 triangles, light left and dark right.
- No ink outlines (the sprite-sheet reviewer must not ask for them): parts read by their tones and by 6 px ink seams where they meet. No gradients, no circles, no soft shadows inside the asset.
- The face: two solid ink squares for eyes (about one eighth of the head's width each), short eyebrow bars in a darker tone of the head above them, a short ink mouth bar, a box snout or nose where the real one is; p.look shifts the eyes, snout and nose toward that side by up to a quarter of the head's width (and up or down), so the head reads as turned; p.expression tilts the eyebrow bars (sad: outer ends down; alert: raised; happy: eyes as short flat bars) and nothing else; p.eyes squashes the eye squares to 1 art pixel when closed. The face stays visible in every pose.
- Built-in life from p.t: breathing (torso scale 1 to 1.5%), a blink every 3.5 s, a 2 to 4 degree sway of ears, hair, cloth or tail.
- Chunky proportions, like a 90s game model: thick box limbs, big head, small feet or paws as flat boxes, hands as small boxes; hair as 1 to 3 blocks; clothes as coloured blocks with a 6 px ink seam at the edges.
- Objects use the same construction: windows as darker inset rectangles with 2 pale diagonal reflection bars, wheels as 8-sided polygons with a darker hub, doors as panels with 6 px seams.
- A flat 8-sided ink polygon at 30 to 35% under every object that stands on the ground (not a soft ellipse); characters and objects stand on the bottom edge of the box.
- Declare the box at the size the shots mostly show it (a character about 400 to 600 wide and 600 to 900 tall), so its 9 px speckles stay 3 art pixels on screen. Fill the box; never draw outside it. No text.

## shots
- Composition: the place fills the frame (kit.bg). The main character stands, sits or lies on the floor with its base at y 1080 to 1200, centred at x 540 or on a third (x 380 or 700), 480 to 860 px tall in a medium shot (a quarter to 45% of the frame height), up to 1250 px in the turn and ending close shots; never cropped by the frame edges except in a close shot. In every shot at least one asset is drawn 400 px or more on its longer side.
- The camera: wrap everything in the world in one transform: ctx.save(); ctx.translate(cx, cy); ctx.scale(z, z); ctx.translate(-cx, -cy); with z going from 1 to 1.06 to 1.12 by h.easeInOut(lt / sh.dur) and (cx, cy) the character's centre (or add a slow sideways shift instead); draw kit.bg, the far props and figures, the character's contact shadow, the character, the near layer and the near-lens particles; ctx.restore(); then the stamp, outside the camera.
- Props and extra figures the shot draws use the asset craft: blocks with 3 tones, speckles, 6 px seams, an 8-sided contact shadow; never flat single-colour shapes, never outlines. Extra people or animals are simple 2-tone block figures without face details, 150 to 350 px tall, behind the character, mixed 30 to 50% toward the fog colour.
- Near-lens particles: 3 to 8 big squares (24 to 45 px) of the place's particle kind drifting slowly across the front of the frame, for depth.
- The stamp: kit.title(ctx, '[stamp text]', 540, 1310, { size: 52 }), lowercase, shown for the whole shot (or from its line's start); nothing else is written on screen: no labels, panels, titles, numbers or name tags.
- The wipe (first 1.5 s of the time shot): one long object from the story's own world passes right in front of the lens from the right edge to the left, filling the whole frame height (y 0 to 1920): a vehicle's side seen very close, a ship's hull, a cart or wagon's wall, a drifting wall of fog or a curtain of rain. Draw it 1.4 to 2.5 times the frame width long, as polygons (h.poly, never one big fillRect) in the block style with its own real details (a vehicle, hull or wall: panels, 6 px seams, a paint band, windows with pale diagonal reflection bars; fog or rain: 3 or 4 flat bands with checker seams and 3 x 15 px streaks). Its front edge enters at lt 0, it covers the whole frame from lt 0.25 to 1.0 (slowing almost to a stop at about 0.6), then speeds out to the left by lt 1.5. Behind it is already the "[place]_later" background, with the character where the shot's idea puts them.
- Never the same view twice: each shot is a different diorama or the same place from a new camera (wide, medium, close, from the side, from above, inside); when the hero is an object (a ship, a house, a machine), each shot shows it from another side or distance, or shows its people or a detail of it instead.
- Keep the bottom 450 px for the floor, ground or foreground edge only: nothing important below y 1440 (the engine's yellow caption line sits at y 1470 to 1530).
- Never paint a solid fill over the background; the wipe is the one object that may cover the frame, and only for that second.
- No graphs, maps, routes, charts or icons: the story is told in pictures of the place; a journey is the character walking through the place.
- Every frame moves: the camera's slow ease, particles, breathing, the lamp; calm, never busy.

## director
- Flag: dark outlines on anything; smooth gradients, soft glows, blur or lines thinner than 6 px (it must read as chunky 3x pixels); flat single-colour shapes without the 3 block tones and pixel speckles; circles or curves where blocks belong; readable words inside the world (signs, posters, screens); any on-screen text other than a stamp (labels, panels, titles, numbers); a stamp over 5 words, not lowercase, or below y 1440; the character looking different from shot to shot; a place whose layout changes between its two times; a time shot without the wipe or without a visible change of time after it; the character smaller than a quarter of the frame height or cropped outside a close shot; fast, bouncy or shaky motion; a neon, cold or garish palette where the warm relations are asked; a frame or rectangle stroked around the picture; an ending that preaches, gives a lesson or asks to follow.
- Check every name, place, date, number and event against the reference; remove anything it does not support, and any invented dialogue or stated feeling.
