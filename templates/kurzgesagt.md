# kurzgesagt

## about
A flat-vector science and scale explainer in a Kurzgesagt-like look: every object glows on a deep violet star field, lit from one side so it shows a lit crescent and a dark night side, and each object gets its own bright rounded label tag. It opens on a question about the subject, starts from something the viewer already knows, then makes a size, a distance or a count felt by drawing real things at their true ratio (or a process step by step), each with its real number on a dotted measuring line under it. The story picks the view: things side by side, a far bigger thing as a curved horizon, a row of copies for "N fit across", a dive by powers of ten for a far smaller thing, a path with a traveller and a counter for a journey, a column for one quantity across several things. A pink check tag stamps the answer, and the reel ends on a familiar analogy drawn at the true ratio. For sizes, distances, numbers and life stories of things in space, nature, the body, physics and the built world, and the time it takes to cross a distance (astronomical, microscopic, biological, architectural and everyday comparisons): how big, how small, how far, how long to get there, how many, what happens to [named thing]. Not for history, people, machines with moving parts, procedures, data over time or character stories.

## look
- Clean and calm: at any moment the frame holds the star field, the things of one comparison (usually 1 or 2; a row of copies, a dive's dots or a column count as one group), their name tags, their measuring lines and number tags, and in the answer and close shots the answer tag (a ring callout around a dot and a journey's traveller belong to their views). Nothing else: no panels, cards, boxes, icons, arrows, grids, insets, diagrams, titles or paragraphs. The engine draws the spoken line as the caption at the bottom: never draw the line yourself.
- Flat vector art with NO outlines: perfect circles, ellipses and rounded pills separated only by colour; soft radial gradients only for halos, haze and highlights; no texture, noise, grain, ctx.filter or blur.
- One light for everything, from the RIGHT (an unseen star past the right edge). This replaces the general "light from the top-left" rule: every thing's night side is on its LEFT, its lit crescent on its RIGHT, its soft highlight top-right. Nothing sits on a surface in space: no contact shadows, no floor.
- Things that shine are the one exception: a star (the Sun included), a fire, a lamp, lava, anything that gives off light is lit ALL OVER: kit.body with o.star = true, a full bright disc with a hotter core and a wider halo, never a night side or a crescent (a star drawn as a crescent reads as a moon).
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx and only build a path: call ctx.fill() after them. h.asset('id', x, y, scale, params) takes NO ctx (its first argument is the asset id). h.fmt(value, {suffix: ' ' + unit}) writes a number with thousands commas. Never pass a maxWidth to fillText (squeezed text is rejected): shrink the font until it fits. After kit.bg, never paint a solid rectangle over the frame.
- Overshoot with a set strength (the reference's springs): const back = (t, s) => { t = h.clamp(t, 0, 1); return 1 + (s + 1) * Math.pow(t - 1, 3) + s * Math.pow(t - 1, 2); }; s = 1.25 for things sliding in, 2.2 for tags popping, 2.6 for the answer tag.
- Colour is a set of relations (OKLCH). The ground and the tag colours are the style; only the things take the real colours of the topic. Call the ground hue H (about 280, a deep violet-navy); keep it for every topic:
  - palette.bg: the ground, lightness 0.17, chroma 0.075, hue H. The kit derives: the top of the frame lightness 0.21, chroma 0.09, hue H; the bottom lightness 0.24, chroma 0.085, hue H + 20 (a plum tint); the haze lightness 0.35, chroma 0.16, hue H + 5; violet star dots lightness 0.56, chroma 0.18, hue H + 5; pale star dots lightness 0.88, chroma 0.06, hue H + 10; sparkles lightness 0.78, chroma 0.12, hue H + 10.
  - palette.night: lightness 0.25, chroma 0.11, hue H: the night side, laid over every thing at 78% opacity.
  - palette.accent: line violet, lightness 0.67, chroma 0.19, hue H + 5: the measuring lines and the number tags (the engine also colours the caption stripe with it).
  - palette.accent2: mint, lightness 0.85, chroma 0.18, hue H - 125: the measuring line once the answer lands, and the bracket and tag of a highlighted span (text on mint is palette.ink).
  - palette.answer: hot pink, lightness 0.67, chroma 0.23, hue H + 90: the answer tag only.
  - palette.check: warm yellow, lightness 0.86, chroma 0.16, hue H + 165 (75 degrees on from the pink): the check disc only.
  - palette.ink: deep ink, lightness 0.20, chroma 0.08, hue H + 14: the check mark, text on mint or yellow, text shadows.
  - palette.text: near-white, lightness 0.99, chroma 0.01: tag text, highlights.
  - palette.tag_<id>: ONE per named thing (id = the thing's snake_case id, the same id the assets use): the thing's real colour made vivid, lightness 0.58 to 0.76, chroma 0.12 to 0.22, at the real thing's hue (a warm thing's tag turns 10 to 25 degrees toward red from its real colour). White text must read on it, so never above lightness 0.76. Any two things' tags differ by at least 40 degrees of hue or 0.12 of lightness. This template's palette may hold up to 14 colours.
- Each thing's own colours come from its tag colour T = kit.palette['tag_' + id]: base h.mix(T, palette.text, 0.3 to 0.5) (more for things whose real colour is pale), dark detail T itself, a deeper detail h.mix(T, palette.night, 0.2), light detail h.mix(T, palette.text, 0.65), halo the base. A second real colour (patches, a core, a nucleus) keeps its own real hue at a similar lightness to the base.
- Type: kit.font(size, weight = 700) returns `${weight} ${size}px "Outfit", "Selawik", sans-serif` (a round geometric sans like the reference's); write "Selawik" as the plan's style font. Tags 42 px, the answer tag 60 px; sentence case (a capital first letter, the rest as written), never all caps.
- kit.title(ctx, text, x, y, o) draws text only (no box): palette.text, kit.font(o.size || 60, 700), centred on (x, y) (textAlign from o.align, default 'center'; textBaseline 'middle'), with a soft shadow (shadowColor palette.ink at 60%, shadowBlur 14, shadowOffsetY 2; reset the shadow after). o.k pops it (scale 0 to 1 with back(k / 0.25, 2.2)).
- kit.panel(ctx, x, y, w, hgt, o) is the tag's pill: h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, 0.27 * hgt) filled with o.accent || palette.accent at alpha o.alpha ?? 1. No border, no shadow, no gradient.
- The kit also returns these helpers (the shots and the assets call them; write them exactly):
  - kit.body(ctx, x, y, r, o): one glowing flat thing of radius r centred on (x, y). o = {base, dark, light, pattern ('bands', 'blotches' or 'none'), patches (a colour or null), ring (true only for a thing that really has a ring), ringColour, glow (0 to 1, default 1), t (seconds, default 0), seed (integer, default 3), path (optional function (ctx, x, y, r) that builds a non-round silhouette as a path), inner (optional function (ctx, x, y, r) that draws the thing's own features), star (true for a thing that shines: skip the night-side ellipse; instead the middle 60% of the radius is base mixed 45% toward white, as a radial gradient filling the silhouette path again, and the halo reaches 1.6 r)}. Every fill inside the body fills a path (the silhouette, an ellipse, a band built with ctx.rect), never ctx.fillRect. In this order:
    1. Halo: a radial gradient centred (x, y) from radius 0.9 r (base at alpha 0.5 * glow * (1 + 0.08 * Math.sin(t * 2 * Math.PI / 3))) to radius 1.4 r (alpha 0); fill a circle of radius 1.4 r. With o.path instead: the silhouette drawn 3 times, scaled 1.08, 1.16 and 1.24 about (x, y), in base at alpha 0.2, 0.12, 0.06 times glow.
    2. Ring, back half (o.ring): ctx.ellipse(x, y, 2.15 * r, 0.42 * r, -14 * Math.PI / 180, 0, 2 * Math.PI) stroked 0.2 * r wide in ringColour (default h.mix(base, palette.text, 0.2)) at alpha 0.85.
    3. ctx.save(); the silhouette (ctx.beginPath(); ctx.arc(x, y, r, 0, 2 * Math.PI), or o.path(ctx, x, y, r)); ctx.clip().
    4. Fill the silhouette with base: ctx.fill() right after ctx.clip() (the current path is still the silhouette), never a rectangle over its square.
    5. Pattern, seeded with const R = h.rng(o.seed || 3): 'bands': for k = 0 to 6, yy = y - r + (k + 0.5) * 2 * r / 7, a band (ctx.beginPath(); ctx.rect(...); ctx.fill()) from x - r to x + r, top yy - 0.06 * r - (k % 2) * 0.03 * r, height r * (0.10 + (k % 3) * 0.05), colours cycling dark, light, h.mix(dark, palette.night, 0.2), alpha 0.8. 'blotches': 5 ellipses, centre (x + (R() - 0.3) * 1.2 * r, y + (R() - 0.5) * 1.4 * r), radii r * (0.18 + 0.2 * R()) by r * (0.10 + 0.12 * R()), in dark at alpha 0.75. Patches (o.patches): 3 ellipses at (x - 0.3 r, y - 0.2 r) radii 0.5 r by 0.35 r, (x + 0.35 r, y + 0.25 r) radii 0.4 r by 0.3 r, (x + 0.1 r, y - 0.55 r) radii 0.3 r by 0.18 r.
    6. o.inner(ctx, x, y, r), if given.
    7. Night side: ctx.ellipse(x - 0.55 * r, y, 1.02 * r, 1.25 * r, 0, 0, 2 * Math.PI) filled with palette.night at alpha 0.78. It leaves a lit crescent on the right, about a quarter of the width at the middle; the pattern still shows dimly through the dark side.
    8. Highlight: a radial gradient at (x + 0.35 r, y - 0.38 r) from radius 0 (palette.text at alpha 0.16) to 0.3 r (alpha 0); fill a circle of radius 0.3 r there.
    9. ctx.restore().
    10. Ring, front half (o.ring): ctx.ellipse(x, y, 2.15 * r, 0.42 * r, -14 * Math.PI / 180, 0, Math.PI) (the half that passes in front, below the centre), h.mix(ringColour, palette.text, 0.25) at alpha 0.95, the same width.
    When r is under 6: only a circle of radius 6 in base and a circle of radius 14 in base at alpha 0.3 behind it (a dot keeps its glow).
  - kit.tag(ctx, text, x, y, o): a label tag. o = {color (default palette.accent), ink (text colour, default palette.text; palette.ink on mint or yellow), align ('center' default: centred on x; 'left': the tag's left edge at x; 'right': its right edge at x), size (default 42), k (seconds since it appeared; missing = fully shown)}. ctx.font = kit.font(size, 700); measure the text; the pill is the text width + 2 * 0.62 * size wide and 1.62 * size tall, vertically centred on y; clamp it so it stays inside x 60 to 1020. Pop: scale from 0 to 1 with back(k / 0.22, 2.2) about the pill's end that touches its thing (its left end for align 'left', its right end for 'right', its centre for 'center'); k < 0 draws nothing. Draw the pill with kit.panel(ctx, x0, y0, w, hgt, {accent: color}), then the text with fillText at the pill's exact centre (textAlign 'center', textBaseline 'middle'). Returns {x0, y0, x1, y1}.
  - kit.chip(ctx, text, x, y, o): the answer tag, centred on (x, y). o = {k}. Size 60 (shrink until the whole chip is at most 900 px wide); height 1.75 * size; a check disc of diameter 0.62 * height in palette.check, its centre 0.42 * height from the chip's left end; the text in palette.text starting 18 px after the disc; width = 0.42 * height + disc + 18 + text width + 0.42 * height; the pill in palette.answer, radius 0.24 * height. The check mark is a PATH, never a text glyph: with d the disc diameter and (cx, cy) its centre, moveTo(cx - 0.22 d, cy + 0.02 d), lineTo(cx - 0.06 d, cy + 0.18 d), lineTo(cx + 0.24 d, cy - 0.18 d), stroked in palette.ink, lineWidth 0.11 d, lineCap and lineJoin 'round'. The whole chip is rotated about (x, y): -3 degrees at rest; o.k pops it: scale 0 to 1 with back(k / 0.32, 2.6) while the rotation goes from -8 to -3 degrees. Draw the pill with kit.panel and the text with fillText.
  - kit.measure(ctx, x1, y1, x2, y2, o): the dotted measuring line. o = {color (default palette.accent), k (0 to 1, share drawn from (x1, y1), default 1), lt (seconds, for marching dashes), alpha (default 0.8)}. lineWidth 4, setLineDash([8, 14]), lineDashOffset = -(o.lt || 0) * 18, lineCap 'round'; a dot of radius 7 at (x1, y1) and at the drawn end; setLineDash([]) after.
  - kit.sparkle(ctx, x, y, s, colour, alpha): a four-point star: moveTo(x, y - s), quadraticCurveTo(x, y, x + s, y), quadraticCurveTo(x, y, x, y + s), quadraticCurveTo(x, y, x - s, y), quadraticCurveTo(x, y, x, y - s), closePath, fill.
- Backgrounds (kit.bg), 2 of them. Precompute the stars ONCE inside makeKit (const r = h.rng(11)), never inside bg:
  - "space": a vertical linear gradient (top colour at y 0, palette.bg at y 1056, the plum bottom colour at y 1920); an elliptical haze: ctx.save(); ctx.translate(540, 1056); ctx.scale(1, 1.77); a radial gradient centred (0, 0) of radius 650 from the haze colour at alpha 0.55 * (1 + 0.1 * Math.sin(lt * 2 * Math.PI / 5)) to alpha 0; fill the circle ctx.arc(0, 0, 650, 0, 2 * Math.PI) with it; ctx.restore() (it lifts the middle to about twice the corners' brightness: the engine rejects a background whose brightness varies too little); 170 star dots (radius 1 to 3.2 for 85% of them, 3 to 4.5 for the rest; 60% violet, 40% pale; alpha 0.35 to 0.95), every 4th one twinkling: alpha times 0.6 + 0.4 * Math.sin(lt * 2 * Math.PI / p + ph) with its own period p of 1.0 to 2.2 s and phase ph; 14 sparkles (s 6 to 13) in the sparkle colour, alpha 0.2 + 0.5 * (0.5 + 0.5 * Math.sin(lt * 2 * Math.PI / 1.4 + ph)); every star and sparkle drifts up 6 px per second (y = ((y0 - 6 * lt) % 1920 + 1920) % 1920); a vignette last: a radial gradient centred (540, 1000) from radius 650 (transparent) to 1250 (h.mix(palette.bg, '#000000', 0.45) at alpha 0.55).
  - "space_close": the same, with the haze centred at (540, 1000), radius 560, vertical scale 1.35 and alpha 0.65 (a pool of light behind the stage's thing), and 30 sparkles.
- kit.post: nothing but a very light vignette (corners darkened about 12%); no grain, wash or scanlines.

## motion
- One thing happens at a time, on the word that names it, and every arrival springs: a new thing slides in from the side, starting 760 px off its place and turned 40 degrees, both unwinding with back(k / 0.34, 1.25) (k = seconds since it started); it comes from the right in one shot and the left in the next. A thing that simply appears in place (the hook's subject) pops: scale 0 to 1 with back(k / 0.35, 2.5).
- Tags land with their words, never in a chain of delays after the thing: its name tag pops as it lands, or on the word of its name (h.cue) when that comes later (back(k / 0.22, 2.2)); its measuring line draws itself in 0.4 s (h.easeOut) ending on the word of its number, and its number tag pops on that word (h.cue of the spoken number, for example h.cue('six hundred')), at most 0.5 s after the voice says it. A tag shows only while its thing is on screen.
- Continuity: a thing that was on screen at the end of the previous shot starts this shot exactly where and how big it was, with its tags, then moves or shrinks to its new place in 0.6 s (h.easeInOut). Things that leave slide out to the left in 0.3 s (h.easeIn) with their tags.
- Every move shows something new (an arrival, a comparison, a zoom to a new scale, a traveller crossing a gap); between moves things, lines and tags hold still where they landed so they read (no drift of the content). Quiet life stays: halos breathe (kit.body o.t = lt), measuring-line dashes march (o.lt = lt), the stars twinkle and drift.
- Zoom out (to show something far bigger): everything already on stage shrinks about its own place to its true size beside the newcomer over 1.0 s (h.easeInOut) while the newcomer arrives (it slides in, or a horizon rises from the bottom of the frame); things that end under R 3 become dots with a ring callout (see shots).
- Zoom in (to show something far smaller): the dive by powers of ten in the shots section.
- The answer lands on a visual hit (the reference's payoff): the moment the view proves it (the last thing lands in the column, the last copy lands in the row, the traveller reaches the end, the counter lands), the whole content pulses (scale about the stage centre (540, 1000) from 1 to 1.035 in 0.12 s with h.easeOut, back to 1 in 0.45 s with back(k / 0.45, 2)), the measuring line turns from palette.accent to palette.accent2 in 0.2 s (h.mix), and 0.05 s later the answer tag pops; it then holds still to the end.
- Easing: back() for arrivals and pops, h.easeOut for lines drawing, h.easeInOut for moves and zooms, h.easeIn for leaving.

## story
- The fact sheet holds every real number a picture draws, with its unit: the sizes or distances of the things compared and the ratio computed from them; for "N fit across" both sizes and N; for a journey the distance, the speed and the time computed from them, in the unit the line says, with the assumption the line states (non-stop, or so many hours a day); for the close the familiar object's real size and the other thing's size scaled by the same ratio.
- The story sets the shots: as many as it needs, each with one point. The usual order is the one that works: open on a question about the subject, measure something the viewer knows, compare step by step, answer, close on the analogy. A yardstick, step or turn the story does not need is left out.
- The cast has one speaker, "narrator" (voice george: a warm British narrator), calm, curious and a little wry, at a measured pace; address the viewer as "you" now and then. A shot takes the sentences its point needs (one to three): a shot that explains a comparison takes more words, a shot that lands a number fewer. Numbers are written as words, usually one new number per sentence, each said once (the steps give their numbers, the answer gives the answer, the close gives the analogy without repeating an earlier number). Tones: the hook curious, facts matter-of-fact or warm, the turn awed or surprised, the answer confident, the close warm or wry.

## plan
- First decide the THINGS and the NUMBERS, all real:
  - SUBJECT: the brief's main thing (the one the question is about).
  - YARDSTICK: one familiar thing every viewer has a feel for, to start from.
  - STEPS: 0 to 3 real things between the yardstick and the subject, each the next one up (or down) in size or distance; for a process, each the next real stage of the subject instead.
  - QUANTITIES: what each picture compares (width, length, height, distance, speed, time, mass or count), each a real value with its unit. Things compared in one picture share one quantity and unit; the story may move from one quantity to the next (a distance, then a speed, then the time it takes). If you are not certain of a value, pick another thing you are certain of; never invent one.
  - ANSWER: the answer to the hook's question in at most 4 plain words, or one real number with its unit (at most 20 characters). Never a multiplier written with "x" and never a hype word.
  - ANALOGY: a familiar object standing in for one thing and the size the other thing gets at the same ratio ("if [small thing] were a [familiar object, real size], [big thing] would be [scaled size] across"), computed from the real sizes.
- Pick each shot's VIEW by its question and its ratio (the bigger real size over the smaller), from the shots section: side by side (ratio up to 8; a lineup on one ground line for things that stand); horizon (ratio 8 to 1000); copies (the line says how many fit across, up to 250); dive (ratio above 1000, or a chain going far smaller; backwards for far bigger); gap (a distance between things); journey (how long to cross a gap at a speed); column (one quantity across 3 to 5 things). The answer and the close use the view that proves them. The VIEW is how this template stages the shot's device from the story (its yardstick, compare, zoom, counter, route or reveal).
- Scale: in each shot every thing on screen is drawn at one scale, so sizes and gaps on screen are in their TRUE ratio: R values from h.toScale with the real sizes (divide two R values and two real sizes: they must match). Pick the scale so the things read: together they span at least a quarter of the frame width, nothing important is cut off unless the view crops on purpose (the horizon, the dive), and a thing that comes out under R 3 is a dot with a ring callout. Never enlarge one thing alone, and never shrink a ratio to make both fit (a thing a hundred times wider drawn three times wider is a wrong fact on screen).
- Every shot idea starts with these lines, then says what moves on which word:
  - "VIEW: [side by side | lineup | horizon | copies | dive | gap | journey | column]".
  - "THINGS: [id] R [px] at ([x], [y]), ..." for every thing on screen with its true-ratio R ("dot" for one under R 3), or "COLUMN: [id] R [px], [id] R [px], ... top to bottom".
  - "TAGS: [id] '[Name]'" for each name tag; "NUMBERS: [id] '[value] [unit]'" for each number tag (digits with thousands commas and the unit, written the way people read it).
  - "ANSWER: '[the answer]'" in the answer and close shots.
- Structure: the story's shots in order, one beat each:
  1. hook: the subject alone, big (R 250 to 280) at the stage centre (540, 1000), popping in on the line's first word; its name tag; its measuring line draws itself under it but no number tag yet (the number is the question). Line: ONLY one question that ends with "?", naming the brief's subject (about its size, distance, number or fate); never a definition, and never the answer or its number after the question (the answer belongs to the answer shot).
  2. yardstick: the yardstick alone (R 200 to 260); its name tag, its measuring line and its number tag. Line: start from what the viewer knows: the yardstick and its number.
  3. step (0 to 3 shots): the next thing compared with the previous one by the view their ratio needs, both with their tags and numbers. Line: the new thing, its number and how it compares with the previous one, in plain words.
  4. turn (optional): the surprise that changes the picture: "But [the surprising real fact]", often a zoom out to a new scale. The line starts with "But".
  5. answer: the view that proves the answer, landing on the visual hit with the answer tag (the last thing lands in the column, the row of copies fills, the traveller reaches the end as its counter lands). When a step already said the answer's number, the answer line gives the SAME truth as a new vivid real comparison instead, never the number again. Line: the answer to the hook's question, plainly, the answer words in its second half. This is the music's payoff_shot.
  6. close: the ANALOGY drawn at the true ratio with the view that ratio needs: the familiar object where the viewer can see it, the other thing at its scaled size, each with its name tag and its number tag; the answer tag at the top fully shown from the first frame (it does not pop again). Line: the analogy, calm and a little wry, echoing the hook's question.
- The picture changes every shot: a new thing arrives, the scale zooms, a traveller moves or a new comparison appears. The same single thing alone fills at most 2 shots (the hook and one more).
- On screen only names and numbers: tags hold a thing's real name (1 to 3 words, at most 18 characters) or its number with its unit (a speed in km/h, a time in hours or years: never a speed tagged as a distance); the answer tag holds the answer. Never colour names, filler words or sentences.
- Assets, 2 (or 3): the SUBJECT first (its id is the subject's id), then "bodies" (every other named thing, the familiar analogy object included, chosen by param "which"); see the assets section. Lines, tags, the answer tag, ring callouts, the traveller, the column line and the bracket are drawn by the shots with the kit's helpers.
- Palette: the style colours named in the look section, plus one tag_<id> per named thing (the subject and every value of "which").
- Music: Uplifting, Mystical, Bright (a wondrous synth bed with a gentle pulse), 90-110 bpm (the reference is silent; this is the usual feel of the kind); payoff_shot = the answer shot. Sound effects: a soft pop when the answer tag lands and a whoosh on a zoom; nothing else.

## assets
- Two assets (a third only for one more thing with a shape "bodies" cannot carry):
  1. The SUBJECT (hero, listed first), kind "prop", animated true: the brief's main thing. Params "state" (only if the steps change it: its real states, for example its stages; otherwise one state) and "glow" (0 to 1, default 1).
  2. "bodies", kind "prop", animated true: every other named thing, one drawing per thing chosen by param "which" (exactly the thing ids used in the palette's tag_<id> names), plus "glow" (0 to 1, default 1).
- Geometry contract (the shots rely on it): box 800 x 800 (1400 x 800 if one of its things really has a ring); the thing is centred on (0, 0) and its radius (half its longest side for a non-round thing) is exactly 272 px at scale 1, so a shot draws it with radius R using scale R / 272. A thing that stands (a person, a tree, a building) is drawn upright with its feet at y 272, so it stands on a ground line at y with h.asset(id, x, y - R, R / 272). Halo and ring stay inside the box. A state changes the look (colours, pattern, flares, a collapse to a bright point), never the drawn size: the shots set the size.
- Draw every thing with kit.body(ctx, 0, 0, 272, {...}) and its colours from T = kit.palette['tag_' + id] as the look section says; o.t = p.t, o.glow = p.glow, a different o.seed per thing. Then make it that real thing: pattern 'bands' for a striped surface, 'blotches' for a mottled one, patches for a second real colour, o.inner for its own features (darker spots, pits, swirls, a core, a nucleus), o.ring only if the real thing has a ring, o.path for a non-round silhouette (an oval, a rod with round ends, a disc with a dimple, a lumpy blob, a standing figure, a building) at its real proportions. Parts that stick out (spikes, tails, flares, jets) are drawn after kit.body: thin rounded shapes in base on the right half and h.mix(base, palette.night, 0.6) on the left half, never outlined.
- Every thing must read as itself at R 20 as well as at R 300 (true scale often draws a thing small): its own colour, its own pattern and its own silhouette, clearly different from the other things.
- No text inside assets.

## shots
- Draw order: kit.bg; then ctx.save() and the content (the pulse scale): measuring lines and the column line, the things (h.asset), ring callouts, the tags; ctx.restore(); then the answer tag (kit.chip). Use "space_close" when one thing fills the stage (hook, yardstick), "space" otherwise.
- Things: const t = h.asset('<subject id>', x, y, R / 272, {state, glow: 1}) or h.asset('bodies', x, y, R / 272, {which: '<id>', glow: 1}); companions and copies glow 0.7. Each thing's R and place as in the idea's THINGS or COLUMN line. Keep what h.asset returns (its drawn box): every tag and line of that thing is placed from it, so they stay with it while it moves.
- Tags and lines belong to their thing and are placed from it (name tag above, dotted measuring line under it, number tag under the line, one colour per thing):
  - Name tag: kit.tag(ctx, name, ...h.label(t, 'above', 64), {color: kit.palette['tag_' + id], k}).
  - Measuring line: kit.measure(ctx, t.x0, t.y1 + 44, t.x1, t.y1 + 44, {k, lt}); for a height, a vertical line from (t.x1 + 30, t.y0) to (t.x1 + 30, t.y1).
  - Number tag: kit.tag(ctx, number, ...h.label(t, 'below', 108), {k}) (palette.accent, white text); for a height, kit.tag(ctx, number, ...h.label(t, 'right', 60), {align: 'left', k}).
  - A distance between two things: const m = h.measure(a, b); kit.measure(ctx, m.from[0], m.from[1], m.to[0], m.to[1], {k, lt}); its number tag at (m.mid[0], m.mid[1] - 90).
  - A thing under R 30 gets no measuring line under it: its number tag sits on its right (h.label(t, 'right', 30), align 'left').
  - A thing under R 3 at the shot's scale is a dot with a callout: draw it with h.asset at its true R (never bigger, and no ring of your own); the engine draws it as a bright dot with a ring of R 25, and the box h.asset returns is that ring's, so its tags go beside the ring, never on the dot: kit.tag(ctx, name, ...h.label(t, 'above', 50), {color}).
  - Where two tags would touch, move one to the 'left' or 'right' of its own thing (align 'right' or 'left'), never to a row away from it; 24 px between pills, inside x 60 to 1020.
- Composition: the stage centre is (540, 1000), a little below the middle of the frame. The picture's weight sits in the middle of the frame, its tags above and its numbers below, and the band above the caption (y 1100 to 1440) holds measuring lines, numbers, a horizon or a ground line, never nothing. Keep it there: the engine's frame audit rejects a reel whose picture sits high (it wants at least a third of the detail in the lower half and the detail's centre between 42% and 62% of the height), so a row of tags near the top of the frame or things centred high fail it. A thing with its tags fits y 200 to 1440: R 280 at most at the stage centre.
- Views (the idea's VIEW line):
  - side by side (ratio up to 8): one thing at (540, 1000); two things centred on y 1000, the previous on the left, the new one on the right, 80 px between their edges, the pair centred on x 540 (x1 = 540 - (2 * R1 + 80 + 2 * R2) / 2 + R1, x2 = x1 + R1 + 80 + R2; for a thing with a ring use 2.15 * R as its half-width), the bigger R 200 to 260 and the smaller from h.toScale; if the pair is wider than 920 px, both R shrink by one factor.
  - lineup (things that stand): their feet on one invisible ground line at y 1250 (no floor is drawn), left to right with 60 px between them, heights from h.toScale with the tallest 600 to 800 px tall, each with its height line and number tag on its right.
  - horizon (ratio 8 to 1000): RB = 2200 (up to 4000 when the small one would come out under R 3) and RS from h.toScale; the big one centred at (540, 1200 + RB), so only its top edge shows, a glowing curved horizon across the frame at y 1200; the small one at (540, 1200 - RS - 60) with its name tag above it and its number tag on its right (align 'left'); the big one's name tag sits on its own surface at (540, 1300) and its number tag at (540, 1390). The horizon rises from below the frame (its centre from y 1920 + RB + 200 to 1200 + RB in 1.0 s, h.easeInOut) while the small one shrinks to its true size.
  - copies (the line says how many fit across, N up to 250): the big one whole at (540, 900) with R 400; the copies at r = 400 / N, edge to edge along its middle (copy i at x 140 + r + 2 * r * i, y 900), each an h.asset at r (glow 0.7; the row is the point, so a copy stays at its true r even under R 3), filling from left to right in 1.5 s so the last one lands on the word of N; under the big one, at h.label(big, 'below', 64), a count tag in the small thing's colour counting the copies as they land ('[count] [plural name]').
  - dive (ratio above 1000, or a chain going far smaller): the picture dives by powers of ten about the small thing's place (px, py): z = Math.exp(Math.log(ZF) * h.easeInOut(k)), k from 0 to 1 between the word that starts the dive and the word that names the small thing (1.5 to 3 s), ZF the zoom at which the small thing has R 100 to 140. Every thing is drawn at its true R times z, centred at (px + (x - px) * z, py + (y - py) * z), while that point drifts to (540, 1000); a thing whose R passes 1500 fades out (alpha 0 by R 6000, then it is not drawn) with its tags; the small thing is a dot with a ring callout and its name tag from the dive's first frame until its R reaches 20, so the frame is never empty mid-dive. Real things in between may pass by, tagged while their R is 20 to 400. A scale bar makes the dive felt: a fixed dotted line kit.measure(ctx, 340, 1320, 740, 1320, {lt}) and under it, at (540, 1384), a number tag saying what 400 px spans now, as a power of ten with its unit (for example 1 m, 10 cm, 1 cm, 1 mm, ...), changing as the zoom passes each power. Run it backwards (z from 1 down to 1 / ZF) for a far bigger thing.
  - gap (a distance): both ends and the gap at one scale on y 1150 (the band above the caption), centre to centre 840 px (h.toScale([distance, radius 1, radius 2], 840), the ends at x 120 and 960); an end under R 3 is a dot with a callout; the distance line from h.measure(a, b) draws itself, ending on the word of the distance, its number tag above its middle. The ends are usually shown big in the shot before, then zoom out to the gap.
  - journey (how long a gap takes at a speed): the gap, plus a traveller and a counter. The traveller is a bright dot, kit.body(ctx, x, 1150, 4, {base: its tag colour}), moving from m.from to m.to (x = h.lerp(m.from[0], m.to[0], p), p = h.easeInOut from the word that starts the trip to the word of the time); 80 px under it (its x kept inside 200 to 880) a counter tag in the traveller's colour rolls the elapsed time, total * p in the line's unit (h.fmt), and lands on the total on that word. The speed is on the traveller's name tag ('[speed] km/h') in the shot that says it. When the story shows who travels, that thing is drawn big in an earlier shot and shrinks to the dot.
  - column (one quantity across 3 to 5 things): one vertical dotted line kit.measure(ctx, 540, 400, 540, yEnd) drawn first, drawing itself downward in the first 0.4 s. The things stack TOUCHING along it, centred on x 540, from y 400 down: k = Math.min(1000 / (sum of the real sizes), 600 / (the biggest real size)); R = k * size / 2; each centre = the previous thing's bottom + R; the whole stack fits y 400 to 1400. A thing that really has a ring keeps R at most 210 (its ring reaches 2.15 R). For a process, the stages instead sit in order with 30 px gaps, the last (the result) R 150 to 170 and the others R 70 to 100. Each tag beside its thing, alternating sides from the right: kit.tag(ctx, name, ...h.label(t, 'right', 26), {align: 'left', color}) or kit.tag(ctx, name, ...h.label(t, 'left', 26), {align: 'right', color}). Things under R 30 share ONE tag, always on the left, at the first small one's height (their names joined by commas). If a tag would come within 80 px (vertically) of another tag on the same side, it goes on the other side; never two tags over each other. Every thing in the column is measured in the same quantity and unit.
  - Timing in the column: the column fills from the line's first word, in the order the line names the things (the others after): the i-th to arrive (from 0) starts sliding in at sh.lines[0].start + 0.2 + i * 0.3 and lands 0.34 s later, its tag popping as it lands, so a thing the line names has landed with its tag by 0.5 s after its name's word (h.cue); the answer moment TP is the moment the last one lands; at TP the pulse, the mint line and the answer tag.
- The answer tag: kit.chip(ctx, ANSWER, 540, 270, {k: lt - TP}) in the answer shot (TP: the view's hit); in the close shot kit.chip(ctx, ANSWER, 540, 270) with no k (fully shown from the first frame). Nothing else above y 360.
- A highlighted span (only if the answer is about room left over or the part that matters): a mint bracket on the left of the column (a 5 px palette.accent2 line with 16 px feet, round caps, 16 px left of the widest thing in that span) and a mint tag (palette.ink text) left of it with that span's real number and unit.
- Every new thing, tag and number arrives on the word that names it (h.cue of that word); each holds still and readable at least 0.8 s.
- The answer tag (kit.chip) is drawn ONLY in the answer and close shots; any other shot shows no chip.
- Halos, haze and glows are radial gradients filled inside a circle (ctx.arc), never ctx.fillRect over the frame (the engine rejects a solid fill over 30% of the background).
- Text: tags 42 px, the answer tag 60 px; measure every text and shrink (never under 36 px) until its pill fits inside x 60 to 1020. Never two tags or two texts overlapping, never a tag over another thing's disc (a horizon's own tags sit on its own surface), nothing important below y 1440 (the caption lives there).
