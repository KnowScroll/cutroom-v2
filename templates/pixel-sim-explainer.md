# pixel-sim-explainer

## about
A low-resolution pixel-art explainer of a dramatic physical event at two scales. First the hidden mechanism plays out as a simple game-like simulation of the real process, while a live counter climbs a real quantity to its tipping point; the event erupts, the frame burns to white, and the same event is shown at its real scale under a few big stat cards. It ends by pushing into a point of light and cutting back to its own first frame, so it loops. For explosions, collapses, chain reactions, eruptions, impacts, outbreaks and anything with a micro cause and a macro effect. Not for anatomy tours, procedures, history narratives or character stories.

## look
- Pixel art: the engine pixelates every frame to 180 art pixels across (each art pixel is a 6 x 6 px block), then draws all text crisp on top. Draw for that grid: every shape, line, dot, border and gap at least 6 px and preferably a multiple of 6; no hairlines, no thin strokes, no soft shadows, no blur, no ctx.shadowBlur. kit.post draws nothing (no grain, vignette, wash or pixelation of its own).
- Shading is flat colour steps, never smooth gradients: each surface uses a 3 or 4 step ramp of one hue family (shadow, base, light, highlight), with the light from the top-left (a lighter crescent top-left, a darker crescent bottom-right). No dark outlines: shapes are separated by colour steps. A glow or sky is 4 to 6 flat bands or concentric rings, never a radial or linear gradient.
- Colour is a set of relations; pick the actual hues for the brief (the real colour of its main thing), then keep these relations:
  - Micro world: a near-black ground (OKLCH lightness about 0.09, chroma about 0.03) tinted cool (blue-violet), covering about 80% of the frame. The main subject is ONE bright hue family in a 4-step ramp from lightness 0.47 to 0.78, chroma up to 0.16. Secondary elements (products, particles of another kind, a second material) take the split-complement hues of the main subject (about +150 and -150 degrees round the hue wheel) at similar lightness. Small fast particles are near-white.
  - The event (fire, light, energy): a hot ramp from deep red through orange and amber to cream (lightness 0.45 to 0.92, the hue turning about 40 degrees toward yellow as it gets lighter, chroma highest in the mid tones), then pure white for the flash.
  - Macro world: a dark ground or deep space (lightness 0.10 to 0.20, low chroma) and a cool backdrop (violet to indigo bands, lightness 0.27 to 0.42, chroma about 0.08) with single-pixel stars; the event in the warm family about 120 to 155 degrees round from the backdrop hue, the hot ramp where it still burns.
  - Contrast: the brightest highlight at least 10:1 against the ground.
- Backgrounds (kit.bg), one per world: "micro" is the near-black ground with a faint field of dim dots or a grid of the real medium (enough variation to read, never a flat colour) drifting slowly; "macro" is where the event really happens: a landscape with a horizon if it happens on a planet's surface, deep space with nebula haze and twinkling star pixels if it happens in space. Haze and sky are full-width horizontal bands or soft round clouds of flat colour, never floating rectangles or blocks that look like glitches.
- The subject is the brightest, most saturated thing on screen and reads as the real thing at a glance (its true colour, lit from the top-left). Around it the background stays dark: no discs, target rings or halos behind a subject wider than 1.2 times the subject itself.
- kit.title draws text only (no box, no backing of its own); kit.panel draws the boxes. HUD panels (kit.panel) are the only typography backing: dark navy fill (lightness about 0.19, slightly cool, 90% opaque), a 6 px border in a muted tint of the current world's colour (micro: a dark tone of the main hue; macro: a dusty rose), two small 6 px corner ticks top-left and top-right, square corners.
- Type (kit.font): bold monospace ("Consolas", then "Courier New"), UPPERCASE, near-white. Sizes: caption lines 52 to 60 px, the big counter or stat number 110 to 150 px, labels under objects 40 to 46 px. Text is crisp (the engine draws it after pixelating), so never draw text with a pixel effect of your own.

## motion
- Never still: in every frame at least three things move (particles flowing along the process, the subject's inside churning or pulsing by 3 to 6%, the background drifting, sparks or debris travelling), so no two frames 0.1 s apart look the same. A counter shot animates the process the number measures, not just the number.
- One thing happens at a time, and the viewer is told what it is: every visible event (a hit, a split, a collapse, a flash, a count step) takes 0.8 to 1.5 s, holds its result at least 0.5 s, and happens while the caption panel names it. Never several unexplained changes at once.
- Cause and effect are drawn: a moving particle leaves a 3 to 5 block trail along its path; what it hits flashes for 2 frames in the hot colour before it changes; arrows (6 px thick, stair-stepped) point from cause to effect when the link is not obvious.
- The simulation follows the real process of the topic, never an invented one: it shows the real parts (named with labels) doing what they really do, as simple shapes (balls, blocks, shells, layers) on a regular layout. Use h.rng(seed) for any randomness, so it is deterministic.
- The counter is the heartbeat: one panel shows one real quantity of the topic as a NUMBER in digits (110 to 150 px) with its unit on a small line under it, climbing to its real tipping value, updating every 0.5 s with values from the REFERENCE. A word in the counter's place is wrong. The counter's quantity is the one this shot's narration talks about (if the line says the core's temperature climbs, the counter shows the temperature), never a statistic from another beat. The number snaps to each new value (no rolling digits), with a 1-frame flash of the panel border.
- The build-up reads as spreading or growing: the reacting part grows, spreads ring by ring or packs tighter until it fills the middle of the frame.
- The flash: when the event peaks, the frame brightens through the hot ramp to near-white over 0.6 s, holds pure white for 0.4 s, and the next shot starts from that white (fade in from white over 0.3 s).
- Macro scene: the event grows slowly and steadily (a flash, a shock ring racing outward, the shape swelling and spreading): never still. One passing darkening (a checkerboard-dithered dark overlay, every other art pixel, sliding across) adds drama once.
- Camera: mostly locked. One pull-back in the micro scene (from one part to the whole, 0.8 to 1.2 s, easeInOut) and one push-in at the very end toward a small sparkle, scaling up until its glow fills the frame.
- Loop ending: the last shot pushes into a bright round glow, then match-cuts on that round shape to an extreme close-up of the opening subject, which pulls back to exactly the hook's composition (title panel with its text, subject, label), held for the last second.
- Easing: easeOut for things arriving, easeIn for growth that accelerates, easeInOut for the camera.

## plan
- Structure (7 shots, one line each; adapt to the brief). Give every shot one beat name:
  1. hook: the title panel (the brief's subject in 1 to 3 words) over the whole real thing the viewer knows (its familiar outside: the volcano, the star, the planet), big in the middle, with the micro part shown at its heart (a cut-away, a glowing core, an inset) and labelled; one surprising true fact or question in the line.
  2. mechanism: the one real cause of the event, as a simple cause-and-effect animation of its real parts, each part labelled with its real name.
  3. buildup: the mechanism repeats or accumulates while the counter panel climbs a real quantity toward the tipping point; the caption names what is building up.
  4. climax: the tipping point (the counter's final real value), the event erupts and the frame burns to white; the caption gives how fast it happens (a real time figure).
  5. scale: from the white, the event where it really happens, at real scale, growing; the caption names the most famous real instance with its year or place, if the reference gives one.
  6. stats: the event still growing; 2 or 3 stat cards ONE AT A TIME in the same place, each replacing the previous one (each: a short top line, a big number in digits with its unit, a short bottom line), each the most striking real number about it.
  7. loop: push into a point of light on the event; match-cut to the opening subject close-up, pulling back to the hook's exact composition. The line lands the takeaway: why it matters.
- Every on-screen number, name and step must be real and come from the REFERENCE; leave a stat out rather than invent it. The counter's quantity and values are real (for example a mass, a temperature, a count of reactions), never a made-up "population", "level" or "index": the quantity's name and unit must appear in the REFERENCE (no invented units such as "energy units"; a scale such as an explosivity index only under its real name and range).
- Panel and label words name real things, real steps or real numbers of the topic, in the narration's terms: no filler panel titles ("MONITOR", "SYSTEM", "STATUS"), no colour names as labels.
- Lines: one narrator, bf_emma. Short punchy sentences; every number and name in a spoken line is written as words (the voice reads them); digits appear only in the panels. Say the brief's subject in line 1.
- List the hero FIRST in "assets": the real thing in cut-away, its familiar outside with the inner part that drives the event visible inside it (a volcano cone cut open to its magma chamber and vent, a star cut open to its layers and core), with a param "state" listing each real state the mechanism needs (for example calm, building, collapsing, erupting) and "heat" (0 to 1: how strongly the inner part glows in the hot colour). The hook shows it whole; the mechanism and buildup shots push in on its inner part. The second asset is the event at its real scale with param "grow" (0 to 1: from a first flash to its full size and true shape) and "burn" (0 to 1: how much of the hot ramp still shows). A third asset only if the macro scene needs one distinct crafted object for scale (a planet, a landmark). Particles, counters, panels, rings, sparkles, arrows and the white flash are drawn by the shots.
- Asset descriptions give the silhouette in simple pixel terms (how many art pixels across, which round clusters, rings or blocks, which ramp colours where) and the event's TRUE shape for the topic: a ground explosion is a dome and cloud, an exploding star is an expanding glowing shell with ragged filaments around a bright point, a volcanic eruption is a tall billowing grey-brown ash column spreading into an umbrella cloud with glowing ejecta at its base (never a capped T or a mushroom), an impact is a crater with a ring wave.
- Music: feel Driving Action Intense, 110-130 bpm (the reference bed is 120 bpm under the whole reel); payoff_shot is the climax shot. Sound effects: a pop when each caption or stat card appears and on counter steps; a whoosh into the white flash and on the final push-in; one boom when the event appears at real scale.

## assets
- Draw at the final size in 1080 px space but think in 6 px art pixels: build every shape from filled circles, rings and rectangles whose sizes and positions are multiples of 6; no line thinner than 6 px.
- The hero sprite: the real micro part as a compact readable shape 240 to 400 px across (a round cluster of 7 to 15 balls, a layered sphere, a crystal, a cell), each part with its own 3-step ramp (dark crescent bottom-right, base, light spot top-left) and at most a thin halo of 1 or 2 rings of the darkest ramp tone at falling opacity, no wider than 1.2 times the sprite (never a big disc or target rings around it). Each "state" must look clearly different at a glance (compressed is visibly smaller and brighter, split is two separated pieces, collapsed is a tiny bright point). p.heat tints it toward the hot ramp and adds a hot rim ring.
- The macro event: the topic's true shape at real scale, 600 to 900 px wide at grow 1, built from overlapping rings, blobs and filaments, each a 3 to 4 step ramp; a bright core in the hot ramp scaled by p.burn; at small grow it is a bright point with a small flare. It must read as one clear shape at full size and at a quarter size.
- No text inside assets.

## shots
- Draw the shot's world background first (kit.bg micro or macro), then the simulation or scene, then the HUD panels on top. Never paint a solid fill over the background except the white flash.
- Subjects are big and whole: every subject stays fully inside x 60 to 1020 (never cropped by the frame edge). In the hook and mechanism shots the hero fills 35 to 60% of the frame width, centred around x 540, y 850 to 1050, and stays readable: glows around it stay under its own brightness so its shape and colours never wash out to a white blob; in the buildup one large instance of the hero (400 px or more) stays the focus in the middle while smaller copies or particles around it react; in the scale, stats and loop shots the event fills 50 to 85% of the frame width, centred at y 750 to 1150. Never a tiny sprite alone in an empty frame. Nothing important below y 1400 (captions live there): the whole event, from its base or ground to its top, fits between y 380 and 1380, so a landscape's horizon and landmarks sit at y 1100 to 1350.
- HUD layout: the caption panel centred at the top (y 200 to 340), the counter or stat panel directly under it (y 370 to 620) with a gap of at least 24 px. Panels sit above the subject, never over it and never over each other. At most 2 panels on screen. Every shot shows its caption panel for its whole length (only the pure white hold of the flash may hide it).
- Every panel is drawn in exactly the frames its text is on screen: draw kit.panel and its text together, sized to the measured text plus 24 to 32 px padding on each side; never an empty or placeholder panel, never text wider than its panel. Caption lines are UPPERCASE, at most 22 characters each, at most 2 lines. Stat cards and the counter: a short top line (40 to 46 px), the big number (110 to 150 px), a short unit or bottom line (40 to 46 px), each on its own row inside the card with at least 12 px between rows and 18 px from the border, so no text touches the border or another line. Numbers are written the way people read them: 1,000 or 1.2 BILLION or 10+, never "1000S" or "10S".
- Labels (1 to 3 words, the real name of the part) sit right under their object, 40 to 46 px, each on its own small kit.panel centred on the label text; no other boxes anywhere (no empty frames, brackets or placeholder rectangles).
- Panel content switches on exact moments (no fade): new content appears in one frame with a 0.1 s border flash, and holds at least 1.5 s.
- Each shot starts moving in its first 0.3 s and something is always growing, spreading, ticking or drifting.
- The white flash covers the full frame: paint it with a radial gradient from white at the centre to near-white at the edges (a solid full-frame rectangle is rejected), drawn before the panels. The last shot's final second matches the hook's first frame (same title panel and text, subject, label and positions).

## checks
pixel grid: 180
beats: hook, mechanism, buildup, climax, scale, stats, loop
required beats: hook, mechanism, buildup, climax, scale, loop

## director
- Flag: a counter or stat card without a number; stat cards stacked or overlapping; a shot without its caption panel; big rings or discs behind a subject; anything that is not on the pixel grid (smooth gradients, hairlines, blurred glows, soft shadows); dark outlines; a simulation that shows an invented process instead of the topic's real mechanism; any moment where the viewer cannot tell what is happening (several unexplained changes at once, a change the caption does not name, sprites too small to read); an empty or placeholder panel; panel text wider than its panel or longer than 2 lines; filler panel titles; labels that are colour names or not the real names of parts; a counter that does not show one real quantity with its unit; subjects smaller than the shots section asks; anything important in the caption band below y 1400; a white flash longer than 1.5 s; a macro scene that does not show where the event really happens; an ending that does not return to the opening frame.
- Check every number, name and step against the reference and standard knowledge; remove a stat rather than keep a doubtful one.
