# speedramp-mechanism-explainer

## about
A chunky pixel-art machine explainer that makes time a visible variable. It opens on an everyday device at REAL SPEED with the question "how does it work?", pushes into the part that does the work, and slows time down 256 times so the motion becomes visible. Then it walks down the device one part per shot, from the working end toward the power source, each step a numbered question ("what drives that?") answered on a title placard, while the speed doubles back up shot by shot. At the end it pulls back to the whole device in x-ray, speeds through to real speed and closes on its own first frame. A speed HUD at the top (speed badge, dial, rate counter) is always on screen. For machines and devices whose working cycle is too fast to see (motors, pumps, engines, printers, drives, screens, switches, anything that repeats many times a second). Not for slow processes, history, biology tours or character stories.

## look
- Clean and readable above all: at any moment the frame holds the HUD row, the title placard, ONE part of the device (big) and at most 3 callouts. Nothing else: no extra insets, icons, arrows, floating diagrams, scattered labels, frames or boxes around the subject, and no glows, halos or light blobs wider than 1.1 times the part they belong to.
- Pixel art: the engine pixelates every frame to 180 art pixels across (each art pixel is a 6 x 6 px block), then draws all text crisp on top. Draw for that grid: every shape, line, border and gap at least 6 px and preferably a multiple of 6; no hairlines, no soft shadows, no blur, no ctx.shadowBlur. kit.post draws only a light vignette (no grain, no pixelation of its own).
- Rendering is toon-shaded 3D faked in 2D: each surface is 3 flat bands of one hue (shadow, base, light) with the light from the top-left, a 6 px dark ink rim around every part, and a 12 px seam of checker dither between two bands (alternate 6 x 6 squares of the two tones: for each 6 px cell, fill it with the darker tone when (col + row) % 2 is 0). Cylinders get vertical bands (light stripe left of centre, shadow at the right edge); boxes get a light top face, a base front face and a shadow side face. No smooth gradients on parts.
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() or ctx.stroke() after them. h.asset('id', x, y, scale, params) takes NO ctx either (its first argument is the asset id string).
- After kit.bg, never paint a solid rectangle over the frame or a large part of it (no full-frame wash, no flat backdrop, no blur panel): kit.bg is the whole background.
- Colour is a set of relations (OKLCH); keep them, whatever the topic:
  - Inside world: ground deep navy (lightness 0.20 to 0.24, chroma about 0.06, hue about 280) covering most of the frame; out-of-focus background shapes in the same hue a little lighter (lightness 0.28 to 0.34, chroma 0.04). The inside background is DARK: its brightest point stays under lightness 0.36, so the parts and the HUD are the brightest things on screen.
  - Parts: each part in its REAL colour if it has a typical one (copper is copper, glass is pale and see-through); otherwise neighbouring parts get different hue families at least 60 degrees apart, lightness 0.60 to 0.90, chroma 0.10 to 0.18, so each part reads on its own against the navy. Metal greys are cool greys (lightness 0.55 to 0.85, chroma 0.02, hue about 270).
  - HUD and text: cream (lightness about 0.95, chroma about 0.04, hue about 100) plates; ink (lightness about 0.22, chroma about 0.04, hue about 300) for text on cream and for rims; PRIMARY red (lightness about 0.63, chroma about 0.20, hue about 29) for the REAL SPEED badge, step badges, sublines, lit chips and pop words; SECONDARY blue (lightness about 0.54, chroma about 0.16, hue about 262) for the SLOWER badge; GOLD (lightness about 0.82, chroma about 0.14, hue about 85) for the dial rim, the counter's label plate and the FASTER flash.
  - Everyday world (hook and loop only): the real place where the device is used, mid-light (lightness 0.55 to 0.70, chroma about 0.08), with a cream floor or table band.
  - palette.accent is the PRIMARY red (the engine colours the caption bar with it); palette.accent2 is the GOLD; palette.bg the navy ground; palette.ink the ink; palette.cream the cream; palette.blue the SECONDARY blue.
- Backgrounds (kit.bg):
  - "inside": the navy ground with 4 to 6 huge out-of-focus vertical shapes (rounded columns 120 to 260 px wide, as tall as the frame, lightness 0.28 to 0.34) drifting sideways 10 to 20 px per second; behind the subject a set of 6 to 9 concentric rings (6 px wide, cream at 10 to 16% opacity, spaced 60 px, centred at x 540, y 1000) that expand slowly outward (radius + (lt * 30) % 60); a vignette darkening the corners.
  - "everyday": the real place where the device is used, drawn simply in flat bands (a wall, a floor or table band, 2 to 4 big simple shapes typical of the place, a few round warm lights as flat discs with a 2-ring halo), mid-light so the HUD reads on it; a slow light breathing so it is never still.
- Type: kit.font is "Gelasio" (bold) for headlines and the speed badge. Sublines, callouts, chips and counter digits use ctx.font = "bold <size>px Barlow" (counter digits "bold <size>px Cascadia Mono"). Everything UPPERCASE.
- kit.title draws text only (no box). kit.panel draws a cream plate with a 6 px ink border, square corners.
- The kit also returns three HUD helpers that every shot calls (so the HUD is identical in every shot):
  - kit.hud(ctx, lt, s) with s = {slow, prevSlow, since, unit, rate}: slow is the current slowdown (1 = real speed, 256 = 256 times slower), prevSlow the slowdown before the last change, since the seconds since that change (a large number if none), unit the counter's unit (2 to 4 letters, e.g. a per-minute or per-second unit), rate the device's REAL rate in that unit. It draws, all inside y 200 to 330:
    - the speed badge: a plate at x 60, y 200, 76 px tall, as wide as its measured text plus 48: "REAL SPEED" on a PRIMARY red plate when slow is 1, otherwise "<slow>x SLOWER" on a SECONDARY blue plate; only when prevSlow differs from slow, for the first 0.6 s after the change it reads "x<prevSlow / slow> FASTER" when slow < prevSlow (or "<slow / prevSlow>x SLOWER" when slow > prevSlow) on a GOLD plate with ink text, scaling from 1.15 to 1; when prevSlow equals slow there is never a flash. Text 42 px Gelasio bold, cream (ink on gold), centred on the plate.
    - the dial: a half disc centred at x 540, base at y 300, radius 84: cream face, 6 px GOLD rim, 9 tick marks (6 px wide, 18 px long, ink), the last 30 degrees of the arc filled PRIMARY red; a 6 px ink needle from the centre with a red tip, at angle Math.PI + f * Math.PI where f = 1 - Math.log2(slow) / 8 (256x points left, real speed points right), easing from the old angle to the new one over 0.5 s after a change (easeBack), plus a 1 to 2 degree tremble.
    - the counter: a plate x 700 to 1020, y 200 to 276: a GOLD part x 700 to 806 with the unit in 34 px ink Barlow bold, then a near-black window x 812 to 1014 with 4 digit cells (each 46 px wide, 6 px apart) holding the value rate / slow, rounded, written with 4 digits and zero padded (0045); when the value is under 10, one decimal in the cells (02.5); leading zeros drawn at 35% opacity; digits 46 px Cascadia Mono bold cream. After a change the digits roll to the new value over 0.4 s.
  - kit.placard(ctx, lt, p) with p = {step, head, sub, chips, lit, k}: kit.panel(ctx, 90, 340, 900, 170), a 24 px SECONDARY blue end cap inside each end (x 96 to 120 and x 960 to 984) with 3 short cream horizontal ticks; head centred at (540, 398) in Gelasio bold ink, 60 px, shrunk until it fits 780 px (never under 40 px); sub centred at (540, 464) in Barlow bold PRIMARY red, 40 px, shrunk until it fits 780 px (never under 36 px). If chips (an array of 2 to 5 short words) is given instead of sub, it draws them in a row centred at y 464: each chip a plate 48 px tall, as wide as its 36 px Barlow text plus 24, 18 px apart with a small ink triangle between chips; the first lit chips (count = lit) are PRIMARY red with cream text, the rest a darker cream (lightness 0.86) with ink text at 45%. Only if step is a number of 1 or more (null, 0 or missing draws NO badge), a step badge: a PRIMARY red disc of radius 34 at (112, 352) with a 6 px cream ring and the number in 40 px Gelasio bold cream. p.k = seconds since this placard's text appeared: the plate pops in (scale 0.9 to 1 with easeBack over 0.25 s) when k is under 0.25.
  - kit.callout(ctx, text, tx, ty, px, py, k): a cream plate centred at (tx, ty), 60 px tall, as wide as the measured 38 px Barlow bold text plus 40, 6 px ink border, ink text centred; a 6 px ink leader line from the plate's nearest side to (px, py), ending in an 18 px cream square with an ink border on the part. k = seconds since it appeared (the line draws itself over 0.25 s, then the plate pops).

## motion
- The mechanism always runs. Every moving part moves at the CURRENT speed: its cycle position is phase = (lt * cyclesPerSecond / slow) % 1, where cyclesPerSecond is the real rate per second; at real speed (and at 2x or 4x) the moving part becomes a smear: draw it 4 to 6 times along its path with falling opacity (30% down to 6%) instead of once.
- One continuous camera that never stops: every shot opens with a 0.5 s push-in (the subject scales from 0.86 to 1 around its centre, easeOut) as if the camera travelled from the previous part to this one, then keeps creeping in (scale 1 to 1.12 over the rest of the shot) while drifting sideways 40 to 60 px; the hook pulls in toward the working end in its last 0.6 s.
- Speed changes happen on a line's first word: call kit.hud with the new slow and since = lt minus that moment; whoosh sound. The badge flashes, the needle swings, the counter rolls; the parts visibly change speed on the same frame.
- The working action is marked, in step 1 ONLY (no pop words in any other shot): in the step shot about the working end, each time the action happens (once per cycle) a pop word (the sound of that action, 1 short word plus "!", 64 px Gelasio bold, PRIMARY red with a 6 px cream stroke) pops beside the action (scale 0.6 to 1.1 to 1 over 0.2 s) and fades after 0.5 s.
- Callouts appear on the word that names their part, one at a time, 0.4 s apart, and stay to the end of the shot.
- The loop: the last shot slides the shell closed over the x-ray, pulls back to the hook's exact composition and holds it for the last second.
- Easing: easeOut for things arriving, easeIn for leaving, easeInOut for the camera; easeBack for the needle and pops.

## plan
- First pick ONE real kind of the device (the most common one today) and keep every part, step and number true to that kind; never mix parts of two kinds (for example two different power systems or two different drives).
- Decide the HUD once: the counter's UNIT (2 to 4 letters) and the device's REAL RATE in that unit (one real figure for that kind). Every shot idea starts with its exact HUD line and PLACARD line ("PLACARD: step none, head ..., sub ..." or "PLACARD: step 2, head ..., sub ..."), for example "HUD: slow 64, prevSlow 128, change at 0 s, unit [UNIT], rate [RATE]" (prevSlow is the previous shot's last slowdown; the hook uses prevSlow 1 and no change), with the SAME unit and rate in every shot.
- Step badges (the numbered red disc) only on step and chain shots, numbered 1, 2, 3 ... in order; the hook, blur, ramp and loop placards have no badge (their PLACARD line says step none and their shots pass step: null).
- Structure: 8 or 9 shots in exactly this order, one beat each. The slowdown of each shot is fixed (the shot idea states it):
  1. hook (REAL SPEED, slow 1): the device visibly at work (a screen shows a bright picture, a tool touches what it works on, a motor's part spins), in the "everyday" background (the real place where it is used, with 2 to 4 of that place's typical big shapes). The device is big (700 to 900 px long), tilted 15 to 25 degrees as it is held or mounted in use, its working end touching the thing it works on (drawn simply by the shot as one big shape). Placard head "HOW DOES A [DEVICE] WORK?", sub: the real rate as a number with its unit, and what that means per second. The idea gives the device's exact placement as a DEVICE line ("DEVICE: x 540, y 1000, scale 0.9, angle -20, xray 0"); the loop idea repeats this DEVICE line word for word. Line: a hook with that surprising number, naming the device.
  2. blur (REAL SPEED, then 256x, "inside" background): push into the working end at real speed, a smear you cannot read; placard head says it is too fast for the eye (in your own words), sub gives the real time of one cycle with its unit. On the second line's first word the speed drops to 256x SLOWER and the motion becomes visible. Line 2 says time is now slowed two hundred fifty-six times.
  3. step 1 (128x): the working end alone, big: what really happens there, each time. Placard step 1, head a short question about it, sub the answer. Callouts name the 1 or 2 parts doing the work, plus one callout with a real number and unit (a gap, a travel, a count); pop words on each action.
  4. step 2 (64x): the part that moves the working end, alone and big. Step 2, head "WHAT [DRIVES] IT?" in the topic's own verb, sub the answer.
  5. step 3 (32x): the part behind that (how its motion is made or shaped). Step 3, head a question, sub the answer. Never the same picture as step 2: a different asset, or the same asset pushed in at least 2 times closer on a different part of it.
  6. step 4 (16x, only if the device really has a fourth link before the power; otherwise leave this shot out and use 8 shots): the power source or the part that turns power into motion. Step 4.
  7. chain (8x): the whole chain from the power source to the working end, pushed in on the device in x-ray, its parts named by callouts (up to 4 here). Placard head names the chain in your own words ("[SOURCE] TO [WORKING END]"), and chips instead of a sub: the parts' short names in order from the power source to the working end (2 to 5 chips, each at most 8 characters); the chips light one by one 0.4 s apart while a bright pulse travels along the parts in the same order.
  8. ramp (8x, 4x, 2x, then REAL SPEED): the whole device in x-ray, speeding up: 2 lines; the speed doubles on the first word of line 1, at its middle, on the first word of line 2 and at its middle (8x, 4x, 2x, 1). The placard head shows how many working actions happen per second at the current speed (real per-second count divided by slow), as digits with the unit, and the sub says what changes. The rings in the background tighten as the speed rises.
  9. loop (REAL SPEED): the shell closes over the x-ray and the camera pulls back to the hook's exact composition (everyday background, device in use, the hook's placard and HUD). The line lands the takeaway with the real per-second number.
- Every step shot names ONE real part by its real name and gives ONE real number with its unit on screen (a size, a distance, a count, an angle, a voltage, a time), in a callout or in the sub. The counter's rate and unit are the device's real working rate (a real per-minute or per-second figure from the REFERENCE). Every number must be real; if a value is not known, choose another quantity that is, never invent one.
- Write in each shot idea: its HUD line (above), the placard's exact step, head and sub (or chips), each callout's exact text and which part it points at (one of them a number with its unit in every step shot), and what moves.
- Text lengths: head at most 18 characters, sub at most 30 characters, callouts 1 to 3 words (at most 16 characters), all UPPERCASE; numbers on screen are digits with units, written the way people read them (5 MM, 1/60 S, 12,000).
- Lines: one narrator, bf_emma. Short, plain sentences, 9 to 13 words each; every line adds a new fact; numbers in lines are written as words. Say the device's name in line 1. Never repeat a fact the placard already showed in an earlier shot.
- Assets, exactly 3:
  1. "device" (hero): the whole device with its REAL outside silhouette and colour, the shape people know at a glance (its real proportions, grip, buttons, head and outline; never a plain box or slab), standing upright (its long axis vertical), every inner part in its REAL order along its length (working end at the top, power source at the bottom), param "xray" (0 = closed shell in its real colour; 1 = the shell drawn as a pale see-through outline with 15% fill, every inner part visible), param "phase" (0 to 1, the cycle position: the moving parts move with it), param "lit" (-1 or the index of the part, counted from the top, that gets a bright cream rim and a 20% lighter tone). Box about 440 x 1000.
  2. "working_end": a close-up of the part where the work happens, wide and big, param "phase" (0 to 1: its moving part at that point of the cycle). Box about 900 x 620. Seen so close that its key structure is big: what really does the work is shown as it is built (repeated teeth, pins or cells: 6 to 10 across the width, each 60 to 120 px wide; a stack of layers: a cross-section with each layer 50 px or more thick), the moving or changing part travelling or changing 40 px or more per cycle; never a thin strip or rail.
  3. "drive": a close-up of the part that makes the working end move (the link, gear, coil, valve or circuit that turns power into the working motion), param "phase", and "xray" (0 to 1: its casing becomes see-through so the inside shows). Box about 720 x 900.
  Everything else (the power source in close-up, pop words, the pulse, callouts, placards, HUD) is drawn by the shots or the kit; for a close-up of the power source, draw "device" at scale 2 to 2.6 with xray 1 and the source centred in the frame.
- Asset descriptions give the silhouette in simple shapes with the real proportions: which cylinders, boxes, rods, plates and teeth, their sizes relative to each other, where they join, and which part moves and how (slides, turns, swings, flashes).
- Music: driving. Sound effects: whoosh on every speed change, pop on every placard change, callout and pop word.

## assets
- Toon-shaded and chunky: 3 flat bands per surface (shadow, base, light top-left), a 6 px dark ink rim around every part, a 12 px checker-dither seam between bands (6 x 6 squares). Every line and gap at least 6 px. No gradients, no text, no labels inside assets.
- Big readable parts: few, large, simple shapes (cylinders, boxes, rods, teeth, plates, coils as stacked bands), real proportions; at most 8 distinct parts in "device".
- Moving parts really move with p.phase (slide by their real travel, turn by their real angle) so a shot can show the motion just by passing phase.
- "device" with xray 1: the shell is a pale outline (cream at 60%, 6 px) with a 15% fill of its real colour, and every inner part is drawn whole and fully opaque inside it, in order, with 12 to 24 px gaps so each reads apart.
- Fill the box: the asset spans 85 to 95% of its box's long side. No floor shadow inside the asset.
- Keep the 4 corners of the box empty (the engine rejects an asset that paints all 4 corners): no backing plate, frame, panel or background behind the parts; a flat rectangular object (a board, a grid, a screen, a slab) is drawn inset 6% from the box edges with its outer corners rounded by 48 px or more.

## shots
- Draw order: kit.bg, the subject (one asset, big), pop words, callouts, kit.placard, kit.hud last. Never paint a solid fill over the background.
- Fixed layout in every shot:
  - HUD: kit.hud at the top (y 200 to 330), in EVERY shot for the whole shot.
  - Placard: kit.placard (y 340 to 510), in every shot for the whole shot; its content switches on the line it belongs to (k restarts).
  - Subject: one asset, big, centred x 540, between y 560 and 1440: "working_end" at scale 1 to 1.15 (900 to 1040 px wide, never stretched wider or flatter), centred y 950 to 1050; "drive" 640 to 800 px tall centred y 980 to 1040; "device" 820 to 880 px tall centred y 1000 (whole device), or scaled 2 to 2.6 for a close-up of one of its parts (that part centred at y 980). A close-up may be cut by the left and right frame edges, never by the placard.
  - Callouts: at most 3 (4 in the chain and ramp shots, where they name the device's inner parts in a column at x 60 to 320, top to bottom), each plate fully inside x 60 to 1020 and y 560 to 1420, beside the part it names (left side if the part is right of centre, and the other way round), never over another callout or the subject's moving part.
  - Nothing important below y 1440 (the caption lives there).
- Every frame moves: the mechanism runs at the current speed, the background shapes drift, the rings expand, the needle trembles.
- In the hook and loop the device is shown in use in the everyday place with the SAME h.asset call (the DEVICE line's x, y, scale, angle and params) and the same placard and HUD, so the loop's final 1.2 s match the hook's first frame exactly.
- Pass the HUD line of the idea to kit.hud exactly: {slow, prevSlow, since: lt minus the change moment (a large number such as 99 when the shot has no change), unit, rate}; the same unit and rate in every shot. A shot whose speed changes mid-shot passes the new slow and prevSlow from that moment on.
- "device" in the chain and ramp shots: 820 to 880 px tall, centred x 540, y 1000, alone on the background (no frame, box or plate around it).

## director
- Flag: a shot without the HUD row or without the placard; a counter unit or rate that differs between shots; a "FASTER" or "SLOWER" flash when the speed did not change; step badges on the hook, blur, ramp or loop, or numbered out of order; pop words outside step 1; glows or light blobs wider than their part; a device that does not have the real outside silhouette; parts of two different kinds of the device mixed together; a working end drawn as a thin strip; two step shots that show the same picture; a loop that does not match the hook's frame; an inside background lighter than lightness 0.36; a speed badge, dial or counter that does not match the shot's slowdown, or a counter value that is not rate / slow; parts that do not move or move at the same speed whatever the slowdown; more than one subject in a frame, or clutter beyond HUD, placard, one subject and 3 callouts; a step shot with no real number and unit on screen; callouts that point at nothing, overlap, or name things that are not real parts; placard text that spills out or is cut; smooth gradients, hairlines or soft blur (it must read as pixel art); parts without the dark rim or without 3 tone bands; a chain whose chips are not in the real order; an ending that does not return to the hook's frame; anything important below y 1440.
- Check every part name, order, motion and number against the reference and standard knowledge; remove a number rather than keep a doubtful one.
