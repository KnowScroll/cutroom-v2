# toon3d-story-2d

## about
A funny true story about an ordinary person, in a toon-shaded 3D cartoon look: ball-headed characters (a huge shaded sphere head, a small tube body, stick arms and legs ending in balls) in warm lamp-lit rooms, cut fast like a sitcom between wide shots of the room, reaction close-ups of the huge face, over-the-shoulder views and full-frame inserts of the paper or screen that holds the key number. It opens on the absurd outcome, jumps back to how it started, and ends on a deadpan punchline. A quick, dry narrator, one word of caption at a time. For true anecdotes with a twist: a mistake that paid off, a loophole, a lucky or unlucky deal, an odd purchase or bet, a stunt that worked (a person, the odd thing they did, what happened, the catch, how it ended). Not for explainers of mechanisms, lists, data comparisons or sad stories.

## look
- Toon-shaded 3D, faked in 2D canvas: every object is a simple 3D form with smooth shading from one warm light at the top-left and a dark ink outline. Three recipes, written once as small inner functions and used for everything:
  - Sphere (heads, hands, shoes, bulbs, knobs, sweat drops): function ball(x, y, r, col) { const g = ctx.createRadialGradient(x - 0.35 * r, y - 0.4 * r, 0.05 * r, x, y, r); g.addColorStop(0, h.mix(col, '#ffffff', 0.55)); g.addColorStop(0.5, col); g.addColorStop(1, h.mix(col, INK, 0.4)); ctx.fillStyle = g; ctx.beginPath(); ctx.arc(x, y, r, 0, Math.PI * 2); ctx.fill(); ctx.lineWidth = Math.max(3, r * 0.04); ctx.strokeStyle = INK; ctx.stroke(); } with INK = the palette's ink (kit.palette.ink in assets and shots, palette.ink inside makeKit).
  - Box in one-point perspective (desks, counters, cabinets, machines, doors, books, signs, boxes): the front face is a rectangle; each back corner is its front corner moved a fraction k (0.08 to 0.25, deeper things larger) toward the room's vanishing point V: back = [x + k * (V.x - x), y + k * (V.y - y)]. Draw the top face when the box is below V.y (the bottom face when above), the right side face when the box is left of V.x (the left side face when right of it), then the front face. Front = base colour, top = base mixed 25% toward the lamp light, side = base mixed 30% toward the ink; every face outlined. Things in a room share one V, so they line up.
  - Cylinder (bodies, legs, cords, mugs, lamp cords): a horizontal linear gradient across it: base mixed 20% toward white at the left edge, base at 55%, base mixed 35% toward the ink at the right edge.
- Ink outlines on everything: 6 to 8 px on characters, 4 to 5 px on furniture and props, 3 px on small far details, always palette.ink. Only light pools, shadows, the vignette and the dust are drawn without an outline.
- Shadows: a soft contact shadow under everything that stands on a floor or a desk (an ink ellipse at 25 to 35%, as wide as the object); a darker band 30 to 50 px tall where a wall meets the floor.
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() or ctx.stroke() after them. h.asset(id, x, y, scale, params) takes no ctx either. h.mix and h.rgba take hex colour strings.
- Colour is a set of OKLCH relations by role; keep them whatever the story (palette names in brackets, 10 in all):
  - Ink (ink): warm near-black, lightness 0.13 to 0.16, chroma about 0.03, hue about 25. Ground (bg): dark warm brown, lightness about 0.26, chroma 0.03 to 0.05, for deep corners, floor shadows and the vignette (about 15% of the frame).
  - Cream (cream): lightness 0.95 to 0.97, chroma 0.04 to 0.06, hue about 100: rails, mouldings, papers, lamp light. Skin (skin): lightness 0.93 to 0.95, chroma about 0.03, the same hue: every head and hand.
  - Two saturated families about 140 degrees apart carry the rooms and the clothes: warm red (warm) lightness 0.55 to 0.60, chroma 0.21 to 0.24, hue about 30; cool teal-green (cool) lightness 0.62 to 0.72, chroma 0.10 to 0.12, hue 165 to 180.
  - Wood (wood): lightness 0.45 to 0.52, chroma about 0.10, hue 55 to 65: floors, doors, furniture. Amber (amber): lightness 0.62 to 0.70, chroma about 0.13, hue 70 to 80: lamp-lit walls of a second room, brass, shelving.
  - Highlighter yellow (accent): lightness 0.90 to 0.93, chroma about 0.17, hue about 100: the bar behind a key number or a word on a paper, bulb glow. Screen green (accent2): lightness 0.50 to 0.58, chroma 0.13 to 0.15, hue about 148: screens, signs, one lamp shade.
  - Vivid colours stay at lightness 0.55 to 0.72; the lightest cream is about 14 times brighter than the ground.
  - Strength: the warm red is the most saturated colour on screen (chroma 0.21 to 0.24 is near the limit a screen can show for red at that lightness: a pure tomato red, not brick or salmon); the cool family is a clear teal-green, not grey-green. Skin and cream are a neutral cream (hue about 100), never pink, peach or beige. Mid browns and tans (wood, amber) never cover more than a third of the frame: every room is dominated by its saturated wall pair.
- Rooms (kit.bg), one per real place of the story (3 to 5), each a one-point-perspective interior seen from about a standing person's chest height, filling the whole frame:
  - V at (540, 780 to 860). Ceiling from y 0 to about 260: a vertical gradient from the ground colour at the top edge to cream mixed 35% toward wood at its bottom, brightened only by the lamp's pool. The back wall from there to the floor line at y 1050 to 1150, across the full width; the floor below it to the bottom edge, its wood darkening toward the bottom edge to lightness about 0.30.
  - Each room has its own wall pair: an upper wall in one family with vertical wallpaper stripes (36 px wide every 72 px, 6 to 8% lighter), a cream picture rail (26 px) under the ceiling and a cream dado rail (22 px) at about two thirds of the wall's height, both outlined; under the dado a wainscot in the other family with raised panel rectangles outlined in a darker tone. Pairs: cool over warm (the person's own room); cream over a deep band of cool mixed 50% toward the ink; warm over cool. At most one room may be amber all over (with an accent2 lamp). Never the same pair for two rooms.
  - Floor: wood planks converging to V (lines 70 px apart at the floor line, 300 px apart at the bottom edge), 2 or 3 plank tones, a few dark knots; in some rooms a rug in perspective (a trapezoid) in the other family with a 12 px accent border stripe.
  - 6 to 10 props of the place ON THE BACK WALL or standing against it, all boxes, spheres and cylinders with outlines (a door with a knob, a wall clock with moving hands, a shelf of books in 4 or 5 colours, framed posters, cabinets and shelving units whose bottom edge sits on the floor line, a grid of numbered-looking lockers, a plant in a corner): enough that the room reads at once. Everything stands on the floor line or hangs flat on the wall (a shelf on two brackets, a frame on the wall's face); nothing floats in the air.
  - kit.bg is the room's SHELL only: ceiling, walls, floor, rug, lamp, light and things against the back wall. It never draws anything standing out in the room (no counter, desk, table, chair, stool, crate or machine on the open floor, nothing below y 1250): kit.bg is always drawn first, so a character placed later would stand on top of it. Furniture people stand behind, sit at or lean on is drawn by the shots, in depth order (see shots).
  - One hanging cone lamp at x 380 to 700: an ink cord from the top edge, a cone shade (a trapezoid 300 to 360 px wide at its rim, in warm, cool or accent2, with the cylinder gradient), a glowing cream ellipse under its rim; it swings 1 to 2 degrees with lt. Its light: a radial gradient pool of cream at 30 to 40% at the bulb fading to 0 over 650 to 750 px, breathing plus or minus 3%.
  - A vignette over all of it: a radial gradient from transparent at 320 px from the centre to the ground colour at 65 to 75% in the corners. About 15% of every frame ends up as dark as the ground (lightness under 0.30): the top corners, the bottom edge, shadows under furniture and inside doorways.
  - Always moving: the lamp's swing and breathing pool, the clock's second hand, 12 to 20 small cream dust motes (3 to 5 px, 20 to 30%) drifting in the lamp light.
  - No readable words in kit.bg (signs, posters, screens and labels are colour blocks and stripes): the shots zoom into the room, and a word cut by the frame edge fails the engine's text check. Draw the room as shapes every frame, no h.cache: shots zoom into it up to 2.6 times and a cached picture turns soft.
- kit.panel(ctx, x, y, w, hgt, opts) draws the face that holds words: by default a paper (cream, corners rounded 8 px, a 5 px ink outline, and a 10 px strip of cream mixed 25% toward the ink along its bottom and right edge as its thickness); with opts.accent a screen (the accent colour mixed 45% toward the ink as the glass, a lighter band across its top quarter, inside a 24 px dark bezel of the ground colour with an ink outline). kit.title(ctx, text, x, y, opts) draws text only, no box and no outline: bold, opts.color (default palette.ink; on a screen pass the cream or the accent).
- Type (kit.font): "Segoe UI", weights 800 to 900. kit.post: nothing but a faint warm grain is allowed (or nothing at all); the vignette belongs to kit.bg.

## motion
- Sitcom timing: holds, then snaps. An expression changes in one frame or over 0.1 s on the word it belongs to, never as a slow fade.
- The reaction take (on every surprise): the head jolts up 25 px and back in 0.25 s (easeBack), the eyes pop into white rings with tiny pupils, the brows jump up, the mouth becomes an open o, and 3 or 4 light-blue sweat drops appear around the head and bob.
- Characters are never frozen, but calm: breathing (body scale 1 to 1.5%, period about 3 s), a blink every 3 s, a small head tilt now and then, hands that gesture on the words. Their feet stay planted where they stand (a bob or sway never lifts them off the floor or moves them through furniture).
- Each setup (see shots) holds with a slow push-in: the camera zoom grows 4 to 6% over the setup, easeInOut. Cuts between setups are hard cuts on a word. Correct staging matters more than movement.
- A number that CHANGES in the story (a balance rising, a price climbing, a count growing) counts up on its screen or paper from the old value to the new one over 0.6 to 1.0 s (easeOut, h.fmt for the separators), then holds still at least 1 s. A number that is simply stated (an offer, a price paid, a date) pops in whole (scale 0.8 to 1 with easeBack over 0.2 s), never counting. The highlight bar wipes in left to right over 0.3 s under the number or the fine print as the narrator says it.
- A time jump flips the date card: its top page squashes up (scale y 1 to 0 over 0.25 s, easeIn) and the new date unfolds under it.
- The world drifts softly: the lamp swings, its pool breathes, dust floats, the clock ticks.

## plan
- Structure: 6 to 9 shots, one beat each, in this order. The reel opens on how it ended, then goes back to the start. hook, setup, thing, surprise, reveal and ending are ALWAYS there. question, act and twist are used only when the REFERENCE has the real event for them; a beat the reference has no fact for is left out, never filled with an invented event, date, number, authority or reaction:
  1. hook: the person in the place of the outcome, a WIDE then a CLOSE on a sly face, then an INSERT of the result's number. Line: "[Who, in a few words] [did the small odd thing] [as a joke or on a whim], and [the absurd real result, with its number]." One sentence, 14 to 20 words.
  2. setup: back to the start, the person's own room; the date card shows the real year (and month and day when known). Lines: one ordinary detail of their life with a small real number, then "It's [year]." as its own short line.
  3. thing: the odd thing arrives or is found or is made (a letter, an ad, a post, an offer, a ticket, an idea); an INSERT of it with its real name and its key number on the highlight bar. Line: what it was and what it said, with the number.
  4. act: what the person did with it, casually (signed, sent, bought, posted, swapped, filed), shown up close: a CLOSE of the face and hands, then an OTS at the machine, counter or screen it went into.
  5. surprise: "[Time] later, [they check, or it arrives]." then a reaction line of one to three words as its own line, on the reaction take; an INSERT of the new number counting up; the date card flips.
  6. question: the narrator asks the viewer "So what [did they do] with [the thing or the number]?" and answers in one or two words with the unexpected real choice, then what they really did. The second character may appear here (someone they asked).
  7. twist (only when the reference tells a real later turn): "[Time] later, [the other side acts]" (a demand, a freeze, a bill, a refusal, a recall, the price jumping), with the real date from the reference; the date card flips; the second character in the role of that other side, angry or stunned, in a TWO-SHOT or an OTS; an INSERT of the warning or the new number.
  8. reveal: "You see, [one true fact from the reference that changes how the whole story reads: a rule or fine print that decided it, what the thing was really worth then or later, the record it set]." A CLOSE of a smug face (half-lidded eyes, one finger up), then an INSERT of that detail with the highlight bar. The shortest line after the reaction line. Never state what someone knew, planned, thought or felt unless the reference says so in those words.
  9. ending: how it ended, with the final real number, in a TWO-SHOT held to the end (both characters, a paper or screen with the number on the table between them); the last line is a deadpan punchline that is a true fact and flips the meaning of the story, never a moral or a call to follow.
- Facts: a TRUE story from the REFERENCE. Every name, date, amount, count and event comes from it; never an invented number, line of dialogue or feeling. Real people appear only as the generic ball-head characters.
- Lines: one narrator, am_echo (a casual American male storyteller; it is chris on ElevenLabs), quick and dry, like telling a friend a story at a table: plain short words, contractions, 4 to 16 words a sentence, several sentences per shot; at least one line of one to three words (the reaction). Never stack labels before a name ("[city] [job] [name]"), never encyclopedia words ("notable", "commemorated", "enthusiasts", "recognized as"). Say each number once in the whole reel (the hook's result may come back only in the punchline). Numbers in lines are written as words. Say the brief's subject by its name in line 1 or 2, then call the person "he" or "she". Tones: wry for the hook, matter-of-fact for setup, thing and act, surprised for the reaction line, curious for the question, deadpan for the twist and the ending, confident for the reveal.
- On-screen words live only on things in the world: INSERT papers and screens and the date card. At most 3 text rows per insert: a header (what the thing is, at most 22 characters), the key number with its unit or currency in digits (at most 12 characters, for example a price, an amount, a count with its unit) and one detail row (at most 26 characters: the fine print, a date, what it is for). No brand or company names on screen (say "two pizzas", not the chain). Every number and date on screen is stated in the REFERENCE (a "worth today" value only if the reference gives it, with its date), and its unit is right (a dollar value in dollars, a coin count in coins). Write every insert's rows and every date card's text in the shot's idea. Numbers on screen are digits. No other words on screen: no banners, titles or labels in the room.
- Date card text: the strip holds the month and year (at most 9 characters), the big text the day number, or the year alone when there is no day; never the same number in both.
- Exactly TWO character assets, whatever the cast size: the main person and one supporting person who plays everyone else by "role". Never a separate asset for each person.
- Assets: FIRST the main person (kind "character") in the ball-head build (see assets), with params "pose" (the pose list is made after this plan from what your shots need, so describe the body on each line in every idea: standing at the counter, sitting at the desk holding the paper up, one finger raised, arms crossed, scratching the head), "expression" (neutral, happy, surprised, smug, worried, angry, sad), "facing" (front, left, right), "sweat" (0 to 1) and "eyes" (0 to 1, for blinks); give them one outfit colour and a cap or hair in the other family. SECOND one supporting person (kind "character") who plays every other person (a clerk, an official, a buyer, a boss, a friend), the same build and params plus "role" that changes their hair, cap, glasses, moustache and outfit. A THIRD only for the one machine or object the story turns on that stands in 3 or more shots (a computer, a car, a vending machine, a phone, a safe), built from boxes, its screen or label a plain coloured face with no words. Rooms are backgrounds; papers, screens, the date card, flyers, cups, drops and small props are drawn by the shots.
- Backgrounds: 3 to 5 real places of the story (the person's home room, the place where it happened, the other side's office or counter, a back room), each with its wall pair, floor, lamp colour and 6 to 10 props, as in look.
- Each shot idea gives: the background; its 2 or 3 setups in order (WIDE, MEDIUM, CLOSE, OTS, TWO-SHOT, INSERT) and on which line or word each starts; each character's pose and expression per setup; the insert rows (header, number, detail) and the date card text if any; the reaction take if any.
- Music: feel Humorous Bouncy, 95-115 bpm (the reference has no music bed, so the band comes from the kind: a light comedy pulse under a quick voice); payoff_shot is the reveal shot. Sound effects: a few, 1 or 2 per shot: a pop when an insert's number appears, a whoosh when a shot opens in a new place, a knock on the twist's first line. Nothing on every word.

## assets
- The ball-head build (these proportions ARE the style: the head is half the height on purpose; the sprite-sheet review must keep it, never shrink it to a quarter): in a box of height H with the feet on the bottom edge (y = H / 2) and the centre at 0:
  - head: a sphere of radius 0.25 * H centred at (0, -0.24 * H), skin colour, the ball recipe, 7 px outline;
  - body: a short tube (rounded rectangle, corner radius 0.05 * H) 0.26 * H wide from y 0 to 0.25 * H, its top tucked behind the head, the outfit colour with the cylinder recipe;
  - legs: two short ink sticks (line width 0.022 * H, round caps) from the body's bottom at x plus or minus 0.05 * H down to y 0.46 * H, no longer than the body is tall; shoes: ink ellipses 0.12 * H by 0.06 * H at the bottom, toes turned outward;
  - arms: two short ink sticks (line width 0.018 * H) from the body's top corners; a hanging hand never reaches below the body's bottom; hands: skin balls of radius 0.045 * H (no fingers; one raised finger is a small skin rounded rectangle on the ball).
  - No ears, no neck, no nose: nothing else sticks out of the sphere except the cap's brim, hair puffs and glasses.
- The face sits on the lower front of the head (r = the head's radius, (0, 0) = its centre): eyes at (plus or minus 0.33 * r, 0.05 * r), brows above them at y -0.2 * r as thick ink bars (line width 0.06 * r, round caps, 0.32 * r long), the mouth at (0, 0.45 * r), stubble as 20 to 30 small dots (radius 0.018 * r) in the wood colour at 50% around the mouth and jaw. No nose. p.facing left or right shifts eyes, brows and mouth 0.12 * r toward that side.
  - neutral: eyes are ink ovals 0.07 * r wide and 0.1 * r tall, brows level, the mouth a short flat line;
  - happy: eyes are upward arcs, brows raised, a wide smile arc;
  - surprised: eyes are white rings (radius 0.17 * r, ink outline) with tiny ink pupils (0.05 * r), brows high and arched, the mouth an open ink o;
  - smug: half-lidded eyes (a flat lid line across the top half of each oval, the lower half showing), one brow raised, a one-sided smirk;
  - worried: brows tilted up in the middle, a wavy mouth; angry: brows slanting down to the middle, a downturned arc; sad: brows tilted up in the middle, a downturned arc, eyes looking down.
  - p.eyes closes the eyes to a flat line when below 0.2; p.sweat draws 1 to 4 light-blue drops (the ball recipe with a pointed top) beside the head, bobbing with p.t.
- Hair and headwear on the sphere: a cap is the top 35 to 40% of the head (clip to the head's circle, fill above a gentle curve) in its own colour with the ball shading, an ink line along its edge, and a brim as a flat rounded bar (0.75 * r long, 0.17 * r tall) across the forehead sticking out to one side; a bald head gets a small white shine at the top-left and hair as two side puffs (small grey balls over the ears); glasses are two ink rings with a bridge; a moustache is a thick curved ink bar. The supporting person's p.role changes these and the outfit (a suit is a dark tube with a small warm tie triangle; a uniform a coloured tube with a cream collar).
- p.pose is one of the poses in the asset's pose list; draw each exactly as its look says. Write head, face, body, arms and legs once as inner functions and build every pose from them, so the person is the same in every pose. Sitting: legs bent with the knees forward at the box's lower third (the shot puts a chair or the desk in front). Holding a paper: a blank cream rectangle with an ink outline held by both hands in front of the body (the shots write on papers only in inserts).
- Built-in life from p.t: breathing (body scale 1 to 1.5%), a blink every 3 s, a slight head bob.
- Fill the box (the head and body 85 to 95% of its height); no floor shadow inside the asset (the shots draw it); no text; never fill the box with a background.
- A third asset (a machine or object) uses the box recipe for its faces with V at its own top-centre, outlines on every edge, round parts with the ball recipe, a plain coloured screen or label face.

## shots
- Camera: every shot cuts between 2 or 3 setups, each held 1.5 to 3.5 s (the ending's last setup up to 6 s), switching on a line start or a word inside a line. Write the setups as a list of start times from sh.lines (const cuts = [0, sh.lines[0].start + 2.1, sh.lines[1].start]) and pick the current one from lt. Wrap the world in one camera transform per frame: ctx.save(); ctx.translate(540, 960); ctx.scale(z, z); ctx.translate(-cx, -cy); then kit.bg, the props and the characters; ctx.restore(). Keep z at 1 or more and clamp cx to 540 / z ... 1080 - 540 / z and cy to 960 / z ... 1920 - 960 / z, so the room always fills the frame. The slow push-in multiplies z by 1 + 0.06 * h.easeInOut(time in this setup / its length).
- The characters are always placed with h.asset (never redrawn by the shot; the only exception is the back of the head in OTS), and every shot has at least one setup where a character asset is drawn 400 px or more tall (the engine rejects a shot without one).
- Depth and staging (2D canvas has no depth: draw order IS depth, and every mistake shows). For every setup:
  - Give everything that stands in the room a floor y: where it touches the floor (a character: its feet, = centre y + 0.5 * Hb * s; a counter, desk or table: the bottom edge of its front face; a chair: the bottom of its front legs). Higher on screen (smaller floor y) is further away. Draw in this order: kit.bg; then all standing things sorted by floor y, smallest first, each with its contact shadow just before it; then things held or resting on a surface; then the OTS head; then, after the camera's restore, the insert and the date card. Write it as a list of draw functions with their floor y and sort it, never by hand.
  - Behind a counter, desk or table (the most common staging): first choose the furniture's front face (left, right, top edge yT, bottom edge yB on the floor). The character stands behind it: centre y = yT - 0.12 * Hb * s, so its waist is level with the counter top and its feet (at yT + 0.38 * Hb * s) are inside the front face; make the face at least 0.45 * Hb * s tall (yB - yT) so the legs and feet are fully hidden. The character is drawn BEFORE the furniture, which then covers it from the waist down. Its hands may rest on the top face (drawn after the furniture as two hand balls at y about yT, only if the pose has the hands forward). Wrong: feet or legs showing on or in front of the counter, a character standing on the top face.
  - Sitting at a desk facing the camera: the same, with the chair drawn first (behind), then the character in a sitting pose, then the desk's front face covering it below the chest.
  - In front of furniture: the character's feet are below the furniture's bottom edge (larger floor y) and it is drawn after it; it may overlap the furniture, never the other way round.
  - Two characters side by side never overlap: their centres are at least the head diameter plus 40 px apart; both at the same scale when they are at the same depth.
  - Props: one resting on a surface has its bottom edge on that surface's top face (y between the top face's back and front edges) and is drawn after the surface; one on the floor stands on the floor below the floor line with a contact shadow; one held is drawn at the character's hand (the pose's hand position: the hands of a standing pose are at about x plus or minus 0.2 * Hb * s and y = centre y + 0.12 * Hb * s) or is part of the pose. Nothing floats in the air, nothing sits half inside another object, nothing crosses a face.
  - Check each setup's frame the way a viewer would: what stands where, what is in front of what, are the feet on the floor or hidden behind furniture.
- The setups (the hero is the first asset; Hb = its box height):
  - WIDE: z 1 to 1.15; the hero full-figure, 650 to 800 px tall, head top at y 420 to 600, feet at y 1200 to 1320, on a third (x 380 or 700) or centred; the upper third of the frame is the room and its lamp; furniture as boxes around them; any other character at the same scale.
  - MEDIUM: z 1.6 to 2.0 toward the hero's chest: head and body fill the frame's middle, the head 480 to 650 px across, the whole head inside the frame.
  - CLOSE: the room drawn at z 1.4 to 1.8 behind, and the hero drawn on top OUTSIDE the camera transform with h.asset at scale s = 850 / (0.5 * Hb), centred at x 540 and y = 250 + 0.49 * Hb * s, so the whole head (850 px across) sits between y 250 and 1100 with its face fully visible and the body running down out of the frame. This is where expressions and the reaction take play.
  - OTS (over the shoulder): the back of the hero's head drawn by the shot as one big ball in the skin colour (radius 300 to 380 px) centred at x 60 to 160, y 1150 to 1350, cut by the left edge, its top 40% in the hero's cap or hair colour with an ink edge line, no face; in the right two thirds, the supporting person with h.asset (500 to 800 px tall, head 300 to 420 px across) facing left toward the hero.
  - TWO-SHOT: both characters side by side at the same scale (heads 360 to 460 px across, centres at x about 330 and 750, y 800 to 950), behind a table or counter drawn with the box recipe whose front face hides their bodies below y 1150 to 1300.
  - INSERT (the money shots): a setup of its own (never a small paper beside a character, never over a face): the world zoomed 2.2 to 2.6 times toward the desk, counter or machine, a translucent ink layer at 25 to 35% over it (never a solid fill), then in screen space (after the camera's restore) the paper or screen at exactly this size: kit.panel(ctx, 90, 360, 900, 600) (a screen: opts.accent = kit.palette.accent2), its rows centred at x 540: the header at y 460 (48 px, 800), the key number at y 660 (110 to 150 px, 900) on a highlight bar (kit.palette.accent, h.rr around the measured text plus 30 px each side, 150 px tall, behind the number), the detail row at y 840 (42 px, 700). On a paper, two skin balls of radius 60 to 70 (the ball recipe) hold its lower corners at (130, 940) and (950, 940). Measure every text with ctx.measureText and shrink it (never under 40 px) until it fits the panel with 40 px to spare on each side.
- The date card, in screen space, in every shot whose line gives a new time: kit.panel(ctx, 90, 230, 240, 280) with a warm strip 70 px tall across its top (y 230 to 300); the strip text (month and year, 34 px, 800, cream) centred at (210, 265); the big text (the day, or the year, 110 px, 900, ink; shrink until it fits 200 px wide) centred at (210, 410). It flips when the time jumps (see motion). Never two date cards at once.
- Props the shots draw (desks, chairs, cups, flyers, phones, drills, boxes, coins, food) use the same recipes: boxes, balls and cylinders with outlines, the shared V of the room, a contact shadow. Never a flat single-colour blob or a shape without an outline.
- Speaking is the narrator's only; characters act on the narrator's words: the expression changes on the word it belongs to, a new pose or expression at least every 4 s whenever the hero is on screen.
- Readable words only on the INSERT panel and the date card; nothing written in the room, on clothes or on the third asset.

## director
- Flag first, in every frame: a character standing on a counter, desk or table, or its legs and feet drawn over furniture it stands behind; furniture or props floating in the air or standing in front of the room's floor line inside kit.bg; two characters overlapping; a prop crossing a face or hanging off nothing; a number or date on screen the reference does not give, or with the wrong unit; an invented event to fill a beat.
- Also flag: a head that is not about half the character's height, or a face missing in a pose; flat single-colour shapes with no shading or no outline; furniture drawn flat (no top or side face) or with sides that do not point to one vanishing point; a shot that never cuts (one setup for the whole shot) or a setup held over 3.5 s (except the ending); a room with empty edges or bare walls; readable words anywhere but an insert panel or the date card; an insert whose number is missing, invented, wrapped or spilling out of its panel, or more than 3 rows; a surprise without the reaction take; an expression that does not match its line; the hero smaller than a third of the frame height in a wide or its face cut by the frame edge in a close-up; a character drawn by the shot instead of its asset; a pale, beige or washed-out room (no saturated wall pair, no dark corners); a stated number counting up; the same number said in two shots; a line claiming what someone knew or felt; a solid fill hiding the room; an ending that preaches or asks to follow.
- Check every name, date, amount and event against the reference; remove a number rather than keep a doubtful one, and never let the punchline be an invented fact.
