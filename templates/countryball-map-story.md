# countryball-map-story

## about
A history story told by countries as flag-balls on a real map. Every country in the story is a ball painted with its own flag, with big cartoon eyes and thin noodle arms; it sits on its own country, which is filled with its flag's main colour, while every other country stays pale beige. The balls act out what passed between their countries (one asks and the other refuses, they argue, join, split, hand something over, sign) in short spoken lines; the narrator carries the facts; a wooden sign planted on the map carries the single word the story is about: that word changes as the story turns, and its last change is the ending. The camera cuts between real maps as the story moves (the stage, one country, a border, a far capital, an ally, the whole region); big hand-lettered titles hold the dates and numbers; a dark, quiet night scene can hold the decisive human moment before the bright map returns. For wars, independence, breakups and unions of countries, border changes, disputed land, empires rising and falling, treaties, referendums and diplomatic standoffs, between any countries in any era. Not for machines, biology, personal stories or anything without countries on a map.

## look
- Clarity first: at any moment the frame holds the map, at most 3 balls (except the small marching crowd of the event kind), at most 1 sign, at most 1 title (one or two lines) at the top and at most 3 map labels. Nothing else: no panels, cards, legends, compasses, insets, charts or icons floating free.
- The map is REAL: land, coasts and country shapes come from the engine's Natural Earth data through kit.map (below). Never draw a coastline, a country outline or a region by hand, and never fill a country with a hand-made polygon.
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() or ctx.stroke() after them. h.asset('id', x, y, scale, params) takes NO ctx either; it returns the drawn box {x, y, x0, y0, x1, y1} and, for a ball, its head and hand points in frame coordinates (head, handL, handR). Never pass a maxWidth to fillText (squeezed text is rejected): shrink the font until the text fits.
- Map helpers from the engine (use them, never write your own projection or country data):
  - h.countryView(names, x, y, w, hgt) returns a flat-map view {proj: 'merc', x, y, r, lon0, lat0} that fits the named countries' land (every piece at least 4% the size of the biggest, so far islands too) inside the box w x hgt centred on (x, y). names is one Natural Earth country name or an array of them ('Russia', 'United Kingdom', 'United States of America', 'Czechia', 'Bosnia and Herz.' ...); a historic union is named by its members, or by 'USSR', 'Yugoslavia' or 'Czechoslovakia'. Always today's Natural Earth names, also for a historic republic, province or kingdom: its land is the country it became ('Russia', 'Ukraine', 'Kazakhstan', never a period name such as '[Name] SSR' or '[Name] SFSR'). A wrong name throws an error that lists the right ones.
  - h.landPath(v), h.coastPath(v), h.borderPath(v), h.countryPath(names, v): Path2D objects already projected for the view v: ctx.fill(path, 'evenodd') or ctx.stroke(path).
  - h.geo(lon, lat, v) returns [x, y, z]: the frame position of a place (z is always 1 on a flat map). h.countryCentre(names) returns [lon, lat], the middle of the country's biggest piece of land: for a curved or long country it can lie outside the country (on a neighbour), so balls stand at kit.seat (below), never at the centre.
  - A view is {proj: 'merc', x, y, r, lon0, lat0}: the place (lon0, lat0) lands on screen at (x, y), and r is the map's scale (px per radian of longitude). A BIGGER r is CLOSER: r × 2 shows half as much land, twice as big; a smaller r shows more land (r × 0.3 turns one country into a continent). To go closer, make r bigger, never smaller. Because h.countryView fits far islands and territories too, the view of a country with distant islands is mostly sea: then look closer at the part the story is about with kit.near.
- Colour is a set of relations (OKLCH); keep them, whatever the topic:
  - Sea (palette.bg): steel blue, lightness 0.54, chroma 0.08, hue about 233. Along every coast a pale halo on the sea side: lightness 0.62, chroma 0.07, hue 225.
  - Land of every country NOT in the story (palette.land): pale warm beige, lightness 0.80, chroma 0.05, hue about 85.
  - Countries in the story are filled with their flag's main colour, at map strength: palette.red (lightness 0.52, chroma 0.155, hue 25), palette.green (0.58, 0.10, 152), palette.accent (saffron orange, 0.71, 0.145, 66, also the caption's number colour), palette.yellow (0.82, 0.14, 95), palette.blue (periwinkle, 0.66, 0.09, 270: lighter and more violet than the sea so it never merges with it), palette.cream (0.97, 0.025, 100, for a white flag). A country's fill is always a colour that is IN its own flag (a white-blue-red flag fills blue or red, never yellow or green); two neighbours in the story never share a fill colour: give the second one another colour from its own flag (cream for white).
  - Ink (outlines, coast lines, label text): warm near-black, lightness 0.17, chroma 0.03, hue 25. Text on titles and captions: palette.cream.
  - Wood (the sign): palette.wood, lightness 0.55, chroma 0.07, hue 60, with darker grain (lightness 0.42) and lighter edges (0.65).
  - Flag-balls use the REAL colours of their country's flag (the only colours outside the palette), drawn exactly as the flag looks.
  - The night background: near-black blue (lightness 0.14, chroma 0.02, hue 260) with a warm light pool (lightness 0.45, chroma 0.06, hue 75).
- The only text on screen: the title, at most 3 map labels (place names), medallion names and the sign's word. No timeline captions, era names, legends or other labels.
- No pixel grid; flat fills; outlines 5 px ink on balls, the sign, papers and medallions; the map has a 2.5 px ink coast line and no outlines elsewhere.
- Type: kit.font(size, weight) returns `${weight} ${size}px "Selawik"` (labels, captions). Titles use the hand-lettered face `bold ${size}px "Kalam"`, uppercase. Map labels use `600 ${size}px Gelasio`, uppercase, ctx.letterSpacing = '6px' (reset to '0px' after).
- kit.title(ctx, text, x, y, o): one line of hand-lettered title: uppercase, `bold ${size}px "Kalam"` (o.size default 104), palette.cream fill over a 12 px ink stroke (strokeText first, lineJoin round) and a soft ink drop shadow (shadowOffsetY 6, blur 10, ink at 45%), rotated by o.rot (default -0.03 rad) around (x, y); it shrinks the size until the measured text is at most 900 px wide; o.k pops it in (scale 0.7 to 1 with easeBack over 0.3 s, opacity 0 to 1 over 0.12 s); o.color replaces the fill (palette.accent for the number in a title). A title of two lines is two calls, the second 112 px lower.
- kit.panel(ctx, x, y, w, hgt, o): a small wooden plank (palette.wood, 5 px ink outline, corner radius 10, two dark nail dots at the ends, three thin grain lines), for the rare label that needs a backing.
- The kit also returns these helpers (the shots call them; write them exactly):
  - kit.view(names, box) = h.countryView(names, 540, box ? box.y : 860, box ? box.w : 1500, box ? box.h : 1000): the default box keeps the map's subject between y 360 and 1360, clear of the title and the captions; a wide country overflows the frame's sides a little so its main body fills the width (the reference crops its maps).
  - kit.near(V, lon, lat, k) = {...V, lon0: lon, lat0: lat, r: V.r * k}: the view V, k times closer (k above 1, usually 1.5 to 4), with the place [lon, lat] where V's centre was on screen.
  - kit.cam(lt, A, B, t0 = 0, dur = 1) returns a view moving from view A to view B between lt = t0 and t0 + dur with e = h.easeInOut(progress): x, y, lon0 and lat0 interpolated linearly with e, r = A.r * Math.pow(B.r / A.r, e), proj 'merc'. Before the move it is A; after it (and when B is A) it is B, held still: the camera moves only to show something new.
  - kit.pt(lon, lat, v) returns [x, y] = h.geo(lon, lat, v).slice(0, 2); kit.centre(names, v) returns kit.pt of h.countryCentre(names).
  - kit.seat(names, v, avoid = [], gap = 380) returns [x, y], where a country's ball (or the sign on a place) stands in the view v: a point deep inside that country's own land, near kit.centre(names, v), inside the ball area (x 200 to 880, y 640 to 1240) and at least gap px from every [x, y] in avoid (the seats taken before it in this frame). Search: c = kit.centre(names, v) clamped into the ball area; the points of a 24 px grid within 25 steps of c in each direction, sorted nearest to c first; on(x, y) = probe.isPointInPath(h.countryPath(names, v), x, y, 'evenodd'), with probe = h.cache('seat', 1, 1, () => {}).getContext('2d') made once in makeKit; free(x, y) = in the ball area and at least gap px from every avoid seat. Return the first point that passes, trying three tests in turn over all the points: (1) free, on it, and its four neighbours 40 px left, right, up and down on it too (deep inside); (2) free and on it; (3) free (no room left on a small country: the nearest free spot beside it); else c. Remember every answer in a Map keyed by the names, gap, the avoid seats and the view (x, y, r, lon0 and lat0, rounded), so a held view searches once.
  - kit.map(ctx, v, o): the whole map for the view v, in this order: (1) the coast halo: ctx.lineJoin = 'round'; stroke h.coastPath(v) with the halo colour 30 px wide at 30%, then 14 px at 45%; (2) ctx.fillStyle = palette.land; ctx.fill(h.landPath(v), 'evenodd'); (3) for each f of o.fills (an array of {names, colour, a}; a union is ONE entry naming all its members): const P = h.countryPath(f.names, v); ctx.globalAlpha = f.a ?? 1; fill P with kit.palette[f.colour] || f.colour; stroke P with that colour mixed 30% toward ink, 3 px; ctx.globalAlpha = 1; (4) stroke h.coastPath(v) with ink at 70%, 2.5 px; (5) draw the paper texture image (made once in makeKit with h.cache('paper', 1080, 1920, ...): 1400 seeded soft blotches 6 to 28 px wide, half ink at 2 to 4% opacity, half cream at 2 to 5%, plus 200 short fibre strokes at 3%: a fine, barely visible grain, never cloudy patches) with ctx.drawImage(image, 0, 0); (6) for each l of o.labels (at most 3) ({text, names, dx, dy} or {text, lon, lat, dx, dy}): kit.label at kit.seat(l.names, v) (on the country's own land; or kit.pt(l.lon, l.lat, v)) shifted by (dx, dy) (default 0, -240), light when it sits on a dark fill. Fills listed later draw over earlier ones (a member's new fill over its old union's fading one).
  - kit.label(ctx, text, x, y, o): a map label with NO panel: uppercase Gelasio 600, o.size default 44, letterSpacing 6px, centred; ink at 65% on light land (lightness over 0.6) or cream at 90% on a dark fill (o.light true), a 4 px stroke of the land colour under it for contrast; o.k pops it (opacity 0 to 1 over 0.2 s). It measures the text and moves it inward so the whole text stays inside x 70 to 1010 and y 480 to 1400 (a country centre near the frame edge must not push its name off the frame). One fillText call (never letter by letter). kit.label, kit.medallion and kit.title draw nothing when their text is missing or empty (never the word "undefined").
  - kit.medallion(ctx, x, y, rad, name, o): a portrait medallion for a person in the story, never a likeness: a cream ring (rad, 10 px) and a palette.green ring inside it (8 px), a sepia disc (lightness 0.55, chroma 0.04, hue 70) with a dark generic bust silhouette (a head circle at 0.42 rad and rounded shoulders; o.cap true adds a peaked cap), a soft ink shadow, the name under it in `bold 40px "Kalam"` ink with a 6 px cream stroke; o.k pops it (scale 0.6 to 1, easeBack, 0.3 s).
  - kit.paper(ctx, x, y, w, hgt, o): a document (cream sheet, 4 px ink outline, 4 grey text lines, a red wax seal disc at the bottom right when o.seal), rotated by o.rot; o.k pops it.
  - kit.flags = the style guide's "flags" object, unchanged: kit.flags['<cast id>'] is that ball's flag object.
  - kit.flag(ctx, x, y, s, colours, lt): a small flag on a thin ink pole (pole 90 * s tall, cloth 60 x 40 * s in the given colour bands, top band first), its free edge waving with lt (sine, 2 Hz); its foot (x, y) is where it stands.
  - kit.dots(ctx, x1, y1, x2, y2, k, colour): a dotted route as a gentle arc (control point 18% of the length above the middle): 9 px dots every 26 px, the first k share of them drawn; returns the [x, y] of the last drawn dot (a small paper or ball travels there).
  - kit.arrow(ctx, x1, y1, x2, y2, k, colour): a dashed movement arrow (6 px, dash [22, 14], the first k share of its length) with a solid arrowhead (34 px) at its tip once k reaches 1.
  - kit.wash(ctx, a): the flashback tint: a full-frame cream layer (palette.cream) at opacity a (0.35 to 0.45) drawn with globalCompositeOperation 'source-over', then a 12% sepia layer; it lightens and desaturates everything under it.
- Backgrounds (kit.bg), exactly these 3:
  - "sea": palette.bg with a soft lighter pool (lightness + 0.08 at its centre) centred at (540, 800) of radius 900, a vignette to lightness - 0.11 in the corners (the engine rejects a background whose brightness varies too little), the paper texture image, and 25 faint pale wave marks (short 40 px arcs, cream at 10 to 14%) drifting 6 px per second and breathing. Shots draw kit.map on it.
  - "overview": "sea" plus kit.map(ctx, {...V, r: V.r * (1 + 0.01 * lt)}, {}) with V = kit.view of the story's main countries (the plan's background description names them as "FIT: name, name"), no fills, and a small ink ring (radius 14, 4 px) at kit.seat of each of those countries in that view in turn (each avoiding the seats before it). It shows the kit's map and seats at work (its slow push-in is only here: shots draw on "sea" or "night").
  - "night": the night colour with a warm light pool (radius 700) at (540, 1050) as if from car headlights or a lamp, a ground plane (a slightly lighter band from y 1150 down, its top edge soft), a vignette, and 70 dust motes rising 25 to 45 px per second through the light (3 to 6 px, cream at 20 to 50%, brighter inside the pool); the pool breathes (radius +- 8% and brightness +- 6% on a 3 s cycle), so two frames 1.5 s apart clearly differ.
- kit.post: none (return post as a function that does nothing).

## motion
- The balls are always alive: a bob of 4 px and a 3% squash on a 1.2 s cycle, a blink every 2.5 to 4 s, pupils that glance at whoever is speaking. Acting, written into the code by time: every ball on screen changes its expression at the start of every line (the speaker shows the line's mood, the listener reacts), and its pose changes where its RELATION's action happens (on the cue word), for example const L1 = h.line(1).start; const ex = lt < L1 ? 'worried' : 'angry'; const pose = lt < h.cue('refused') ? 'receive' : 'turn_away'. Every ball's h.asset gets who: '<cast id>' (its mouth moves with its lines and its speech bubble sits above it).
- Things pop on the word that names them: titles and labels pop (easeBack 0.25 to 0.3 s), a sign drops 80 px and bounces once (easeBack 0.35 s), papers and medallions pop, flags spring up from 0 height.
- The sign's word changes with a flip: its swap param runs 0 to 1 over 0.5 s on the line that says the new word (the plank turns edge-on and back, the word switches at the middle), with a small dust puff at the post's foot.
- A country changing hands or colour cross-fades its fill over 0.4 s (the old fill underneath at full, the new one on top with a rising from 0 to 1) and its ball (if any) reacts at the same moment.
- Size is power (the odds kind only): the stronger ball grows to about 1.6 times its size over 0.8 s (easeOut) while the other shrinks to about 0.6, each moving away from the other as it changes, so they never cover each other; both keep bobbing.
- Movement on the map: dashed arrows draw themselves over 0.6 to 1 s; a crowd of 6 to 12 small helmeted balls (the ball asset at scale 0.16 to 0.24, helmet true, no who) marches along an arrow, staggered 0.15 s apart, each bobbing; messages travel along kit.dots routes over 0.8 to 1.2 s; a handed-over thing travels from hand to hand (see shots).
- The map camera holds one view per shot (kit.cam); a shot may make ONE move (0.8 to 1.2 s, easeInOut) to show something new, for example from the country to one region of it; between places the cut does the travelling. Balls stand in a held view: in a shot that moves its camera they arrive after the move or leave before it.
- The night moment is slow: moves of at most 0.5 s, a long hold, one gesture, dust drifting; no pops and no titles.
- Easing: easeInOut for the camera, easeOut for arrivals, easeIn for leaving, easeBack for pops.

## plan
- First decide the CAST and the WORD, all real:
  - CAST: the 2 to 4 countries (or unions) whose dealings the story is (at most 3 balls on screen at once), each with its real flag (as of the story's time: a union's flag while it exists, its successors' flags after). Only countries and unions with a real flag are balls and speakers; an alliance, treaty or organisation without its own country is never a ball. People (leaders, commanders) appear only as portrait medallions with their real names, never in "cast" and never speaking.
  - WORD: the single word or short name the story is a fight over (what a place is called, which country it belongs to, what a union calls itself, whose flag flies over the capital), and its REAL values in order, each one a name or status the place officially had, with the date it changed: the first value, any changes in between, and the last value (2 to 4 values). Never a descriptive word made up for the sign. The sign shows these, uppercase, at most 10 characters each (the short official form or abbreviation).
  - FLAGS: put every ball's flag ONCE into the style guide as "style": {..., "flags": {"<cast id>": {"dir": "h", "bands": ["#hex", "#hex", "#hex"], "emblem": "none", "emblemColour": "#hex"}}}, with the flag's real colours, stripe order and emblem (see the ball asset); a ball whose flag changes in the story gets a second entry "<cast id>_new". The kit hands them to every shot as kit.flags; never describe a flag in words.
  - FILLS: while a union or empire exists it is ONE fill (its flag colour) over all its members, in every shot that shows it, its balls standing each on its own member inside that one fill. A member gets its own fill only from the moment it leaves: in the shot where the union ends, the one fill cross-fades to the members' own fills on the word that says so; every later shot draws only the members' own fills. Never a member's fill on top of its union's fill while both exist.
  - The DATES and NUMBERS: every title number is a real date, count or duration with its unit, written in digits ("[D] [MONTH] [YEAR]", "[N] DAYS"); if you are not certain of a value, use another you are certain of. Never a ratio or score invented to show who is stronger.
- Every shot idea starts with these lines, then says what moves on which line:
  - "VIEW: FIT [country names]" (kit.view of those countries), "VIEW: FIT [country names] x[k] at [lon, lat]" (kit.near of that view: k times closer, centred on that real place; k above 1), or "VIEW: NIGHT" for the moment. FIT the 1 to 3 countries the shot is about, so each ball's own country is on screen and wide enough to hold its ball (about 300 px): for neighbours, or a country with far islands, a closer view of the part where they meet ("x[k] at" a point there). A shot with 2 or more lines may add "then FIT [names] on line [n]": a HARD CUT (no move) to a second view at the start of that line.
  - The map changes with the story, like the reference's (it cuts to a new place every 2 to 3 s): the stage view (the countries of the conflict together) for the hook, the payoff and the shots where they deal with each other across the map; a closer view (one country, a border, a capital's surroundings), another place (an ally, a far capital) or the wider region (a spread, a war) for the rest. At most half the shots use the stage view, and no view is used in more than 2 shots in a row.
  - "FILLS: [country or union [members]] = [colour role]; ..." every country in the story that is on screen with its fill (red, green, accent, yellow, blue, cream), and any change: "[country] red -> yellow on line 2"; a union is one fill until it ends: "union [Name, Name] = blue -> Name = red, Name = blue on 'word'".
  - "BALLS: [cast id] on [country] ([expression], [pose], [size])" for each ball on screen (at most 3; each stands on its own country at kit.seat); [pose] is one of the ball's poses (arms_down, point, give, receive, turn_away, arms_up). Size: 0.75 to 0.9 with two or three balls, up to 1.1 for one ball alone; the moment 1.1 to 1.2; the odds kind as under motion.
  - "RELATION: [kind]: [a] [does what to] [b] on '[cue word]'" in every shot with two or more balls: what passes between the countries in this shot, as it really happened; kind is one of ask/refuse, ask/agree, argue, threaten, join, split, hand over, sign, help (staged as under shots). Two balls standing side by side while the line tells what passed between them is never enough: the shot acts the relation out on its cue word, each relation with its own staging.
  - "SIGN: '[WORD]' on [country]" only in the shots where the WORD matters: the hook, a shot where it changes ("-> '[NEW WORD]' on line [n]"), a shot whose line says it, and the payoff. Every other shot has no sign.
  - "TITLE: '[TEXT]'" (or "TITLE: none"); "MEDALLION: [real name] on [country]"; "MARKER at [Town] [lon, lat]" for a named town or city (a small flag on a pole with its map label), with its real coordinates (longitude, latitude, east and north positive).
- Shot kinds (each shot's beat): the story picks which, how many and in what order, as its facts need; the hook is the first shot and the payoff the last; a kind may come twice (two pressure shots) or not at all:
  - hook: the stage map with the 2 main balls in their RELATION as the story opens and the sign showing the first value of the WORD; a title with the place's name and the year the story starts. Line: the conflict as one surprising claim with a real number in it (a year, a count, a size), or a question that ends with "?", naming the brief's subject; never a definition or a general statement.
  - setup: who the countries are and what ties them (a union, a border, a claim), acted as their RELATION: one or two short ball lines to each other; the narrator adds who they are.
  - flashback: under kit.wash: how the situation began (a real date as the title); one ball says one short line or the narrator tells it.
  - word: the sign's word changes (the flip on the line that says the new word); the other ball reacts.
  - cost: a human-scale detail on a closer map: a real count of people, a protest, a named town (MARKER) or one small figure; the line gives the number.
  - pressure: years pass or the pressure grows: a demand and its answer, dashed arrows, a fill creeping, a title with the years.
  - ally: another map (a far capital or an ally's country filled in its flag colour): one ball asks a third ball for the help it really asked for, and the third answers as it really did.
  - odds: only when the facts hold a real comparison of the SAME quantity for both sides, with units (two populations, two areas, two armies: "[N] MILLION VS [M] MILLION"): size as power (under motion). Line: says those numbers.
  - order: a leader's medallion on its home country; a rolled paper travels along a kit.dots route to where it acts; a title quoting the order or its date.
  - event: the decisive event on the map: a crowd of small helmeted balls marching along arrows, a vote, or countries changing their fills one after another (cross-fades 0.3 s apart), with at most 2 labels naming what happens.
  - outcome: how it ends: a union splits, a treaty is signed, land or power changes hands, a white flag (kit.flag in palette.cream) rises over a town; a title with the real date or duration.
  - moment: VIEW NIGHT: the two balls large in the light pool, facing each other; ONE gesture that decides it (a paper handed over, a flag lowered and another raised, a turned back); the narrator's line is short and quiet; no title.
  - aftermath: an iris opens from the night onto the bright map (or a cut to it): what happened after, with a real number of years or a count as the title; the balls changed (new flags, a helmet off, a new country filled).
  - payoff: the hook's composition (its view, the balls at the same seats, the sign in the same place); the sign's word changes for the last time to the final value; a title states the result in at most 4 words; the line lands on the WORD itself, echoing the hook.
- Length: the story sets the shots: as many as the word budget carries at 10 words a shot or more (a 45 s reel has about 6 to 8 shots); a shot holds one to three lines, as many as its point needs (a ball's short line and the narrator's).
- Lines: one narrator plus the story's balls; every line's "who" is a cast id, never a voice name: the narrator is "narrator" (voice am_onyx: a deep male narrator, calm, dry, a wry edge; the reference's narrator speaks about 2.2 words a second with short pauses), and each ball's id names its country. Ball voices: the stronger or holding power am_adam, the challenger am_fenrir (gruff), a third party am_michael; never the narrator's voice for a ball. (These are the engine's Kokoro names; it maps them to the same kind of voice in the other voice engines.) Ball lines (each at most 6 words) are where the countries deal with each other: in the shots with a RELATION, one or two per shot, 2 to 8 in the reel (the moment has none); everything else is the narrator. Conversational and concrete, with no pointless repetition. Every number in a line is written as words, years too ("nineteen ninety-one", "the eighth of December"), decimals with "point" ("fifty-one point seven million"): digits appear only in titles. Time runs forward: the shots' dates (titles and lines) never go back, except a flashback the line announces; "that night" or "that day" means the date of the shot it is in.
- Tones (when the voice engine takes them): the hook dry and intriguing; facts matter-of-fact; ball lines indignant, smug or flat; the moment hushed and slow; the payoff wry.
- Text: titles at most 18 characters per line (count them; a longer title is split into 2 lines or shortened), at most 2 lines, uppercase; a title is a date, a count with its unit or a place name (a quote of at most 4 words only in the order kind), never a summary or a sentence ("THE X UNRAVELS", "THE [PLACE] BREAK FREE"); labels name places, sides or events only (at most 14 characters).
- Assets: exactly 2, "ball" (hero, kind "character", every country and the marching crowd) and "sign", as described under assets. The ball's description ends with "Poses: arms_down (hands hanging low), point (right arm straight out, pointing), give (right arm reaching out, hand closed on what it holds), receive (both arms out, open hands palms up), turn_away (face slid to the side it faces, arms folded), arms_up (both hands up)." Maps, medallions, papers, flags, arrows, titles and labels are drawn by the kit and the shots, never assets. Asset states are plain param objects such as {"expression": "smug"}, never wrapped in another key, and never set flip (the stage mirrors in the shots).
- Music: Suspenseful, Driving (a tense history story with a pulse), 102 to 122 bpm; payoff_shot = the shot that answers the question (where the outcome lands). Sound effects: a pop when a title or the sign's word lands, a knock when a sign is planted or flipped, a whoosh on a jump to another map and on the iris; about 1 per shot, none in the moment except one soft knock on its gesture.

## assets
- "ball" (kind "character", hero, w 640, h 520, animated true): a country as a flag-ball. The body is a circle of radius 170 centred at (0, -20). Params:

- The sign stands on the country it names, seated after the balls: const g = kit.seat(country, v, [a, b], 300); h.asset('sign', g[0], g[1], 0.7, {word, from, swap, crossed}) (the seat is the sign's centre, so the 300 px gap keeps the plank, about 250 px wide and 100 px above the seat, clear of the balls; on a small or narrow country the post's foot, 180 px below the seat, may stand on the coast or sea beside it). Draw the sign before the balls. A ball never covers the sign's word, the sign never covers a ball's face, and the space above a speaking ball stays free for its bubble.
- Each RELATION is acted out with its own staging, its action on the cue word (the hand points h.asset returns place what hands hold):
  - ask/refuse: the asker leans 20 px toward the other on its line (receive or give, hopeful or determined); on the refusal the other turns away (turn_away, facing away from the asker), smug or angry, and the asker's face falls (worried or shocked). ask/agree: the other turns to it with receive and a happy nod (a 10 px dip and back over 0.4 s).
  - argue: both face each other and point (point), angry or determined; each leans 20 px toward the other on its own line and back after; the listener's brows go up.
  - threaten (a show of force, an ultimatum): the threatening ball points and grows to 1.25 times over 0.5 s; the other shrinks to 0.85 and looks worried; they never touch.
  - join (a union, an alliance, a merger): the two face each other and reach out (give) as they slide 30 px toward each other; on the cue the union's one fill fades in over both countries, and both turn happy.
  - split (independence, secession, a breakup): on the cue the union's fill cross-fades to the members' own fills; the leaving ball turns away (turn_away, facing away) and slides 60 px further onto its own land; the other reaches after it (receive), shocked or sad.
  - hand over (a crown, a paper, a key, a flag, land, power): the giver holds the thing in its hand (give: drawn at the handR its h.asset returns); on the cue it travels on a gentle arc (60 px high) from the giver's hand to the receiver's hand over 0.6 to 0.8 s, and stays in the receiver's hand (receive, at its handR); land that changes hands cross-fades its fill to the receiver's colour on the same word.
  - sign (a treaty, an accord, an act of union): kit.paper (w 150, h 190) on the map midway between the two, 40 px below their centres, both reaching toward it (give); on the cue its red seal pops (o.seal) and the title gives the date.
