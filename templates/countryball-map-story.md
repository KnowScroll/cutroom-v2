# countryball-map-story

## about
A history story told by countries as flag-balls on a real map. Every country in the story is a ball painted with its own flag, with big cartoon eyes and thin noodle arms; it sits on its own country, which is filled with its flag's main colour, while every other country stays pale beige. The balls argue in short spoken lines, the narrator carries the facts, and one wooden sign planted on the map carries the single word the whole story is about: that word changes as the story turns, and its last change is the ending. The camera cuts between real maps (the stage, a far capital, an ally, the whole region); big hand-lettered titles hold the dates and numbers; power is shown as size (a ball swells while the other shrinks); one dark, quiet night scene holds the decisive human moment before the bright map returns for the aftermath. For wars, independence, breakups and unions of countries, border changes, disputed land, empires rising and falling, treaties and diplomatic standoffs. Not for machines, biology, personal stories or anything without countries on a map.

## look
- Clarity first: at any moment the frame holds the map, at most 3 balls (except the small marching crowd of the event beat), at most 1 sign, at most 1 title (one or two lines) at the top and at most 3 map labels. Nothing else: no panels, cards, legends, compasses, insets, charts or icons floating free.
- The map is REAL: land, coasts and country shapes come from the engine's Natural Earth data through kit.map (below). Never draw a coastline, a country outline or a region by hand, and never fill a country with a hand-made polygon.
- Canvas helpers, exactly as the engine defines them: h.rng(seed) returns a FUNCTION (const r = h.rng(7); then r(), r(), r() give numbers from 0 to 1); never use h.rng(seed) itself as a number. h.rr(x, y, w, hgt, r) and h.poly([[x, y], ...]) take NO ctx argument and only build a path: call ctx.fill() or ctx.stroke() after them. h.asset('id', x, y, scale, params) takes NO ctx either. Never pass a maxWidth to fillText (squeezed text is rejected): shrink the font until the text fits.
- Map helpers from the engine (use them, never write your own projection or country data):
  - h.countryView(names, x, y, w, hgt) returns a flat-map view {proj: 'merc', x, y, r, lon0, lat0} that fits the named countries' main land inside the box w x hgt centred on (x, y). names is one Natural Earth country name or an array of them ('Russia', 'United Kingdom', 'United States of America', 'Czechia', 'Bosnia and Herz.' ...); a historic union is named by its members, or by 'USSR', 'Yugoslavia' or 'Czechoslovakia'. Always today's Natural Earth names, also for a historic republic, province or kingdom: its land is the country it became ('Russia', 'Ukraine', 'Kazakhstan', never a period name such as '[Name] SSR' or '[Name] SFSR'). A wrong name throws an error that lists the right ones.
  - h.landPath(v), h.coastPath(v), h.borderPath(v), h.countryPath(names, v): Path2D objects already projected for the view v: ctx.fill(path, 'evenodd') or ctx.stroke(path).
  - h.geo(lon, lat, v) returns [x, y, z]: the frame position of a place (z is always 1 on a flat map). h.countryCentre(names) returns [lon, lat], the middle of the country's main land.
- Colour is a set of relations (OKLCH); keep them, whatever the topic:
  - Sea (palette.bg): steel blue, lightness 0.54, chroma 0.08, hue about 233. Along every coast a pale halo on the sea side: lightness 0.62, chroma 0.07, hue 225.
  - Land of every country NOT in the story (palette.land): pale warm beige, lightness 0.80, chroma 0.05, hue about 85.
  - Countries in the story are filled with their flag's main colour, at map strength: palette.red (lightness 0.52, chroma 0.155, hue 25), palette.green (0.58, 0.10, 152), palette.accent (saffron orange, 0.71, 0.145, 66, also the caption's number colour), palette.yellow (0.82, 0.14, 95), palette.blue (periwinkle, 0.66, 0.09, 270: lighter and more violet than the sea so it never merges with it), palette.cream (0.97, 0.025, 100, for a white flag). A country's fill is always a colour that is IN its own flag (a white-blue-red flag fills blue or red, never yellow or green); two neighbours in the story never share a fill colour: give the second one another colour from its own flag (cream for white).
  - Ink (outlines, coast lines, label text): warm near-black, lightness 0.17, chroma 0.03, hue 25. Text on titles and captions: palette.cream.
  - Wood (the sign): palette.wood, lightness 0.55, chroma 0.07, hue 60, with darker grain (lightness 0.42) and lighter edges (0.65).
  - Flag-balls use the REAL colours of their country's flag (the only colours outside the palette), drawn exactly as the flag looks.
  - The night background: near-black blue (lightness 0.14, chroma 0.02, hue 260) with a warm light pool (lightness 0.45, chroma 0.06, hue 75).
- The only text on screen: the title, at most 3 map labels (place names), medallion names and the sign's word. No timeline captions, era names, legends or other labels.
- No pixel grid; flat fills; outlines 5 px ink on balls, the sign, papers and medallions; the map has a 2.5 px ink coast line and no outlines elsewhere.
- Type: kit.font(size, weight) returns `${weight} ${size}px "Segoe UI"` (labels, captions). Titles use the hand-lettered face `bold ${size}px "Segoe Print"`, uppercase. Map labels use `600 ${size}px Georgia`, uppercase, ctx.letterSpacing = '6px' (reset to '0px' after).
- kit.title(ctx, text, x, y, o): one line of hand-lettered title: uppercase, `bold ${size}px "Segoe Print"` (o.size default 104), palette.cream fill over a 12 px ink stroke (strokeText first, lineJoin round) and a soft ink drop shadow (shadowOffsetY 6, blur 10, ink at 45%), rotated by o.rot (default -0.03 rad) around (x, y); it shrinks the size until the measured text is at most 900 px wide; o.k pops it in (scale 0.7 to 1 with easeBack over 0.3 s, opacity 0 to 1 over 0.12 s); o.color replaces the fill (palette.accent for the number in a title). A title of two lines is two calls, the second 112 px lower.
- kit.panel(ctx, x, y, w, hgt, o): a small wooden plank (palette.wood, 5 px ink outline, corner radius 10, two dark nail dots at the ends, three thin grain lines), for the rare label that needs a backing.
- The kit also returns these helpers (the shots call them; write them exactly):
  - kit.view(names, box) = h.countryView(names, 540, box ? box.y : 860, box ? box.w : 1500, box ? box.h : 1000): the default box keeps the map's subject between y 360 and 1360, clear of the title and the captions; a wide country overflows the frame's sides a little so its main body fills the width (the reference crops its maps).
  - kit.cam(lt, A, B, t0 = 0, dur = 1) returns a view moving from view A to view B between lt = t0 and t0 + dur with e = h.easeInOut(progress): x, y, lon0 and lat0 interpolated linearly with e, r = A.r * Math.pow(B.r / A.r, e), proj 'merc'. After the move (and when B is A) the view keeps a slow push-in: r = B.r * (1 + 0.006 * (lt - t0 - dur)) for lt > t0 + dur.
  - kit.pt(lon, lat, v) returns [x, y] = h.geo(lon, lat, v).slice(0, 2); kit.centre(names, v) returns kit.pt of h.countryCentre(names).
  - kit.map(ctx, v, o): the whole map for the view v, in this order: (1) the coast halo: ctx.lineJoin = 'round'; stroke h.coastPath(v) with the halo colour 30 px wide at 30%, then 14 px at 45%; (2) ctx.fillStyle = palette.land; ctx.fill(h.landPath(v), 'evenodd'); (3) for each f of o.fills (an array of {names, colour, a}): const P = h.countryPath(f.names, v); ctx.globalAlpha = f.a ?? 1; fill P with kit.palette[f.colour] || f.colour; stroke P with that colour mixed 30% toward ink, 3 px; ctx.globalAlpha = 1; (4) stroke h.coastPath(v) with ink at 70%, 2.5 px; (5) draw the paper texture image (made once in makeKit with h.cache('paper', 1080, 1920, ...): 1400 seeded soft blotches 6 to 28 px wide, half ink at 2 to 4% opacity, half cream at 2 to 5%, plus 200 short fibre strokes at 3%: a fine, barely visible grain, never cloudy patches) with ctx.drawImage(image, 0, 0); (6) for each l of o.labels (at most 3) ({text, names, dx, dy} or {text, lon, lat, dx, dy}): kit.label at kit.centre(l.names, v) (or kit.pt(l.lon, l.lat, v)) shifted by (dx, dy) (default 0, -240: above where the country's ball sits), light when it sits on a dark fill. Fills listed later draw over earlier ones (a republic over its union).
  - kit.label(ctx, text, x, y, o): a map label with NO panel: uppercase Georgia 600, o.size default 44, letterSpacing 6px, centred; ink at 65% on light land (lightness over 0.6) or cream at 90% on a dark fill (o.light true), a 4 px stroke of the land colour under it for contrast; o.k pops it (opacity 0 to 1 over 0.2 s). It measures the text and moves it inward so the whole text stays inside x 70 to 1010 and y 480 to 1400 (a country centre near the frame edge must not push its name off the frame). One fillText call (never letter by letter). kit.label, kit.medallion and kit.title draw nothing when their text is missing or empty (never the word "undefined").
  - kit.medallion(ctx, x, y, rad, name, o): a portrait medallion for a person in the story, never a likeness: a cream ring (rad, 10 px) and a palette.green ring inside it (8 px), a sepia disc (lightness 0.55, chroma 0.04, hue 70) with a dark generic bust silhouette (a head circle at 0.42 rad and rounded shoulders; o.cap true adds a peaked cap), a soft ink shadow, the name under it in `bold 40px "Segoe Print"` ink with a 6 px cream stroke; o.k pops it (scale 0.6 to 1, easeBack, 0.3 s).
  - kit.paper(ctx, x, y, w, hgt, o): a document (cream sheet, 4 px ink outline, 4 grey text lines, a red wax seal disc at the bottom right when o.seal), rotated by o.rot; o.k pops it.
  - kit.flags = the style guide's "flags" object, unchanged: kit.flags['<cast id>'] is that ball's flag object.
  - kit.flag(ctx, x, y, s, colours, lt): a small flag on a thin ink pole (pole 90 * s tall, cloth 60 x 40 * s in the given colour bands, top band first), its free edge waving with lt (sine, 2 Hz); its foot (x, y) is where it stands.
  - kit.dots(ctx, x1, y1, x2, y2, k, colour): a dotted route as a gentle arc (control point 18% of the length above the middle): 9 px dots every 26 px, the first k share of them drawn; returns the [x, y] of the last drawn dot (a small paper or ball travels there).
  - kit.arrow(ctx, x1, y1, x2, y2, k, colour): a dashed movement arrow (6 px, dash [22, 14], the first k share of its length) with a solid arrowhead (34 px) at its tip once k reaches 1.
  - kit.wash(ctx, a): the flashback tint: a full-frame cream layer (palette.cream) at opacity a (0.35 to 0.45) drawn with globalCompositeOperation 'source-over', then a 12% sepia layer; it lightens and desaturates everything under it.
- Backgrounds (kit.bg), exactly these 3:
  - "sea": palette.bg with a soft lighter pool (lightness + 0.08 at its centre) centred at (540, 800) of radius 900, a vignette to lightness - 0.11 in the corners (the engine rejects a background whose brightness varies too little), the paper texture image, and 25 faint pale wave marks (short 40 px arcs, cream at 10 to 14%) drifting 6 px per second and breathing. Shots draw kit.map on it.
  - "overview": "sea" plus kit.map(ctx, kit.cam(lt, V, V), {}) with V = kit.view of the story's main countries (the plan's background description names them as "FIT: name, name"), no fills. It shows the kit's map at work.
  - "night": the night colour with a warm light pool (radius 700) at (540, 1050) as if from car headlights or a lamp, a ground plane (a slightly lighter band from y 1150 down, its top edge soft), a vignette, and 70 dust motes rising 25 to 45 px per second through the light (3 to 6 px, cream at 20 to 50%, brighter inside the pool); the pool breathes (radius +- 8% and brightness +- 6% on a 3 s cycle), so two frames 1.5 s apart clearly differ.
- kit.post: none (return post as a function that does nothing).

## motion
- The balls are always alive: a bob of 4 px and a 3% squash on a 1.2 s cycle, a blink every 2.5 to 4 s, pupils that glance at whoever is speaking. Acting, written into the code by time: every ball on screen changes its expression at the start of every line (the speaker shows the line's mood, the listener reacts) and changes its pose once in the middle of the shot, for example const L1 = h.line(1).start; const ex = lt < L1 ? 'worried' : 'angry'; const pose = lt < sh.dur / 2 ? 'arms_down' : 'point'. Speaking balls pass mouth: h.mouth('<cast id>').
- Things pop on the word that names them: titles and labels pop (easeBack 0.25 to 0.3 s), a sign drops 80 px and bounces once (easeBack 0.35 s), papers and medallions pop, flags spring up from 0 height.
- The sign's word changes with a flip: its swap param runs 0 to 1 over 0.5 s on the line that says the new word (the plank turns edge-on and back, the word switches at the middle), with a small dust puff at the post's foot.
- A country changing hands or colour cross-fades its fill over 0.4 s (the old fill underneath at full, the new one on top with a rising from 0 to 1) and its ball (if any) reacts at the same moment.
- Size is power: in the odds beat one ball grows to 2 to 3 times its size over 0.8 s (easeOut) while the other shrinks to about half; both keep bobbing.
- Movement on the map: dashed arrows draw themselves over 0.6 to 1 s; a crowd of 6 to 12 small helmeted balls (the ball asset at scale 0.16 to 0.24, helmet true) marches along an arrow, staggered 0.15 s apart, each bobbing; messages travel along kit.dots routes over 0.8 to 1.2 s.
- The map camera holds one view per shot with the slow push-in (kit.cam); a shot may make ONE move (0.8 to 1.2 s, easeInOut), for example from the country to one region of it; between places the cut does the travelling.
- The night moment is slow: moves of at most 0.5 s, a long hold, one gesture, dust drifting; no pops and no titles.
- Easing: easeInOut for the camera, easeOut for arrivals, easeIn for leaving, easeBack for pops.

## plan
- First decide the CAST and the WORD, all real:
  - CAST: the 2 or 3 countries (or unions) whose argument the story is, each with its real flag (as of the story's time: a union's flag while it exists, its successors' flags after). Only countries and unions with a real flag are balls and speakers; an alliance, treaty or organisation without its own country is never a ball. People (leaders, commanders) appear only as portrait medallions with their real names, never in "cast" and never speaking.
  - WORD: the single word or short name the story is a fight over (what a place is called, which country it belongs to, what a union calls itself, whose flag flies over the capital), and its REAL values in order, each one a name or status the place officially had, with the date it changed: the first value, at least one change in the middle, and the last value (2 to 4 values). Never a descriptive word made up for the sign. The sign shows these, uppercase, at most 10 characters each (the short official form or abbreviation).
  - FLAGS: put every ball's flag ONCE into the style guide as "style": {..., "flags": {"<cast id>": {"dir": "h", "bands": ["#hex", "#hex", "#hex"], "emblem": "none", "emblemColour": "#hex"}}}, with the flag's real colours, stripe order and emblem (see the ball asset); a ball whose flag changes in the story gets a second entry "<cast id>_new". The kit hands them to every shot as kit.flags; never describe a flag in words.
  - FILLS: while a union or empire exists it is ONE fill (its flag colour) over all its members; a member gets its own fill only from the moment it leaves (event, collapse, aftermath, payoff). Never a member's fill on top of its union's fill while both exist.
  - The DATES and NUMBERS: every title number is a real date, count or duration with its unit, written in digits ("[D] [MONTH] [YEAR]", "[N] DAYS"); if you are not certain of a value, use another you are certain of. Never a ratio or score invented to show who is stronger.
- Every shot idea starts with these lines, then says what moves on which line:
  - "VIEW: FIT [country names]" (the map view kit.view of those countries), or "VIEW: NIGHT" for the moment beat. FIT only the 1 to 3 countries the shot is about: the hook, setup, word and payoff use the stage view (the whole union, empire or region); every other shot is closer (one region, one country, a capital's surroundings) or elsewhere (the ally), so the map changes like the reference's (it cuts to a new place every 2 to 3 s). A shot with 2 or more lines may add "then FIT [names] on line 2": a HARD CUT (no move) to a second, closer view at the start of that line.
  - "FILLS: [country] = [colour role]; ..." every country in the story that is on screen with its fill (red, green, accent, yellow, blue, cream), and any change: "[country] red -> yellow on line 2".
  - "BALLS: [cast id] on [country] ([expression], [pose], [size])" for each ball on screen; [pose] is one of the ball's 5 poses (arms_down, point, raise, hold_post, arms_up). Size is normal (scale 0.85 to 1.2) in every beat except odds, where one ball grows to 2 to 3 and the other shrinks to 0.5, and moment, where both are 1.4 to 1.7.
  - "SIGN: '[WORD]' on [country]" and, when it changes, "-> '[NEW WORD]' on line [n]".
  - "TITLE: '[TEXT]'" (or "TITLE: none"); "MEDALLION: [real name] on [country]"; "MARKER at [Town] [lon, lat]" for a named town or city (a small flag on a pole with its map label), with its real coordinates (longitude, latitude, east and north positive).
- Structure: exactly 14 shots in this order, one beat each (this template's count overrides any other shot count in the request); a beat the story cannot fill takes the beat before it again (two pressure shots, for example), but the required beats are always there:
  1. hook: the stage map with the 2 main balls and the sign showing the first value of the WORD; a title with the place's name and the year the story starts. Line: the conflict as one surprising claim with a real number in it (a year, a count, a size), or a question that ends with "?", naming the brief's subject; never a definition or a general statement.
  2. setup: the same map pushed in; the balls speak 2 short lines to each other (one asks or demands, the other refuses); the narrator adds who they are in a few words.
  3. flashback: the same map under kit.wash: how the situation began (a real date as the title); one ball says one short line or the narrator tells it.
  4. word: back to full colour; the sign's word changes for the first time (the flip on the line that says the new word); the other ball reacts (raised brows, a "?" above it).
  5. cost: a human-scale detail on the map: a real count of people, a protest, a named town (MARKER) or one small figure; the line gives the number.
  6. pressure: years pass or the pressure grows: dashed arrows, small signs, a fill creeping, a title with the years.
  7. ally: a cut to another map (a far capital or an ally's country filled in its flag colour): one ball asks a third ball for the help it really asked for in history, and the third ball answers as it really did, in one short line each. If the story has no such real request, this shot is a second pressure beat instead.
  8. odds: back on the stage map: one ball swells to 2 to 3 times while the other shrinks; a title with a real comparison of the SAME quantity for both sides, with units (two populations, two areas, two armies: "[N] MILLION VS [M] MILLION"). Line: says those numbers.
  9. order: a leader's medallion on its home country; a rolled paper travels along a kit.dots route to the stage; a title quoting the order or its date.
  10. event: the decisive event on the map: a crowd of small helmeted balls marching along arrows, or countries changing their fills one after another (cross-fades 0.3 s apart), with at most 2 labels naming what happens.
  11. collapse: the end comes fast: a title with the real duration or date; a fill shrinks or changes, a white flag (kit.flag in palette.cream) rises over a town or a ball deflates. This is the music's payoff_shot.
  12. moment: VIEW NIGHT: two balls large (diameter 480 to 600 px) face each other in the light pool; ONE gesture that decides it (a paper handed over, a flag lowered and another raised, a turned back); the narrator's line is short and quiet; no title.
  13. aftermath: an iris opens from the night onto the bright map (the first 0.6 s): what happened after, with a real number of years or a count as the title; the balls changed (new flags, a helmet off, a new country filled).
  14. payoff: the hook's exact composition (same view and positions); the sign's word changes for the last time to the final value; a title states the result in at most 4 words; the line lands on the WORD itself, echoing the hook.
- Lines: one narrator plus the story's balls; every line's "who" is a cast id ("narrator" or a ball's id), never a voice name. The narrator (cast id "narrator") speaks with voice am_onyx (a deep male narrator, calm, dry, a wry edge; the reference's narrator speaks about 2.2 words a second with short pauses). Ball voices: the stronger or holding power am_adam, the challenger am_fenrir (gruff), a third party am_michael; never the narrator's voice for a ball. (These are the engine's Kokoro names; it maps them to the same kind of voice in the other voice engines.) 5 to 8 ball lines in the whole reel, each at most 6 words, in setup, flashback, ally and payoff (the moment has none); everything else is the narrator. The request's word budget decides the narrator's length: split it over the 14 shots and count every line before returning. Conversational, concrete, one new fact per line; never repeat a fact. Every number in a line is written as words, years too ("nineteen ninety-one", "the eighth of December"), decimals with "point" ("fifty-one point seven million"): digits appear only in titles. Time runs forward: the shots' dates (titles and lines) never go back, except in the flashback beat; "that night" or "that day" means the date of the shot it is in.
- Tones (when the voice engine takes them): the hook dry and intriguing; facts matter-of-fact; ball lines indignant, smug or flat; the moment hushed and slow; the payoff wry.
- Text: titles at most 18 characters per line (count them; a longer title is split into 2 lines or shortened), at most 2 lines, uppercase; a title is a date, a count with its unit or a place name (a quote of at most 4 words only in the order beat), never a summary or a sentence ("THE X UNRAVELS", "THE [PLACE] BREAK FREE"); labels name places, sides or events only (at most 14 characters).
- Assets: exactly 2, "ball" (hero, kind "character", every country and the marching crowd) and "sign", as described under assets. The ball's description ends with "Poses: arms_down, point, raise, hold_post, arms_up." Maps, medallions, papers, flags, arrows, titles and labels are drawn by the kit and the shots, never assets. Asset states are plain param objects such as {"expression": "smug"}, never wrapped in another key.
- Music: Suspenseful, Driving (a tense history story with a pulse), 102 to 122 bpm; payoff_shot = the collapse shot (11). Sound effects: a pop when a title or the sign's word lands, a knock when a sign is planted or flipped, a whoosh on a jump to another map and on the iris; about 1 per shot, none in the moment except one soft knock on its gesture.

## assets
- "ball" (kind "character", hero, w 640, h 520, animated true): a country as a flag-ball. The body is a circle of radius 170 centred at (0, -20). Params:
  - flag: {dir: 'h' or 'v', bands: [the flag's stripe colours as hex, in order: top to bottom for 'h', left to right for 'v'], weights (optional array of relative band sizes), emblem: 'none' | 'star' | 'sickle' | 'disc' | 'canton' | 'cross' | 'nordic', emblemColour, cantonColour}. Draw the bands clipped to the circle. Emblems: 'star' a five-pointed star (radius 40) at the upper left (-70, -80); 'sickle' a small star (radius 16) above a hammer and sickle (a crescent blade and a crossed hammer, about 70 px) at the upper left; 'disc' a circle (radius 46) in the middle; 'canton' a rectangle covering the upper left quarter in cantonColour with a 3 x 3 grid of small white stars or dots; 'cross' a centred cross of bars 44 px wide; 'nordic' a cross whose upright sits at x -50. The flag's stripes fill the whole circle; it is the country's REAL flag for its time. A flag whose field is ONE colour with an emblem has ONE band (bands: ["#hex"]) and the emblem on top: never add stripes a flag does not have; equal stripes are equal bands (use weights only for unequal stripes). Shots always pass flag: kit.flags['<cast id>']; when p.flag is missing draw a plain grey ball.
  - expression: 'neutral' | 'happy' | 'smug' | 'angry' | 'worried' | 'sad' | 'shocked' | 'determined' | 'tired'. Eyes: two white ovals (64 x 80) at (-58, -40) and (58, -40) with 5 px ink outlines and black pupils (radius 22) moved by look; eyelids cut the top of the eyes for smug and tired (a flat ink lid line), brows (12 px ink strokes 30 px above the eyes) angled down to the centre for angry and determined, up in the middle for worried and sad, high for shocked; the mouth (40 px wide) below the eyes at y 40: a smile, a flat line, a frown or an open oval (open while p.mouth is 1).
  - look: [x, y] from -1 to 1, where the pupils point.
  - pose (the engine's pose sheet; exactly these 5): arms_down (both hands hanging beside the lower body), point (the right arm straight out to the side, a pointing hand), raise (the right arm up beside the body, hand open), hold_post (the right arm reaching right to (290, 30), the hand closed around a vertical post there), arms_up (both arms up, hands open). Arms are noodles: 14 px wide tubes (quadratic curves with a soft bend) from the shoulders at (-150, 20) and (150, 20), in the flag's first band colour mixed 45% toward beige, 3 px ink outline, ending in a mitten hand (a 26 px circle with a small thumb bump). flip: true mirrors the whole ball (a ball on the right side faces left).
  - hold: 'none' | 'paper', a small cream paper at the right hand.
  - helmet: true adds a rounded army helmet (palette.green mixed 30% toward ink, a brim line) on top of the circle.
  - Shading: over the flag a radial darkening toward the bottom right (ink at 18%) and a soft highlight at the upper left (cream at 18%); a 6 px ink outline around the circle; a contact shadow ellipse (300 x 40, ink at 28%) at y 238. Blink every 3 s (lids close for 0.12 s, phase from p.t).
- "sign" (kind "prop", w 420, h 560, animated false): the motif. A wooden plank 360 x 140 centred at (0, -150) on a post 26 px wide from y -90 to y 260, the post's foot in a small dirt mound with a contact shadow at y 262. Plank: palette.wood with 4 darker grain lines, lighter top edge, two nails, 5 px ink outline, corners radius 12. Params: word (string, the current value), from (string, the previous value), swap (0 to 1: the plank's height scales 1 to 0 to 1, showing from while swap < 0.5 and word after), crossed (boolean: a red hand-drawn stroke through the word), tilt (radians, default 0.04). The word: uppercase `bold ${size}px "Segoe Print"`, ink, size from 76 down until it fits 310 px wide (measure it), centred on the plank; it is never blank (every shot passes the current word). WORD values are at most 10 characters so they stay big (use the short official form or the abbreviation). This is the ONLY text an asset draws.
- No backing plate or frame behind either asset; the box corners stay empty.

## shots
- Draw order: kit.bg(ctx, lt, 'sea' or 'night'), kit.map(ctx, v, {fills, labels}) (not on 'night'), routes and arrows, signs, balls (back to front), papers and flags held or flying, medallions, kit.wash (flashback only), then titles. Never paint a solid rectangle over the map.
- The view: const V = kit.view([...names from the VIEW line]); const v = kit.cam(lt, V, V2 || V, t0, 1); use this same v for kit.map, kit.pt and kit.centre in the frame, so everything sits on its real place. Never guess a screen position for a country or town.
